Licorice: A Valuable and Versatile Plant

Licorice (Glycyrrhiza glabra) is a perennial plant from the Fabaceae family, known for its medicinal properties and sweet taste. The roots contain compounds like glycyrrhizin, which have anti-inflammatory, antiviral, and sweetening effects. Licorice is used in the food, pharmaceutical, cosmetic, and personal care industries. Due to the importance of this plant, knowing the Licorice yield per hectare and the factors affecting it is essential for farmers and industry professionals.

Factors Affecting Licorice Yield per Hectare

The yield of licorice, like any other crop, is affected by numerous factors. These can be categorized as follows:

1. Variety Type and Seed Quality

Choosing the appropriate licorice variety, considering local weather and soil conditions, plays a significant role in yield. Cultivated varieties typically outperform local landraces. Seed quality also affects germination and plant establishment. Using high-quality, certified seeds can help increase yield.

2. Climatic Conditions

Licorice is a plant that requires a temperate and warm climate. The optimal temperature range for growth is between 20 and 30 degrees Celsius. Adequate sunlight and appropriate humidity are also essential for optimal licorice growth. Water and light deficits can lead to reduced yield.

3. Soil Management and Nutrition

Licorice performs best in fertile, well-drained soils. Before planting, soil should be enriched with organic and mineral fertilizers. Deficiencies in nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium can reduce yield. Conducting soil tests to determine nutritional needs can help optimize feeding and boost yield.

4. Irrigation Methods

Proper irrigation is one of the most important factors in increasing licorice yield. Water deficit can reduce plant growth and yield. Over-irrigation can cause root rot and lower yield. Using modern irrigation methods like drip irrigation can help meet water requirements and improve water-use efficiency.

Drip irrigation using Strip pipe One of the best irrigation methods for this crop. The strip pipe method applies water locally, preventing evaporation and providing the required soil moisture.

5. Pest and Disease Management

Like any other crop, licorice is susceptible to pests and diseases. Timely identification and control can maintain plant health and prevent yield loss. Using Integrated Pest Management (IPM) practices helps reduce pesticide use and protect the environment.

6. Harvest Time

The timing of harvest significantly affects the quality and glycyrrhizin content of licorice roots. The best time to harvest is when the roots have reached maximum growth. Harvesting too early can reduce glycyrrhizin levels, while late harvesting may compromise root quality. It is advisable to consult with relevant specialists regarding the optimal timing.

What is the licorice yield per hectare?

The licorice yield per hectare varies depending on the factors mentioned. Under ideal conditions, dry root yield can reach up to 5 tons per hectare. However, under unfavorable conditions, it may drop to less than 2 tons per hectare. The average yield is approximately 3 to 4 tons of dry roots per hectare. To achieve high yields, all influential factors must be considered, and the best management practices applied. For example, research from the University of Tehran shows that using biological fertilizers and drip irrigation can increase yields by up to 25%.

Strategies for Increasing Licorice Yield per Hectare

To improve licorice yields, the following strategies can be employed:

  • Selection of appropriate, high-quality varieties
  • Proper soil preparation and enhancement with organic and mineral fertilizers
  • Regular and adequate irrigation using modern methods such as drip irrigation
  • Timely control of pests and diseases
  • Harvesting at the optimal time

By following these guidelines, licorice yields per hectare can be significantly increased.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Licorice performs best in fertile soil with good drainage.
