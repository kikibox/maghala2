Soybean Yield Per Hectare and a Comprehensive Guide

Soybean (Glycine max) is one of the most important oilseed and protein crops in the world, playing a vital role in ensuring food supply for humans and livestock. This valuable plant is not only a primary source of plant protein and edible oils but also has widespread applications in various industries, including biodiesel production and animal feed. Therefore, enhancing soybean yield per hectare is considered one of the key goals for farmers and agricultural professionals worldwide. Increasing soybean yields can significantly contribute to food security, economic sustainability for farmers, and reducing dependence on imports. According to reports from the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), global soybean production has been consistently increasing, indicating the growing importance of this crop. In this comprehensive article, we will examine the factors affecting soybean yield per hectare and novel strategies for optimizing and increasing the production of this strategic crop.

Key Factors Affecting Soybean Yield Per Hectare

Soybean yield per hectare is influenced by multiple factors, and the identification and proper management of these elements can lead to a significant increase in productivity. These factors include genetic, environmental, and management aspects.

1. Selection of Suitable Soybean Varieties

Choosing a soybean variety that is adapted to the climatic and soil conditions of the cultivation region is the first and most important step in achieving high yields. Soybean varieties are classified based on Maturity Group, which indicates their growth period. Selecting a variety with an appropriate maturity group for the region ensures that the plant has sufficient time to complete its life cycle and fill its pods. Additionally, variety resistance to pests, diseases, and environmental stresses (such as drought or salinity) is of great importance. Improved varieties with high yield potential can, if other agronomic principles are observed, soybean yield per hectare increase substantially. It is recommended that farmers consult with agricultural experts before planting to select the best variety for their farm.

2. Soil Quality and Nutrient Management

Fertile soil with a suitable structure is the primary foundation for optimal soybean growth. Soybeans require soil with medium texture (sandy loam to clay loam), good drainage, and a pH between 6 and 7. Excessive acidity or alkalinity can limit the uptake of nutrients. Proper plant nutrient management is also critical for increasing soybean yield per hectare Although soybean is a legume capable of fixing atmospheric nitrogen, it may require limited starter nitrogen during early growth stages, particularly in poor soils. Phosphorus and potassium are high-use elements for soybeans that play a key role in seed formation and root development. In addition, micronutrients such as molybdenum, iron, manganese, and zinc are essential for soybean growth and yield. Regular soil testing and fertilization based on the results are very important for meeting the plant's nutritional needs. According to research from the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA), proper soil nutrient management can contribute to up to a 20% increase in soybean yields [1].

3. Climatic Conditions

Climate conditions, particularly temperature and rainfall, directly affect soybean yield per hectare. Soybean requires a moderate temperature between 20 and 30 degrees Celsius for optimal growth. Temperatures that are too high or too low, especially during the flowering and pod-filling stages, can reduce yield. Adequate rainfall and proper distribution throughout the growing season are vital for soybean. Drought stress during sensitive growth stages, particularly flowering and pod formation, can significantly damage yield. On the other hand, over-irrigation or heavy rainfall can lead to waterlogging, reduced oxygen supply to the roots, and an increased risk of fungal diseases. Climate change and rainfall fluctuations highlight the importance of using supplementary and modern irrigation systems.

4. Irrigation Management (Role of Drip Tapes)

Given the importance of water in agriculture and the limitations of water resources, proper selection and management of the irrigation system play a decisive role in optimizing soybean yield per hectare. Traditional irrigation methods, such as flood irrigation, are no longer adequate to meet the needs of modern agriculture due to low efficiency and high water loss. In contrast, the use of modern irrigation methods, particularly drip tape systems, can significantly increase water efficiency and promote better soybean growth.

Drip tape delivers water in droplets directly to the plant root zone, minimizing water losses due to surface evaporation and runoff. This method also enables fertigation, allowing for the simultaneous application of fertilizers, which results in more effective nutrient uptake by the plant. For soybean cultivation, using 20 cm drip tape, 1000 m roll or slotted drip tape can be suitable options. Slotted drip tape, with its internal drippers and specific spacing, ensures a more uniform water distribution along the planting line. 5. Planting Density and Proper Spacing

Plant density and row spacing are agronomic factors that intensely affect Soybean yield per hectare Excess density can lead to intense competition between plants for light, water, and nutrients, thereby reducing yield. Conversely, low density may fail to fully realize yield potential due to suboptimal use of space and resources. 6. Pest, disease, and weed control

Pests, diseases, and weeds can cause significant damage to soybean fields and Soybean yield per hectare severely reduce it. Integrated Pest Management (IPM), which combines agricultural, biological, and chemical methods, is the most effective approach for controlling these harmful factors. Early identification of pests and diseases and timely intervention can prevent the spread of damage. Weeds also compete with soybean plants for light, water, and nutrients, leading to yield loss. Chemical (herbicide) and mechanical (tillage and hoeing) weed control during early soybean growth stages is crucial. According to agricultural experts at Purdue University, regular field monitoring and the use of IPM strategies are essential to maintain soybean plant health and prevent yield reduction [2].

7. Optimal planting and harvesting times

Proper timing for planting and harvesting is also a critical factor in maximizing Soybean yield per hectare . Early spring planting, after the risk of frost has passed and soil temperature reaches the optimal level (approximately 10-12°C), can lead to stronger and more vigorous vegetative growth. This allows the plant sufficient time to build up adequate biomass before flowering. On the other hand, late harvesting can result in pod shattering, quality degradation, and increased risk of damage from adverse weather conditions. Harvesting at appropriate seed moisture content not only minimizes losses but also reduces drying costs. Careful planning based on the sown variety and regional climatic conditions is necessary to determine the best planting and harvesting times.

Among these, drip irrigation using tape drip lines is considered an ideal solution for increasing soybean yield per hectare is known.

Drip irrigation and its benefits for soybeans

The drip irrigation system using a tape line slowly and directly delivers water to the plant's root zone. This method offers several key benefits for soybean cultivation:

  • Water conservation: Reducing surface evaporation, runoff, and deep percolation below the root zone leads to significant water savings. This is critical in areas with limited water resources.
  • Improved fertilizer efficiency (fertigation): By injecting water-soluble fertilizers into the irrigation water, nutrients reach the plant roots directly and at the right time, significantly enhancing nutrient uptake and fertilizer efficiency.
  • Reduced weed growth: Because only the area around the plant roots is irrigated, weed growth between rows is reduced, which in turn decreases the need for weeding and herbicide use.
  • Reduced disease incidence: Leaves and stems remain dry, preventing the creation of a humid environment conducive to the growth of many fungal and bacterial diseases.
  • Uniform irrigation: The tape line with its precise emitters distributes water uniformly along rows and across the field, resulting in uniform plant growth and ultimately an increase in soybean yield per hectare can be achieved.
  • Better soil aeration: Due to the soil not being fully saturated, adequate aeration in the root zone is maintained, which is important for root health and the activity of beneficial soil microorganisms.
  • Cultivation on undulating terrain: Due to the adjustable pressure in drip systems and the use of drip tapes with uniform pressure, it is possible to irrigate fields with gentle slopes, which is difficult with conventional methods.

Calculating and analyzing soybean yield

To evaluate the success of the soybean crop and plan for future seasons, understanding how to calculate and analyze soybean yield per hectare is of high importance. This information helps farmers better understand influencing factors and adjust their strategies to increase performance.

Estimation formulas and their application

Accurately calculating soybean yield before full harvest can be difficult, but estimation formulas can provide an initial estimate. One common method is sampling from several points in the field and counting the number of pods and seeds per plant. For example, the following formula can be used for estimation:

$$\text{Tonnage per hectare (kilograms)} = \frac{\text{Number of plants per square meter} \times \text{Number of pods per plant} \times \text{Number of seeds per pod} \times \text{Weight of 1000 seeds (grams)}}{100}$$

This formula is approximate, and other factors such as seed moisture at harvest time must also be considered. However, by using such formulas and collecting precise field data, farmers can have a realistic picture of expected performance and implement necessary corrections in their agricultural management if needed.

Global and regional average yield

Soybean yield per hectare In various regions of the world, and even within a single country, it can vary significantly due to differences in climatic conditions, soil type, planted cultivars, and agronomic management level. For example, the global average soybean yield is typically reported between 2.5 and 3.5 tons per hectare, but in leading soybean-producing countries such as the United States, Brazil, and Argentina, this figure can reach 4 to 5 tons per hectare, and even more [3].

In Iran as well, soybean yield per hectare varies depending on the region and cultivation methods. However, with the application of modern agricultural techniques and advanced irrigation systems, there is significant potential to increase these yields substantially. The main goal for farmers is not only to reach high averages but to surpass them using sustainable and optimized methods.

What factors have the greatest impact on soybean yield?

Genetic factors (cultivar), soil conditions and nutrition, climate, irrigation management, planting density, and pest and disease control are the key factors affecting soybean yield per hectare.

Drip irrigation significantly improves soybean yield per hectare by saving water, increasing fertilizer application efficiency (fertigation), reducing weed growth, and lowering the incidence of diseases.

What is the best time to plant soybeans?

The best time to plant soybeans is after the danger of frost has passed and soil temperature has reached approximately 10 to 12 degrees Celsius, usually in early spring. This timing helps promote stronger vegetative growth.
