Drip tape for paprika: Applications

Drip irrigation using Drip tape, a modern and efficient method for paprika production. This method helps farmers optimize water use, increase crop yield, and reduce production costs. This article provides a comprehensive review of drip tape applications in paprika cultivation, its benefits, key considerations, and supplementary information.

What is drip tape and why is it important for paprika cultivation?

Drip tape, also known as drip irrigation tape, is a thin, flexible plastic tube containing internal emitters or holes at set intervals. These tapes are used for drip irrigation in fields and orchards, delivering water directly to plant roots. The use of drip tape in paprika cultivation is important for the following reasons:

  • Water use optimization: Drip tape delivers water directly to the root zone, preventing water loss due to surface evaporation.
  • Increased crop yield: Drip irrigation ensures continuous and uniform water supply to plants, which enhances growth and crop yield.
  • Reduced production costs: By optimizing water use and reducing labor requirements for irrigation, production costs are lowered.
  • Reduced plant diseases: Drip irrigation prevents leaf wetness and reduces the risk of fungal and bacterial diseases.
  • Ease of installation and use: The drip tape is easy to install and use, requiring no specialized expertise.

Factors influencing the installation cost of drip tape in pepper farming

The cost of installing drip tape for pepper cultivation depends on various factors, which are discussed below:

1. Drip tape type and quality

Drip tapes are manufactured in various types and quality levels. High-quality drip tapes typically have a longer lifespan and are more resistant to clogging and physical damage. However, they also carry a higher price. To select the best type of drip tape, one must consider the field conditions, soil type, and crop water requirements.

2. Drip tape dimensions and thickness

The dimensions and thickness of the drip tape also affect its cost. Drip tapes with a larger diameter and greater thickness are generally more expensive, but they can withstand higher water pressure and are better suited for irrigation in uneven terrain.

3. Emitter spacing

The spacing of emitters in the drip tape also affects its cost. Drip tapes with closer emitter spacing are typically more expensive, but they can distribute water more uniformly and are better suited for irrigating densely planted crops.

4. Market conditions and price fluctuations

Market conditions and price fluctuations can also impact the cost of drip tape. The price of raw materials, exchange rates, and market demand are among the factors that can influence drip tape pricing.

5. Installation and commissioning costs

Installation and commissioning costs for drip tape must also be considered in calculating the total cost of the drip irrigation system. These costs include labor, fittings, pipes, and other necessary equipment. To reduce installation and commissioning costs, one can utilize the services of companies specialized in this field.

6. Crop type and planting pattern

The crop type and planting pattern also affect the cost of drip tape. For example, in high-density planting, drip tape with closer emitter spacing is required, which increases the cost. Additionally, some crops require the use of specific drip tapes with different characteristics.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is the use of bubble tape suitable for all crops?

Bubble tape is suitable for many crops; however, for specific crops such as large trees, other irrigation methods may be required.

What is the service life of bubble tape?

The service life of bubble tape varies depending on quality, environmental conditions, and maintenance. Typically, high-quality bubble tapes last 3–5 years.

Can bubble tape be placed underground?

Yes, bubble tape can be installed underground. This method reduces water evaporation and prevents physical damage. For more details on this topic, you can refer to the article Subsurface irrigation of plants Please consult.

How can you prevent clogging in tape drippers?

To prevent clogging in tape drippers, use appropriate filters and flush the tape periodically. Using water-soluble fertilizers can also help prevent clogging.

Does using tape irrigation increase crop yield?

Yes, using tape irrigation optimizes water use and ensures uniform water distribution to plant roots, which increases growth and crop yield.

Pepper responses to deficit irrigation for sweet pepper production: Abstract

Pepper (Capsicum annuum L.) is typically grown under irrigation because it is highly sensitive to water stress. Deficit irrigation strategies for sweet peppers can increase production, facilitate mechanized harvest, and save water. We conducted a field experiment applying water deficit either during fruit ripening (T1) or throughout the season (T2) and compared these treatments to a fully irrigated control (T3).

Stem water potential ranged from -0.6 MPa in T3 at the beginning of the season to -1.5 MPa in T2 before harvest. Irrigation water applied to T1, T2, and T3 was 456, 346, and 480 mm, respectively. Water deficit reduced yield but did not affect the percentage of flowers that produced fruit. T2 fruit dry weight at harvest was 66% of T3, but there was no significant difference between T1 and T3.

However, commercial yield (based on color production) was significantly higher in T3 than in the other two treatments because late-season water deficit applied in T1 delayed harvest. We concluded that water deficit, whether continuous or during fruit ripening, is required for mechanized harvest but does not accelerate ripening and is detrimental to commercial yield. Pepper plants must be well-irrigated until harvest to achieve maximum sweet pepper production.

H-230 Guide: Chili Peppers in New Mexico

Chili peppers (Capsicum annuum) have been cultivated in New Mexico for at least four centuries. They are a cultural symbol and an essential part of New Mexico's identity. This crop is very important for the state; chili is not only a valued food but a deeply rooted tradition inseparable from New Mexico's food, economy, and culture, and it is also an important cash crop for producers.

Most chili peppers are grown under contract and sold to various separate processors depending on the chili type used. For example, green New Mexico-type chilies are peeled, then canned or frozen, and packaged whole or chopped. Red chilies are usually harvested at the red stage when ripe and semi-dry, and are further dried to full pods, flakes, or powder at the processor before packaging.

Paprika is a non-pungent or low-pungent red chili pepper that is usually very colorful. A large portion of the paprika crop is processed to produce paprika oleoresin, a red pigment, by extracting color pigments. When selecting a variety, consider factors such as flavor, heat level, yield, disease resistance, and suitability.

Land preparation and irrigation systems

A good crop rotation program is crucial for a profitable chili pepper crop, soil health, water use management, and pest and disease management. A three- to five-year rotation where chili peppers are planted once in a field and a cover crop is planted at least once has been beneficial for New Mexico producers. A sandy loam or loam soil with medium texture and good drainage is ideal for chili pepper production because it provides optimal water retention, nutrient availability, and drainage.

For drip-irrigated farms, subsurface drip irrigation (SDI) systems should be designed based on the farm's water quality to ensure efficiency and longevity. For farms with high-quality water, a permanent drip tape system can be installed, which typically lasts approximately 8 to 10 years with proper maintenance and high-quality materials. Conversely, farms with lower water quality – such as farms with high particulate matter or salinity – may require temporary drip tape, which is generally used for one growing season.

Regardless of whether the system is temporary or permanent, a 30 cm spacing between drippers is generally recommended for optimal water distribution. For effective subsurface drip irrigation, prepare a low bed with a maximum height of 7.5 cm and install the drip tape at a depth of 20 cm.
