Method for Planting Onions Using Drip Irrigation

Onion planting is one of the most important agricultural activities in many regions of Iran. Due to the water scarcity crisis and the need to improve water use efficiency, the adoption of drip-irrigated onion planting has become one of the best options for farmers. This method not only conserves water but also increases crop yield. This article provides a comprehensive overview of the steps and equipment required to implement this method.

Advantages of Planting Onions with Drip Irrigation

  1. Water Conservation: In this method, water is delivered directly to the plant roots, preventing waste.
  2. Increased Yield: The precise delivery of water and nutrients improves plant growth and increases production.
  3. Reduction of fungal diseases: Because leaf surfaces and soil remain dry, the likelihood of fungal disease outbreaks is reduced.
  4. Better fertilizer control: Using water-soluble fertilizers enables precise feeding during irrigation (fertigation).

Onion planting stages using a drip irrigation system

1. Field preparation for onion planting with drip irrigation

The first step in the planting method Onion With drip irrigation, proper field preparation is essential. At this stage, the field soil must be thoroughly plowed to ensure a loose and uniform soil texture. After plowing, leveling operations are necessary to prevent water accumulation in specific areas and to ensure uniform irrigation distribution.

To achieve optimal yield, it is recommended to conduct a soil test before planting. This test helps the farmer identify soil nutritional needs, including nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and organic matter, and develop a precise fertilization program tailored to field conditions.

Proper field preparation provides an ideal bed for onion root growth, which plays a crucial role in increasing yield and product quality.

2. Selecting onion variety and appropriate planting time

In onion cultivation with drip irrigation, selecting the appropriate seed or seedling type plays a vital role in crop success. Depending on the local climate conditions, various onion types such as yellow, white, or red onions can be used. Each variety has specific characteristics and should be selected based on market demand, disease resistance, and environmental conditions.
Key points in selecting seed or seedlings:

Select varieties that are resistant to common regional diseases and pests.

Consider the length of the growth cycle (early, mid, or late maturity).

Check germination quality and seed health.

Appropriate planting time:

In warm regions, the best time to plant onions is autumn; this ensures initial growth occurs at moderate temperatures and the bulbing period continues during the cooler season.

In cold or mountainous regions, onion planting is usually done in early spring to avoid winter frosts.

Appropriate planting timing minimizes thermal stress, maximizing yield and final product quality.

3. Design and Installation of Drip Irrigation Systems for Onion Planting

One of the most critical aspects of growing onions with drip irrigation is the proper design and implementation of the system. Selecting appropriate equipment based on soil type, water source, and pressure directly affects system efficiency.
a) Drip Tape

Drip tape is one of the most common and cost-effective drip irrigation methods for onion cultivation. These tapes feature emitters at specified intervals, distributing water uniformly along planting rows.

Advantages of drip tape:

Suitable for flat terrain and farms with regular, closely spaced planting rows.

Easy installation with lower costs compared to other systems.

Typically placed alongside or under planting rows; soil covering can prevent water evaporation.

Compatible with fertilizer injection (fertigation) systems.

b) Layflat Hose

Layflat hoses are used as main lines or secondary distribution lines in fields. They are usually installed at the field boundary, with drip tapes or drip lines branching off from them.

Features of Layflat Hose:

Durable structure capable of withstanding high pressures.

Lightweight, portable, and storable, making it easy to transport between different farms.

Resistant to sun exposure and environmental factors.

Easy to connect to filters, control valves, and other irrigation system components.

Accurate and standard installation of the drip irrigation system enhances uniform water distribution, reduces energy consumption, and prevents water loss during the growing season.

4. Sowing seeds or transplanting seedlings in a drip irrigation system

After land preparation and irrigation system installation, the next stage is sowing onion seeds or transplanting onion seedlings. In onion cultivation with drip irrigation, both direct seed sowing and seedling transplanting are common, but one method may be preferred depending on climatic conditions and the onion variety.

Sowing onion seeds

In arid regions, direct seeding is typically used. Seeds should be placed 2 to 3 cm deep in the soil to ensure sufficient moisture for germination while preventing rot or desiccation.

Transplanting onion seedlings

In areas with a short growing season or to accelerate production, seedling transplanting is an appropriate option. Seedlings should be planted so that the plant collar is at soil level and the roots are in good contact with moist soil.

Standard planting spacing

Row-to-row spacing: 30 to 40 cm, depending on the onion variety and farm mechanization type.

Plant-to-plant spacing within each row: 10 to 15 cm, to provide adequate growth space and prevent competition between plants.

These spacings ensure roots have sufficient room to grow and that the drip emitters can accurately supply the required moisture to each plant.

5. Irrigation system startup and fertilization schedule for drip-irrigated onion cultivation

In onion cultivation using drip irrigation, the irrigation system startup and fertilization schedule play a key role in plant health and proper growth. The main objective at this stage is to provide uniform moisture in the root zone and supply the nutrients required by the plant.
Irrigation based on plant needs

The amount and frequency of irrigation should be adjusted based on:

Plant growth stage,

Soil texture (light or heavy),

Air temperature and evaporation rate.

During the early growth stages of onions, the plants require higher moisture levels to establish themselves properly. As time passes and the roots develop, the irrigation frequency decreases while the volume per irrigation increases.
Fertilization through the irrigation system (fertigation)

One advantage of drip irrigation is the ability to inject water-soluble fertilizers through the irrigation system. This practice increases fertilizer efficiency and enhances root uptake. Gradual and regular fertilization throughout the growth season ensures uniform growth and larger onion bulbs.
Key points for maintaining the drip irrigation system:

Adequate filtration is essential to prevent clogging of nozzles and drippers. The use of screen or disk filters is recommended.

Regular inspection of lines and tape is crucial to ensure uniform water flow.

To optimize water usage and precisely control soil moisture, soil moisture sensors, automatic valves, and smart irrigation systems can be utilized.

Proper execution of this stage has a direct impact on final crop quality, uniform onion size, and the prevention of water stress.
