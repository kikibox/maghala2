Types of Drip Tape and Advantages of Each

In modern agriculture, the use of drip tape is one of the most effective methods for precise and optimal irrigation. Drip tapes allow farmers to reduce water consumption and improve crop yields. This article examines types of drip tape and the advantages of each, along with considerations such as selecting the correct irrigation tape for various soil types and farming practices.

In-line Drip Tape

One of the most common types of irrigation tape is in-line drip tape used in agricultural projects requiring one-sided irrigation. These tapes are designed with water outlet holes located on one side to distribute water uniformly along one side of the field.

Advantages of welded lateral tape:

  • Uniform irrigation on one side of the field
  • Suitable for sloping fields that require water distribution in one direction
  • Resistant to water and soil as well as pressure
  • Reduced water consumption using precision irrigation methods

This tape is suitable for crops requiring one-sided irrigation, such as certain vegetables or crops planted in specific rows.

Disc emitter irrigation tape

Disc emitter irrigation tape is another option that uses specific discs for precise control of water discharge. These tapes are designed for large farms requiring precise and uniform irrigation.

Advantages of disc emitter tape:

  • Precise discharge regulation using discs
  • Reduction in water loss Due to the uniform and regulated distribution of water
  • Suitable for irrigation in Large-scale farms
  • Resistant to high pressures and harsh environmental conditions
  • Adjustable spacing between plates for Variety in irrigation methods

This type of belt is suitable for large-scale crops such as grains, citrus fruits, or other large agricultural products that require precise control of water consumption.

Comparison of drip line types

Considering the soil type, irrigation requirements, and crop type, selecting the appropriate drip line is crucial. This section provides a comparison table between sewn lateral lines and plastic plate lines.

Feature Sewn lateral irrigation line Plastic plate irrigation line
Design Type One-sided holes Plates for water flow control
Water Distribution Uniform on one side of the field Uniform and precise across the entire drip line
Suitable for Sloped land and one-sided cultivation Large farms requiring precise irrigation
Resistance Resistant to pressure and environmental conditions Resistant to pressure and harsh environmental conditions
Irrigation One-sided (irrigation on one side) Uniform and precise at all points along the drip line

Key points in selecting the appropriate drip line

  1. Land type: If your land is sloped and requires one-sided irrigation, a sewn-side drip line may be a suitable choice. For large, flat farms requiring precise and uniform irrigation, a plate-type drip line is better.
  2. Crop type: Moisture-sensitive crops requiring precise irrigation need plate-type drip lines. In contrast, for row crops and plants requiring irrigation on only one side, sewn-side drip lines are more suitable.
  3. Water pressure level: In areas with low water pressure, plate-type drip lines can help ensure uniform water distribution. Sewn-side drip lines are also efficient under such conditions.
  4. Cost: Seamless lateral tapes are usually cheaper than plaque lateral tapes, making them a suitable option for farmers with limited budgets.
