What should the drip tape spacing be for bean cultivation? ✅

In bean cultivation, the spacing of drip tape is directly related to the planting rows. The most suitable spacing for installing drip tape in row conditions is generally 50 centimeters as this distance ensures uniform water distribution and adequate nutrient supply to the roots. However, in areas where beans are cultivated in blocks, using a square arrangement with organized drip tape design can significantly increase irrigation efficiency and yield. In this article, we examine more precise details regarding optimal spacing selection and drip tape installation models. 🌱

Drip irrigation is one of the modern methods of localized irrigation (under pressure) that increases water use efficiency by delivering water gradually and uniformly to the plant roots. This system is known as drip irrigation due to the intermittent flow of water. One of the most common and cost-effective sub-branches of this system is irrigation with drip tape .

In recent years, drip irrigation with tape has been widely used in farms across Iran, especially in Planting legumes such as beans has proven to be highly successful. The main reasons for this success are high efficiency, low water consumption, low cost, and uniform water distribution across the field.

✅ Benefits of bean cultivation using drip line

  • Significant water savings: Using drip irrigation can reduce water consumption by up to 50% compared to conventional methods.
  • Reduced energy consumption: This system operates at low pressure and therefore does not require high-powered, high-consumption pumps.
  • Increased yield per hectare: By precisely meeting the water needs of beans, a harvest of up to approximately 6 tons per hectare can be achieved.
  • Reduced water losses: Only the root zone is wetted, and evaporation and runoff are minimized.
  • Reduced fungal and bacterial diseases: Because leaves and stems remain dry, the favorable environment for pathogen growth is eliminated.
  • Easy injection of fertilizers and pesticides: With the help of a fertilizer tank next to the system filter, plant nutrition and protection are performed easily and with precision.
  • Reduced weed growth: Only the required section is irrigated, while other parts of the field remain dry.
  • Reducing irrigation frequency: Due to direct water distribution in the root zone, the intervals between irrigation cycles increase.

🔍 Comparison of irrigation methods in meeting the water requirements of beans

The net water requirement for beans is approximately 7,000 to 8,000 cubic meters per hectare . To deliver this amount of water to the plants, different volumes are required for each irrigation method:

  • 🌊 Flood irrigation: approximately 15,200 cubic meters
  • 🌧️ Sprinkler irrigation: approximately 9,200 to 10,000 cubic meters
  • 💧 Drip irrigation (tape drip): approximately 8,300 to 8,500 cubic meters

Importance of drip tape quality in drip irrigation

In drip irrigation systems, drip tape quality It plays a critical role in the performance and longevity of the system. Using high-quality tape lines and employing an appropriate filtration system can prevent issues such as emitter clogging, solar burnout, and reduced efficiency.

Drip tape lines come in various types, and diverse options can be used depending on cropping conditions and crop type:

  • Seamless tape drip line: The most economical option for open-field farms and row crops.
  • Plate-style tape drip line: Suitable for greenhouses and controlled environments with uniform operating pressure.
  • Extended plate-style tape drip line: Recommended for systems requiring precise and uniform water distribution along the line.

In many farms, seamless tape drip lines are used to reduce implementation costs. However, in greenhouse cultivation or fields requiring higher precision in water distribution, Plate-style tape drip line is a better choice.

Standard bean row spacing for drip irrigation

Based on research and field experience of farmers, a 50-centimeter distance between rows and 5 centimeters between plants within the rowis considered the standard bean planting pattern for row cropping. In this pattern, the drip tape is also installed with the same spacing (50 centimeters) and positioned next to each planting row.

Plot or row planting?

Although beans are generally a row-crop plant, they are also planted in plots in some regions of the country. In plot conditions, using a square arrangement can yield the best seed performance. to provide. However, row cultivation, due to agricultural benefits such as easier control of pests and diseases, better soil aeration, and the ability to use modern irrigation systems (such as drip), is a considerably more optimal option.

Suitable soil for bean planting

Beans require light soil rich in organic matter and with good aeration. The best soil type for this crop is sandy-loam soil that, while retaining moisture, possesses high permeability . These characteristics promote better root growth and prevent rot or oxygen deficiency.

Best time for drip irrigation of beans

The best time to irrigate is when the soil has lost its heat. Therefore, the hours of 5 to 6 AM are considered the best time for bean irrigation. At these times, evaporation intensity is minimized, and water is more effectively available to the plant roots.

Bean irrigation frequency (how often?)

In bean cultivation using drip tape, the intervals between irrigations depend on various factors such as:

  • Bean variety
  • Evaporation intensity and air temperature
  • Soil type and texture (permeability level)

It depends. However, the following pattern is applicable for most regions:

  1. One irrigation cycle Immediately after planting
  2. In the first week After planting, the soil must be kept continuously moist for proper seed germination
  3. After seedlings emerge, every 6 to 8 days irrigation is performed
  4. In some regions, such as Lorestan, this interval may be every 4 days instead
  5. At the booting stage where the plant is highly sensitive to water stress, irrigation should be every 3 to 5 days once
  6. 10 days before harvest, irrigation is stopped to allow the plant to ripen for harvest

Key points in bean drip irrigation

  • The flowering and pod formation stage, is the most critical part of plant growth. In this stage, the plant must not experience water stress.
  • In the weeks approaching harvest, the amount of irrigation must be reduced. When approximately 25% of the pods have turned yellow, it is time to reduce or stop irrigation.
  • The bean irrigation method should be frequent but light . That is, the root zone should remain moist without saturating the soil.
  • This light irrigation can be effectively achieved only with drip tape .

Why should drip irrigation be used for bean cultivation?

Plant Beans are highly sensitive to direct sunlight and intense heat . This crop is typically planted during the hot season, which increases the likelihood of heat stress . Specifically, during the flowering and podding stages, which coincide with peak heat, beans become more susceptible to water and heat stress .

Damage to beans caused by heat and water stress

  • At temperatures above 35 degrees Celsius the plant loses its tolerance.
  • Flowers and pods begin to shed.
  • In many cases, only 1 or 2 pods remain on each plant.
  • After flower and pod shedding, yield recoverability is very low.

Challenges of Conventional Irrigation Methods

  • Surface Irrigation: Uneven land topography prevents proper water distribution.
  • Sprinkler Irrigation: Wind drifts water to areas of the field with no crops.
  • Both methods induce water stress and reduce crop yield.

Solution: Drip Irrigation with Drip Lines

Drip irrigation using drip lines is the most precise and efficient irrigation method for bean crops. In this method:

  • Water is delivered directly to the root zone.
  • Fields can be irrigated at any desired time.
  • Water stress is significantly reduced and plant performance is improved.
  • Water use is optimized and efficiency is very high.

When is drip irrigation not suitable?

The use of drip irrigation may be limited in certain conditions. In particular, fields with high soil and water salinity cannot utilize this method. The reason is the accumulation of salts and minerals along the drip irrigation path , which can damage the irrigation system and lead to system failure .

What steps should be taken to prevent this issue?

  • Before installing the drip irrigation system, measure the water salinity level .
  • If there is no salinity in the water or soil, drip irrigation is recommended as the best method.
  • Under such conditions, drip irrigation can help maximize bean yield.

Cost of bean planting with drip tape

To estimate the cost of planting beans using drip tape, various factors must be considered. These factors may include the type of previous irrigation system, the amount of equipment required, and operational costs.

Cost estimation for farmers who previously used flood irrigation

If a farmer has previously used flood irrigation and now wishes to switch to drip irrigation, the following items must be considered:

  • Filtration system: The filtration system is one of the main costs in drip irrigation. Using a filtration system is essential for every hectare of land.
  • Drip tape: To irrigate each hectare of land, approximately 10 to 12 rolls of drip tape are required. Each roll of drip tape usually has a length of 1000 meters.
  • Main branch pipes: To connect the irrigation system to the pump, standard polyethylene pipes are required. For each hectare, usually about 250 to 260 meters of pipe is needed.
  • Sub-branch pipes: For sub-branches, farmers can use L-Flat, threaded, or foldable polyethylene pipes to reduce costs.
  • Fittings: In implementing a drip irrigation system, various fittings are required to connect the pipes to each other and ensure water reaches the plants properly.

Considering these costs and an efficiency of approximately 5 to 6 tons of beans per hectare, farmers will realize that implementing drip irrigation is far more profitable than older methods. In addition, standard polyethylene pipes usually come with a 10-year warranty, meaning farmers do not need to repurchase them in the following years.

Bean Harvesting Time

The sowing and harvesting time for beans depends on the climate region, variety type, and sowing date. From sowing to harvesting beans, it usually takes about 15 weeks. Most farmers sow beans in mid-spring, from late Esfand to the second week of Farvardin, and the crop is harvested at the beginning of autumn.

However, harvesting time also depends on the bean variety. In some varieties, pods do not ripen simultaneously, and harvesting is done when approximately 70 percent of the pods are ripe. Upright varieties are usually harvested more uniformly, while climbing varieties may have pods ripening at different times.
