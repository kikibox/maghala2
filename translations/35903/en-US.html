Key Considerations Before Installing Drip Tape in the Field

Quality of Water Used

The water used for drip irrigation systems must be examined in detail. One important point is that the water should not contain solid particles larger than 100 microns. Larger particles can cause clogging of drip tape emitters and reduce irrigation system performance. Therefore, it is best to use appropriate filters to remove suspended particles. Filters with a mesh size of 120 or 150 microns can be suitable for this purpose.

Field Preparation

Before installing drip tape, the land must be fully prepared. The field should be plowed, and large clods and coarse debris that could damage the tape must be removed from the surface. Additionally, the surface must be free of any stones, roots, or foreign objects to ensure correct installation of the drip tape and uniform water flow across all sections of the field.

Levelness of the ground surface

The ground surface must be completely level, and the slope should not exceed one percent. Otherwise, water distribution will not be uniform, and reverse siphoning may occur, meaning water flows back from the drip tape into the system. This can damage the irrigation system and cause water loss. If a significant slope exists, the land must be graded and corrected to ensure optimal performance from the drip irrigation system.

Direction Drip tape installation

Irrigation tapes must be installed perpendicular to the ground surface and the path of water flow. This ensures that water flow is distributed uniformly along the length of the tape and optimizes system performance. The installation of irrigation tapes must be carried out carefully and in accordance with technical principles to prevent subsequent problems.

Flushing pipes before connecting drip tape

Before connecting the drip tapes to the main irrigation system pipes, the pipes must be thoroughly flushed. Main and sub-main pipes may contain suspended particles, sediments, or other contaminants that can transfer into the drip tapes and clog the emitters. To flush the pipes, the irrigation system should be turned on, and the end-line caps must be opened to allow water to flow completely through the pipes, expelling any sediments or excess particles.

Irrigation system design and technical calculations

The layout of main and sub-main pipes, manifolds, pumping stations, and other system components must be designed precisely and tailored to the site conditions. This design requires specific technical calculations that should be performed by experienced experts. Key parameters in these calculations include appropriate water pressure and flow rate, pipe lengths, pumping station location, and the number of drip tapes.

Consulting experts to determine drip tape length

Determining the length of the drip tape should be done in consultation with experts. The selection of an appropriate tape length significantly impacts water distribution uniformity and system efficiency. Experts will recommend the optimal drip tape length based on field area, crop type, plant water requirements, and other factors to ensure the best system performance.

Using thicker drip tape for sub-surface irrigation

If using drip tape for sub-surface irrigation, you must use tapes with thicker gauge These tapes offer greater durability at soil depth and protect against physical damage or corrosion caused by soil contact. Additionally, thicker drip tapes are generally more resistant to pressure and external damage.

Drip tape temperature tolerance

Drip irrigation tapes must be capable of operating within a wide temperature range of -70 to +70 degrees Celsius. This feature is particularly important for regions with extreme temperature fluctuations or tropical areas with temperatures exceeding 40 degrees Celsius. Therefore, you should use tapes that meet the necessary standards for high-temperature tolerance.

For installing drip tape emitters

Drip tape emitters should be installed facing upward to ensure uniform water distribution along the entire tape. Installing drip tape upside down or with incorrect orientation can cause uneven water distribution and result in reduced system performance.

Suitability of drip tape for flat surfaces

Using drip irrigation tape on flat land is generally appropriate. However, for uneven and sloping terrain, installing drip tape can present challenges and may require specific adjustments or alternative techniques, such as subsurface drip irrigation. Therefore, before installing a drip irrigation system, ensure you assess the site's characteristics and seek technical advice if necessary.

Conclusion

Installing a drip irrigation tape system is a complex process that requires high attention to detail in selecting and correctly installing the system. Adhering to the technical and operational points outlined in this article can help optimize irrigation system performance and prevent potential issues. Consulting with specialists and experts in system design and installation, along with the proper selection of equipment and materials, is crucial for achieving desired results.
