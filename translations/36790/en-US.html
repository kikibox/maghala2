What is the yield of camelina per hectare?

Camelina sativa, an oilseed crop with a short growth period, has attracted significant attention from farmers due to its high potential for biofuel production and low input requirements. This plant, also known as “False Flax” , can perform well under various climatic conditions and is considered a sustainable option for oil and biofuel production. This article comprehensively examines the yield of camelina per hectare, the factors influencing it, and methods to increase it. Given the objectives set in national biofuel policies, utilizing the potential of camelina can play a crucial role in achieving these goals (ScienceDirect, 2021). Therefore, a precise understanding of this crop and its optimal cultivation methods is of great importance to farmers.

Camelina Sativa: A High-Potential Crop

Camelina is an annual or winter crop from the mustard family, native to northern Europe. This crop is distinguished by its unique characteristics, including:

  • High oil content (30-40%)
  • Short growth period (85-100 days)
  • Drought and cold resistance
  • Resistance to common pests

Camelina is recognized as a valuable crop in various regions worldwide (AGMRC, n.d.). Camelina oil is used not only for food but also in biodiesel production and as a food supplement due to its high stability and favorable ratio of omega-3 to omega-6 fatty acids.

 

Camelina yield per hectare

Camelina yield per hectare varies depending on multiple factors, including cultivar, weather conditions, soil type, planting methods, and farm management. Under rainfed conditions, the average yield of camelina is between 1,000 and 1,700 pounds per hectare (approximately 1,120 to 1,900 kg/ha) (AGMRC, n.d.). However, yields in commercial farms are generally reported to be between 800 and 1,000 pounds per hectare (approximately 900 to 1,120 kg/ha).

Factors affecting camelina yield per hectare

To achieve maximum camelina yield per hectare, attention to the following factors is essential:

  • Selection of appropriate cultivar: Different camelina cultivars have varying yield potentials. It is important to select a cultivar compatible with the regional weather and soil conditions.
  • Planting date: Early spring planting gives the plant more time for growth and development and can lead to increased yield. Studies indicate that delayed planting can reduce the yield potential of camelina by up to 25%.
  • Weed management: Weeds can compete with camelina plants for water and nutrients, leading to yield reduction. The use of appropriate weed control methods, including herbicides or mechanical methods, is necessary.
  • Irrigation: Although kale is drought-tolerant, supplemental irrigation during periods of low rainfall can help increase yield.
  • Fertilization: Supplying the nutrients required by the plant, particularly nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, can help improve growth and increase yield.

Methods to increase kale harvest volume

In addition to considering factors affecting yield, using the following methods can also help increase the per-hectare harvest of kale:

  • Crop rotation: Planting kale in rotation with other crops, such as cereals and legumes, can help improve soil structure and reduce the incidence of pests and diseases.
  • Row planting: Row planting, instead of broad-bed planting, can help improve soil aeration and provide better plant access to sunlight.
  • Use of dripline: The use of dripline in drip irrigation is an effective method for conserving water and increasing irrigation efficiency.

The role of drip irrigation with dripline in increasing kale yield

Drip irrigation with driplineis one of the modern irrigation methods that has attracted the attention of many farmers in recent years due to its numerous benefits. By delivering water directly to the plant roots, this method prevents water loss and increases irrigation efficiency. Using dripline in kale cultivation can have the following benefits:

  • Water conservation: Drip irrigation with tape lines prevents surface water evaporation by delivering water directly to the plant roots, thereby conserving water.
  • Yield increase: Continuous and uniform supply of water to meet plant requirements improves growth and development, ultimately resulting in increased yield.
  • Weed reduction: Drip irrigation with tape lines prevents weed growth in other parts of the field by moistening only the plant root zone.
  • Disease reduction: Preventing plant leaves from getting wet reduces the incidence of fungal diseases.
  • Crop quality improvement: Adequate supply of water and nutrients improves the quality of oil and other compounds in camelina seeds.

Can Camellia seed cake be used for animal feed?

Camellia seed cake has similar nutritional value to soybean meal, containing 35-40% protein, 6-12% oil, 6-7% ash, and 41% neutral detergent fiber. It can be used as a feed ingredient in the diets of ruminants (beef cattle and goats) and monogastrics (poultry and pigs).
