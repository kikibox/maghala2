Selecting Agricultural Drip Tape

Selecting the correct irrigation drip tape is crucial for increasing efficiency and productivity in drip irrigation systems. This article outlines key considerations for choosing agricultural drip tape to help you select the best option for your farm.Types of Irrigation Tape and Their Functions

Tape Length

When selecting drip tape length, consider your field area. The length should be sufficient to water all sections of your field uniformly and prevent water waste.Technical Specifications of Irrigation Drip Tape

  • For larger fields, using tapes with greater length is recommended.
  • Remember that drip tape length must be appropriate for the distance between water sources and irrigation points.

Working Pressure Rating

The water pressure in the irrigation system must be compatible with your selected drip tape. Excessively high or low pressure can cause irregular water distribution and affect the performance of the drip tape.

  • Before purchasing drip tape, measure the pressure of your water supply system.
  • Different drip tapes have varying pressure tolerances. Therefore, you must choose one that is compatible with your site's water pressure.

Thickness of Agricultural Drip Tape

The thickness of the drip tape significantly impacts its service life. Thicker tapes may offer greater resistance to physical damage but come at a higher cost.Ideal Wall Thickness of Irrigation Drip Tape

  • For dry and hot environments, thicker drip tapes are recommended.
  • For humid environments or fields with sparse vegetation, thinner tapes are sufficient.

Irrigation Uniformity

One of the key features of drip tape is its ability to distribute water uniformly. This is especially important on uneven or sloped terrain. To ensure uniform irrigation, select a high-quality drip tape that spreads water evenly across the entire field surface.

  • Ensure that your selected drip tape features uniform and high-density emitters.
  • Using drip tapes with regular emitter spacing can help improve irrigation uniformity.

Length of Drip Tape Roll

Drip tapes are typically marketed in 1000, 1500, and 3000-meter lengths. The appropriate length depends on your field size. Generally, longer rolls are more suitable for larger fields.

  • For small to medium-sized fields, 1000-meter or 1500-meter rolls may be suitable.
  • For large fields, 3000-meter rolls can minimize the number of connections required and reduce installation time.

Drip tape inner diameter

The inner diameter of drip tape has a significant impact on water flow rate and uniform distribution. Selecting the appropriate diameter helps ensure that water pressure is correctly distributed throughout the entire tape.

  • In high-pressure irrigation systems, tapes with larger inner diameters may be more efficient.
  • If the water supply system pressure is low, you should use tapes with smaller inner diameters.

Emitter or hole spacing

The spacing between holes or emitters on drip tape must be adjusted according to the type of crop being grown and its irrigation needs. Emitter spacing has a strong impact on irrigation efficiency.

  • For crops with higher water requirements, such as watermelon, a 30-centimeter spacing is suitable.
  • For crops such as beans, which have lower water needs, a 20-centimeter spacing may be appropriate.

By considering these points, you can select the appropriate drip tape for your farmland and achieve high productivity and water savings.

Drip tape problems

When using drip tape, you may encounter problems such as clogging. We have reviewed the causes and solutions for these issues below:

Causes of drip tape clogging Solutions
Clogging due to salts and water salinity
  • Monitoring water salinity and salts
  • Using low-salinity water
  • Adjusting water pressure and flow rate so that salts dissolve properly
Deposition of suspended solids inside the tape
  • Use appropriate filters to remove algae and suspended solids
  • Prevent suspended solids from entering the drip line
Reverse siphonage and ingress of silt into the tip
  • Install filters to prevent reverse siphonage
  • Control and properly manage the water pumping and suction system

With these corrective measures, you can prevent clogging issues in drip irrigation lines and improve irrigation efficiency.
