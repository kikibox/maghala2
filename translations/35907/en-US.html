Using water of appropriate quality

For irrigation, use non-saline and non-hard water. Saline and hard water containing high amounts of salts and minerals can clog drip tape emitters and reduce system efficiency. This shortens the useful life of the drip tape and significantly decreases system performance. Soft, salt-free water is a better option and can extend the system's useful life.

Using water free from suspended particles

Water used in a drip irrigation system must be free from suspended particles and contaminants. Suspended particles such as soil, sand, and organic matter can clog dripper emitters, resulting in blockages within the irrigation system. To prevent this issue, using an appropriate filter to treat the water before it enters the irrigation system is essential.

Fertilizer and pesticide injection

When injecting fertilizer or pesticide into a drip irrigation system, sufficient time must be allocated for system flushing and draining. To prevent chemical mixing with irrigation water and potential damage to plants, the water flow should be gradually shut off, and overuse of chemicals should be avoided. After injection, the system must be flushed for an adequate period with clean water to ensure no residue of fertilizer or pesticide remains in the system.

Precise Irrigation Planning

The water requirement of plants must be calculated and planned precisely. Irrigation must be managed so that plants receive an adequate amount of water, neither more nor less. Accurate calculation of water needs based on plant type, growth stage, climatic conditions, and soil properties is essential. This planning must ensure that all water requirements of the plants are met.

Weed Control and Soil Surface Maintenance

For weed control around the irrigation system, the use of herbicides is recommended. Additionally, sharp or heavy objects must be kept out of the soil to prevent damage to the irrigation system. Sharp objects may puncture the drip tape, leading to water leaks or reduced system efficiency. Therefore, maintaining the soil surface and preventing physical damage to the irrigation system is of great importance.

Proper Transportation of Drip Tape

Irrigation drip tapes and drip hoses must be transported with care. Avoid dragging or twisting them to prevent damage to the tape structure and reduction in efficiency. Proper transportation of the drip tape helps maintain the quality and performance of the irrigation system.

Precision in Drip Tape Installation

When Installing Drip Tape For drip irrigation, any scratching or twisting of the tape must be avoided. Scratches can easily damage the system and reduce water pressure within the tape. The drip tape must be installed straight, without any bending or extra pressure, to ensure optimal system performance.

System Stability in the Field

In the crop bed, no sharp objects should be present, as they may damage the irrigation tape and cause leaks or blockages in the system . In fields containing stones or hard objects, it is better to carefully inspect and clean the soil before installation to prevent potential damage.

Compliance with Appropriate Pressure in the Irrigation System

Water pressure in the drip tape must strictly adhere to established standards. Excessive pressure can cause the tape to burst or increase water consumption, while low pressure can lead to insufficient irrigation and uneven water distribution. The pressure should generally not exceed 1 atmosphere, as high pressure will damage the system.

Installing Drip Tape on or Under the Soil Surface

Drip irrigation tape can be installed on the surface or at a soil depth of 5 to 15 cm. In areas with the risk of snow and hail, it is recommended to install subsoil type tape to prevent damage. Subsoil installation helps protect the system from adverse weather conditions and extends the system's useful life.

Drip tape stability in windy areas

In areas with strong winds, drip irrigation tape installation should be carried out with greater care. Using plastic stakes or installing the drip tape subsoil can prevent displacement. The drip tape must be firmly anchored in the ground to prevent damage to the tape and the irrigation system.
