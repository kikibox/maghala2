Why is 3-inch (75 mm) collapsible pipe a cost-effective option for farms?

In recent years, farmers and irrigation engineers have been seeking solutions that are optimal in terms of both initial cost and installation speed. 3-inch (75 mm) collapsible pipe Due to its standard dimensions and high flexibility, it has become one of the most common choices for water transmission and distribution lines in medium-to-large farms. Unlike rigid metal pipes, this type of pipe can be twisted and installed on uneven and sloped terrain without the need for deep trenching. This feature significantly reduces land preparation costs and system setup time. Below, you will learn about the specific and specialized application of this pipe in drip and trickle irrigation systems.

Application in drip irrigation systems

Drip irrigation systems require a strong and balanced water transmission network. 3-inch (75 mm) collapsible pipe is generally used as the mainline or lateral line in vegetable fields, greenhouses, and fruit orchards. The internal diameter of this pipe is designed to minimize pressure loss along the line, especially when the system operates at medium pressure. Use in drip strip irrigation

In drip strip irrigation, a hybrid method combining drip and strip irrigation, the 3-inch (75 mm) flexible hose plays a key role in conveying water from the main source to lateral strips. The flexibility of this hose enables installation in fields with varying soil densities and slopes without interfering with agricultural equipment traffic. Technical and material specifications of the 3-inch flexible hose

For accurate selection, you must understand the technical specifications of the hose you have or intend to purchase. The 3-inch (75 mm) flexible hose is typically made from linear low-density polyethylene (LLDPE) or durable plastic compounds that resist calcium and chloride deposits. The wall thickness of these hoses is generally defined based on working pressure (PSI). For standard farms, hoses with a working pressure of 8 to 12 bar are sufficient, but high-pressure systems may require thicker hoses.

Comparison with rigid pipes (PE and PPR)

Many people confuse flexible hoses with rigid HDPE pipes (PE) or PPR. The main difference lies in rigidity and cuttability. Rigid pipes require connection with rigid fittings and nuts, whereas flexible hoses are sealed using Faston or Mount connections, which increases installation speed. In terms of cost, flexible hoses are generally cheaper, but their service life may be shorter than high-density HDPE pipes unless surface protection is used. Practical Installation and Field Application Tips

Installing a 3-inch (75 mm) corrugated pipe requires adhering to several golden rules. Following these guidelines prevents costly repair expenses and crop damage.

  1. Main Line Sizing: Lay the pipe along the center of the field or at an appropriate distance from the crop rows. If the field is sloped, position the pipe on the downslope side to follow the gradient.
  2. Using Structure and Tension Wire: Do not lay the pipe directly on dry, rocky soil without a protective plastic mat. This prevents abrasion to the pipe caused by stones.
  3. Proper Connections: When securing the couplings, ensure that the pipe is fully inserted into the fitting to prevent sudden failure. Use crimp connectors or dedicated clamps.
  4. Flow rate calculation: Never allow the input flow rate to exceed the pipe's capacity. A flow velocity exceeding 2 meters per second will cause water hammer and rupture of the collapsible pipe.
  5. Protection from direct sunlight: If the pipe is not buried underground but rests on the soil, use black plastic covering or apply a thin layer of soil to prevent UV degradation.

It is also recommended to regularly flush the system at the ends of lateral drip lines. Sediment pollution accumulates in collapsible pipes during dry seasons, which can cause narrowing in thinner pipe sections at connection points. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about 3-inch collapsible pipes

Are 3-inch collapsible pipes suitable for high pressures?

Yes, but with caution. Collapsible pipes are generally designed for standard farm pressures (5 to 10 bar). For higher pressures, it is recommended to use rigid SDR8 or SDR11 pipes, as the wall of collapsible pipes is not designed to withstand pressure pulses above 10 bar and may thin out or rupture over time.

How long can I leave collapsible pipes on the soil surface?

The useful life of collapsible pipes on soil (without underground protection) is between 3 to 5 years. After this period, the plastic's integrity degrades and UV sensitivity increases. For long-term use, the pipe must be buried underground or protected with a UV-resistant cover (such as thick soil or heavy black plastic).

What is the difference between a 3-inch collapsible pipe and a drip tape?

These two are completely different. A 3-inch collapsible pipe is for transporting high volumes of water (main or submain line). Drip tape is a thinner hose with drippers or small holes installed to distribute water in a narrow band. The collapsible pipe must be installed before the drip tape to deliver water to the tapes.

If the flexible hose gets punctured, how should I repair it?

For small holes (up to 1 cm), use patches specifically designed for plastic hoses (such as Termir). Clean and dry the area, apply the special adhesive, and firmly attach the patch; do not apply pressure for 24 hours. For larger holes, cut out the damaged section and add a coupling or mounting bracket, connecting it with a short 20 cm piece of hose.
