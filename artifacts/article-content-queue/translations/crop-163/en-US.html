Mung bean, a legume crop with a shallow root system and high sensitivity to water stress during seed fill, requires the intelligent management of drip irrigation systems. In this guide, we cover all technical aspects, from spacing theory to field operations, enabling you to confidently adjust your drip tape layout.

Introduction to the Importance of Drip Tape Layout in Mung Bean

Drip tape systems have gained significant popularity due to their lower cost compared to permanent drip systems and their ability to be collected and recycled after the end of the cropping season. However, this system requires high precision in the distance of tapes from the row center and the spacing between adjacent tapes. The primary goal is to create a coherent wetted front that encompasses the mung bean root zone. If the spacing between tapes is too wide, resulting dry gaps will cause water stress during the pod fill stage. If the spacing is too narrow, water is wasted in non-root zones, increasing system costs.

Technical Standards for Drip Tape Spacing in Mung Bean

Determining the precise drip tape spacing depends on variables such as soil type, mung bean root depth, and solar radiation intensity. Nevertheless, general standards for temperate and semi-arid regions of Iran are as follows:

  • Distance of tape from row center: A spacing of 5 to 10 cm is generally recommended. A 10 cm distance is ideal for better seed germination and prevention of stem fungal diseases in high humidity near the base of the plant. Spacings of less than 5 cm may cause plant death due to root asphyxiation in clay soils.
  • Single-line vs. dual-line configuration: For cabbage rows spaced 60 to 70 cm apart, using a single drip line in the center or two lateral lines spaced 10–15 cm from the row center is more common. In a dual-line system, the distance between the two lines should be such that the wet zones of the two lines almost touch each other.

Practical Installation and Maintenance Tips

Proper spacing execution should not remain on paper. In practice, using precision furrowers or guide strips helps you control the deviation of the strip from the center along the row. After planting and before the initial watering, ensure the drip tape is lying on the soil without kinking. Kinking alters flow velocity and creates weak points in the strip over the long term.

Note that the drip tape spacing for beans may vary slightly, as beans differ from mung beans in fine details regarding root structure and water requirements. However, the general principles of drip tape spacing management for bush vegetables are shared and can serve as a good guide for other crops.

Pressure Management and Manifold Line Design

Aligning drip tape spacing is linked to the correct design of the manifold network. If manifold lines flow in parallel and at the same pressure, water distribution along the strip will be uniform. Otherwise, the end section of the strip will be dry and the initial section will be wet, causing asymmetric mung bean growth in the row.

  1. Pressure drop calculation: As the field length increases, pressure at the end of the mainline decreases. To compensate, increase the diameter of the mainline pipe or design shorter parallel lines.
  2. Strip tube perforation: Modern strip tubes feature emitters with a flow rate of 27 liters per hour. This rate is suitable for rice at the vegetative stage, but you may need to increase water volume during the grain-filling stage. In this case, do not change the strip spacing; instead, adjust the irrigation duration.

Impact of strip spacing on yield quality and quantity

Research has shown that optimizing drip tape spacing in mung bean cultivation can save up to 15 percent in water usage while maintaining or even increasing yield per hectare. When the tape spacing is optimal, roots fully cover the wetted zone created by the tape, eliminating the need to search for water at deeper, non-wetted levels. This directs plant energy toward flowering and pod development instead of producing fine root hairs.

Additionally, selecting an appropriate spacing allows for more precise fertilization (Fertigation). If the tapes are too far apart, nitrogenous fertilizers will leach due to their high mobility. If they are too close, the accumulation of nutrients in a single spot can cause root burn in mung beans. Balancing the drip tape spacing creates a path for safe and effective fertilization.

In farms practicing intercropping, or if you are using drip tapes for other vegetables such as lettuce or cucumber, familiarizing yourself with other spacings helps you understand general patterns. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. Is it possible to change the tape spacing after planting?

No, changing the tape distance from the row center after planting is practically impossible, as the tape must be positioned directly under the shade canopy and within the root zone. Therefore, this decision must be specified and fixed on the planting map before sowing.

2. What is the standard spacing for sandy soils?

Due to the high permeability of sandy soil, the tapes should be placed farther from the row center (approximately 15–20 centimeters) to create a wider wetted volume and prevent rapid water loss.

3. What is the role of water salinity in drip tape spacing?

For saline water, engineers typically recommend using tapes with a higher flow rate and maintaining the same physical spacing, while applying a higher volume of water for leaching at the end of the season. The physical distance of the tape from the stem does not fundamentally change in these conditions, but the system flow rate is adjusted.
