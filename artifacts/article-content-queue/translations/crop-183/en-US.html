Introduction: The Critical Importance of Drip Line Maintenance in Cotton Cultivation

Because cotton is highly sensitive to water stress, irrigation systems require precision and reliability. Drip tape, as a cost-effective and efficient drip irrigation method, has become a standard component on cotton farms. However, one of the primary challenges farmers face is the proper maintenance of these lines. An unmonitored drip tape can become clogged, develop uneven pressure, and ultimately lead to reduced pollination and lower cotton fiber quality. This article examines the technical and practical details of maintaining drip tape for cotton plants to ensure your system operates at optimal performance.

Filtration Systems and Their Role in Preventing Clogging

The most critical factor for drip tape integrity is adequate filtration of the incoming water. Cotton is typically grown in clay-loam or loam soils, which can result in suspended solids entering the tape channels. To prevent this issue, the use of multi-stage filters (Disc, Screen, Sand/Media) is recommended.

  • Disc Filter: Used to remove silt and sand particles. This filter serves as the first line of defense and should be cleaned once a month.
  • Screen Filter: Used to remove finer particles. If sourcing water from a well, always use a filter with a 120-150 mesh size.
  • Silt Filter: If your water source is a pond or a river, a silt filter is essential for removing silt and debris.

Remember that a filter only works if it is properly maintained. Excessive pressure on the filter can cause it to burst, allowing all particles to enter the drip tape. Water Pressure Management and Hydraulic Settings

Cotton drip tape systems typically operate with mainline pipe diameters of 2.5 to 4 inches and tape diameters of 16 or 25 mm. The ideal operating pressure for cotton drip tapes is between 1 and 3 bar. Pressures above this range cause unstable washing of the tape and increase surface soil erosion, while lower pressures lead to flow stoppage at the ends of laterals.

Connection Inspection and Flexibility

Drip tape connections to the mainline hose must be completely tight but not stretched. Using appropriate couplings and metal clamps (not plastic ones) in areas where livestock or machinery may pass through extends the lifespan of the connections. Additionally, install a check valve at the beginning of each mainline hose to prevent water from flowing back towards the pump, which can cause pump damage and blockages in the tape due to reverse sedimentation.

Utilizing fertigation and internal flushing

One of the key benefits of drip tape is the ability to perform fertigation. For cotton, the use of aggressive acids such as citric acid or phosphoric acid at low doses can help clean the inside of the emitters. This process should be conducted during the final days of each irrigation cycle to move flow from the start of the line to the end, removing deposited materials from the holes.

  1. Add the acid to the fertigation tank.
  2. Increase the flow rate to the maximum allowable limit (for flushing).
  3. Allow the acid to pass through the entire length of the line for 30 to 60 minutes.
  4. Return the flow rate to normal settings.

Additionally, cleaning the filter tank before each application is essential to prevent deposits from accumulating on the tape. Practical tips for physical maintenance of drip tape

Harvesting and moving the drip tape

Cotton is a long-season crop and may require replaceable (retractable) lateral hoses. When harvesting the tape, completely drain it. Never fold a water-filled tape, as this will tear the emitter holes and cause internal damage. Gather the tape on one side and fold it using a strap or a special bag.

Protection against animals and machinery

Mice and rodents are the main enemies of cotton drip tape. Using polyethylene protective covers or installing the tape below ground in vulnerable areas can prevent punctures. Also, when using a tractor for weeding or planting, ensure that the wheels do not hit the main line or the drip tape.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. Can drip tape be reused in subsequent seasons?
Yes, drip tapes with a thickness of 16.5 microns or greater and UV-resistant structures are typically reusable for 2-3 seasons. However, thinner tapes (less than 16.5 microns) may suffer damage during hot summers in Iran and last only one season.

2. What is the best time for acid flushing of the drip tape?
The best time is on the final days of the irrigation cycle or before completely shutting down the system. The flow must be at its maximum to ensure sufficient pressure inside the emitters to flush out sediment.

3. If the drip tape tears during the season, how should it be repaired?
Use specialized drip tape clamps (Tape Repair Clamp). These clamps have an inner plastic tube that tightly secures them. Cutting the tape completely or making a cut is not recommended unless the damage is extensive, as this causes uneven flow in that section.

4. Can fertigation be performed directly without filtration?
No, never. Directly injecting fertilizer from a separate tank into the drip tape without passing through the main filtration system poses a risk of immediate clogging of the dripper holes. Always inject fertilizer through the pump and the main filtration system.

5. How can I tell when the filter needs cleaning?
If the pressure drop between the filter inlet and outlet exceeds 50 centimeters of water column (approximately 0.5 bar), it indicates the filter is clogged and needs cleaning.

Conclusion: Investing in Maintenance

Drip tape maintenance in cotton farms is a continuous process, not a one-time task. By adhering to filtration principles, pressure management, regular acid flushing, and physical care of the tape, you can extend the system's lifespan and prevent water waste. Cotton is a crop sensitive to water stability; therefore, any defect in the drip tape directly affects the yield and quality of the harvested lint.
