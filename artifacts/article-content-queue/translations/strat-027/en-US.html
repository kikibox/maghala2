Optimizing water use in intercropped systems

Drip irrigation can reduce water consumption by up to 40 percent compared to flood irrigation. However, this reduction is only meaningful when the spacing of the drip tapes aligns with the cropping pattern. For crops such as okra, which have high sensitivity to water stress, understanding okra yield per hectare and its standard water requirement is key to increasing profitability. Under these conditions, drip tapes with emitter spacings of 20 to 40 centimeters generally provide better efficiency.

Appropriate selection based on crop type

Every crop has unique sensitivity to uniform water distribution. For example, in lettuce and bean crops, subsurface drip irrigation can prevent water evaporation and weed growth. If you plan to use standard drip tapes, understanding the technical differences between 3 mm and 16 mm pipes is vital. Practical and technical tips for successful implementation

Technical knowledge alone is not enough; correct on-farm execution is what makes the difference. Below, we share key tips that optimize many years of practical experience.

  • Initial Flushing: Before connecting the drip tape to the manifold, always flush the network at 3 bar pressure to remove impurities that were incidentally formed during the manufacturing process. This prevents initial clogging.
  • Installation Direction: Always align the direction of the dripper openings (in the case of one-way tapes) with the prevailing wind and the land slope to prevent asymmetric water distribution.
  • Pressure Management: The water pressure at the drip tape outlet must be between 1 and 3 bar. Higher pressure causes water to spray into the air and be wasted, while lower pressure results in unstable water distribution along the tape.
  • Irrigation cycle planning: Instead of daily irrigation, use a high-frequency, low-volume method. This method encourages roots to grow deeper.

Adhering to these points directly impacts the sustainability of your crop in 1405. For soil-moisture-sensitive crops like beans, precise control of these variables is of utmost importance. To accurately calculate water requirements, having information about coffee yield per hectare (as an example of a perennial crop with different water needs) conceptually helps account for the differences in water demand between annual and perennial crops.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is drip tape suitable for all soil types?

Yes, but its optimization depends on soil permeability. In heavy clay soils, drip tape can cause local waterlogging of the root zone. In this case, it is recommended to use wider spacing between emitters or larger emitters. In sandy soils, water infiltration speed is high, requiring more frequent irrigation.

What is the useful life of drip tape?

Under ideal conditions with proper filtration, the useful life of drip tape can range from 2 to 5 years. Main factors reducing its lifespan include UV radiation, irregular pressure, and the precipitation of calcium and metal substances in the water. The use of advanced filtration systems will become a necessity in 1405.

Is drip irrigation recommended for green beans?

Drip irrigation is one of the best methods for growing green beans because precise moisture control reduces fungal diseases, which are caused by high humidity around the follicle. Additionally, the ability to use Fertigation together with water allows for better absorption of nutrients by the small bean roots.

What is the difference between a disc filter and a mesh filter?

Disc filters are suitable for surface water containing coarse particles and are easier to clean. Mesh filters are for well water and finer filtration (100 to 120 microns), which is essential for tip lines with small orifices. Combining these two types of filters in one system is a common practice in 1405.

Is tip line suitable for orchard crops such as pomegranate?

Tip lines are primarily designed for vegetables/row crops. For trees with highly branched and deep root systems, standard tip lines usually do not have sufficient capacity to cover the canopy radius. In this case, using linear emitters or permanent drip lines (Drip Line) with a diameter of 16 or 20 millimeters is more appropriate. However, combining tip lines with permanent emitters (Hybrid System) in the budding sections of trees is considered an emerging approach in 1405.
