Introduction: The Importance of Accuracy in Calculating Cotton Seed Input

Cotton, as a strategic crop in Iran's agricultural economy, requires precise resource management, including water and seed input. Cotton seed rate per hectare is not fixed and depends directly on the seed type, whether it is cut-seed (stem-based) or whole-seed, and the farming system employed. Over-seeding leads to overcrowding, reducing seed size and fiber quality, while under-seeding results in reduced production capacity and economic loss due to gaps in the planting line.

In recent years, the shift to drip and tape irrigation systems has altered planting parameters. Stability of moisture during root establishment and the reduction of fungal diseases associated with sprinkler irrigation allow for denser planting patterns with higher quality. Below, we examine the technical details of calculation and operational considerations.

Impact of seed type on determining the rate per hectare

The most important variable in the calculation Cotton seed rate per hectareThis refers to the seed type. In Iran, two types of seed are generally used: stem seed (treated with growth stimulants and fungicides) and kernel seed (clean and pure).

  • Stem seed: Due to the presence of pith inside the stem, its weight and volume are lower. Typically, between 3 to 5 kilograms of stem seed is recommended per hectare. This amount varies slightly depending on the cotton variety (pure line or hybrid).
  • Kernel seed: This type of seed is denser and generally requires a higher amount. For precise calculation, the standard formula can be used. Knowing the thousand-grain weight (usually 30 to 40 grams for common varieties) will yield a more accurate calculation. On average, 6 to 8 kilograms Seed quantity per hectare required.

Note that the seed must have a purity and germination rate of over 95% to prevent input waste. This is similar to managing the per-acre seed quantity for corn, where seed quality also plays a key role in determining the planting rate.

Impact of drip and tape irrigation systems on seed rate

Choosing the Patrick tape line for cotton planting delivers moisture directly to the root zone. This allows for an increase in the density of live stands without concerns about soil disturbance and compounded moisture stress.

  1. Moisture stability during the germination phase: Tape and drip irrigation create more uniform moisture compared to conventional irrigation. This allows seeds planted at closer intervals to germinate simultaneously, preventing improper competition for light.
  2. Reduction of seed-borne diseases: Cotton is susceptible to soil-borne diseases such as Pythium. Using drip irrigation reduces the continuous moisture at the soil surface, preventing fungal growth in the seed furrow. This requires increasing fungicide applications and reducing the rate of weak seed.

In practice, many farmers when using drip irrigation realize that by adjusting the spacing between plants (for example, from 40 to 45 centimeters) and using high-quality stalk seed, they can reduce the seed rate toward the 3.5 kilograms per hectare range without reducing plant density. This optimization reduces input consumption and facilitates weed management in drip irrigation lines.

Precise calculation formula and practical planting tips

For precise calculation Cotton seed amount per hectare without considering occasional factors, use the following formula:

Seed quantity per hectare = (1,000,000 × germination percentage) / (thousand seed weight × plant density per hectare)

  • Plant density per hectare: The product of row spacing (usually 80 cm) and in-row plant spacing (40 to 50 cm). For example, if row spacing is 80 cm and plant spacing is 50 cm, the plant density per hectare is 25,000 plants.
  • Germination percentage: For row-seeded crops, it is usually around 85 to 90 percent.

Practical notes for sowing:

  1. Using high-quality seeds and pre-treated seeds is the most economical option for row sowing.
  2. Cover the seeds with soft, stone-free soil to prevent stem breakage during emergence.
  3. Precisely coordinate planting time with soil temperature; temperatures below 12°C increase the risk of breakage.

If your farm is not equipped with a drip tape system, consider that the long-term costs of installing and maintaining drip irrigation systems reduce seed usage and other chemical input costs. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does the use of drip tape have a negative effect on cotton seed quality?

No; on the contrary, drip tape prevents seed decay in the soil due to moisture control. The only important point is not to plow or damage the drip tape mesh during planting. This is similar to caring for drip tape in other sensitive crops.

What is the best season for cotton planting in different regions of Iran?

Depending on the region, from late Esfand to early Farvardin for tropical areas, and until mid-Farvardin for cold regions. The most important criterion is a soil temperature above 12 degrees at a depth of 10 centimeters.

In case of using seed planting, does the seed rate per hectare change?

Yes, seed planting is heavier and denser. While stem planting requires 3 to 5 kilograms, seed planting may consume up to 8 kilograms. Therefore, choosing stem planting is often more economically beneficial.

Can I use my maize planting experience to calculate the cotton planting rate?

No, cotton is a narrow-leaved plant and has fibers. Maize seed planting calculations differ significantly from cotton. It is better to use specialized articles for each crop.

Summary and final recommendations

Determination Cotton seed rate per hectare There is no fixed formula; it is a pulse based on your seed type, variety, and farm infrastructure. To achieve maximum yield, consider the following recommendations:

  • Use high-quality stalk seed to keep the rate in the range of 3 to 5 kg per hectare.
  • Position planting rows directly over the drip line to improve drip irrigation efficiency and weed control.
  • Accurately calculate the number of plants per hectare and avoid over-planting.
