Technical prerequisites for designing a run-off irrigation system

Run-off irrigation for basil involves spraying fine water droplets at controlled speeds to prevent damage to leaves and stems. Designing this system requires high technical precision, detailed as follows.

1. Regulating input pressure and flushing pipes

One of the most critical prerequisites is precise system pressure adjustment. High pressure causes excessive drift and plant damage, while low pressure results in uneven irrigation coverage. Based on Lucass yield per hectare, it must be ensured that uniform yield requires uniform irrigation. Therefore, pressure equalization across all points of the planting area is vital. This requires installing precise manometers at various field locations and using valves for flow regulation.

2. Selecting appropriate nozzles with spray angle

Basil spray nozzles must be designed to disperse droplets as suspended fine particles (micro-spray). The spray angle should be vertical or near-vertical so that water does not strike the plant canopy directly. Selecting nozzles with the appropriate diaphragm is the key to preventing irregular spray patterns.

Soil and water prerequisites for basil

Basil requires well-drained soil with a neutral pH. Before installing the drip irrigation system, soil and drainage water analysis is essential. Due to water evaporation at the surface, drip irrigation can cause soil salinization at the surface in dry regions if preventive measures are not taken.

Drainage preparation and salinity control

When designing drainage channels, the path for excess water discharge must be considered. Sweet basil is sensitive to waterlogging. Additionally, monitoring the pH of irrigation water is critical. For sweet basil, surface irrigation, particularly during the early growth stage, requires frequent, low-volume applications to prevent water stress on the roots without saturating the soil. Practical tips for implementing furrow irrigation of sweet basil

  • Irrigation scheduling based on climate: In hot and dry conditions, increase irrigation frequency and decrease the volume of each application to minimize evaporation.
  • Prevention of fungal diseases: Avoid applying water to the leaves in the evening, as the foliage will not dry by night. The optimal time is early morning.
  • Planting and row arrangement: Align row orientation with the prevailing wind direction to increase air velocity under the basil canopy and reduce humidity.
  • Hydrostatic control: Prevent excessive runoff that causes soil settlement and clogging of drainage channels.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is drip irrigation better than runoff irrigation for basil?

Drip irrigation is the superior option in terms of water use efficiency and reducing fungal diseases (since it keeps water off the leaves). However, runoff irrigation is feasible if adequate ventilation is in place to prevent rapid leaf drying. In humid regions, drip irrigation is preferred.

When should runoff irrigation be used for basil?

During the germination stage, spray irrigation can be beneficial due to low surface moisture requirements. However, at the vegetative growth and flowering stages, it is recommended to use systems with less leaf wetting, such as drip irrigation, to minimize the risk of root and foliar diseases.

What is the optimal planting density for basil under spray irrigation?

High basil planting density results in a dense canopy cover, preventing spray water from penetrating deep into the soil. Therefore, for spray irrigation methods, maintain a moderate planting density to ensure airflow and water reach to the roots.
