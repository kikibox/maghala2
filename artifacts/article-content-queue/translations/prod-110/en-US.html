Why is it important to estimate the cost of 90 mm threaded pipe?

In the design of a modern irrigation system, selecting the correct sizes for main and sub-pipes is one of the most critical engineering decisions. 90 mm (equivalent to 3 inch) threaded pipes are highly popular in orchards, large greenhouses, and vegetable crops due to their optimal balance between initial cost and water transport capacity. However, without accurate estimation, farmers may face issues such as low pressure or energy waste. In this section, we examine the components that make up the cost of 90 mm threaded pipe and provide methods for optimal budget management.

The role of 90 mm pipe in drip and tape systems

90 mm pipes are generally used as lateral lines over long distances or as main lines in medium-sized farms. In drip irrigation, these pipes must facilitate easy connection to filter chambers and valves. Additionally, in tape systems (often referred to as drip tapes), which are very common for row crops like corn or pumpkin, using 90 mm pipe instead of 110 or 160 mm pipes can reduce costs under specific conditions. To better understand the differences in connection methods and pipes, it is helpful to study the guide on differences between poly, woven, and polyket tape as the method of connecting drippers to the pipe directly affects the final cost.

Factors Affecting the Total Landed Cost of Piping on-Farm

The final cost you pay to set up 90 mm lines is not just the price of buying the pipe. It is a combination of various variables that must be factored into your farm budget. Below are the most important of these variables:

Base Price per Meter and Pipe Material

PE (Polyethylene) pipes are available in various wall thicknesses. The pipe wall thickness directly affects its pressure resistance and durability. Thicker pipes cost more but reduce the risk of rupture from sudden pressure spikes or vehicle crossing. When estimating costs, always check the final landed price per meter from multiple reliable sources, as fluctuations in plastic material prices driven by global oil price changes can alter your budget.

Cost of Connections and Fittings

A common budgeting mistake is overlooking connection costs. On a farm with long pipe lines, the number of elbows, tees, and couplings can be significant. Standard fittings for 90 mm pipes must meet appropriate standards to prevent water leaks. Even a minor percentage of leakage over the season can drastically increase irrigation costs.

Budget Modeling: How to Incorporate into Total Farm Costs

For correct financial management, you must the cost of 90 mm threaded pipe be recorded in the farm's "Capital" (CAPEX) section, not as an operating expense. But how do we compare it with other costs?

  • Excavation and soil displacement cost: If the pipes are underground, the cost of excavation machinery must be calculated. For surface lines, this cost is approximately zero, but they require more maintenance.
  • Pumping cost: Choosing a narrower pipe may reduce the pipe cost, but it causes pressure drop, meaning you need a more powerful pump or will get lower efficiency from your existing pump. You must calculate the additional electricity or fuel cost.
  • Useful life: High-quality 90 mm pipes may last 10 to 20 years. Calculate the annual cost by dividing the total cost by the service life to determine the economic advantage compared to sprinkler irrigation.

Impact on final yield

Precision irrigation systems can increase crop yield by reducing plant root stress. If you are using 90 mm pipes for vegetable irrigation, consulting yield guides can help you calculate profitability. Practical tips for cost reduction and purchase optimization

By following a few practical tips, you can reduce the final cost of your irrigation project without affecting system quality.

  1. Sloped and level design: Establish proper slope from the well or water source to the end of the line. If 90 mm pipes are installed unevenly in the field, air accumulates and causes "air locks." Resolving this issue requires installing additional vent valves, which is costly.
  2. Use of appropriate filtration: 90 mm pipes are usually paired with sensitive drip emitters. Poor filtration causes clogging of emitters, forcing you to replace pipes or incur repair costs. Initial investment in filtration reduces long-term expenses. For more information on filtration selection, refer to the guide on Methods for selecting appropriate drip irrigation tapes .
  3. Bulk purchase and volume discounts: If your farm is large, buying 90 mm pipes in bulk rolls or pieces reduces the cost per meter. Also, compare prices for air bubbles in packaging.
  4. Protection from direct sunlight: If your pipes are above ground, UV (ultraviolet radiation) is the main enemy of polyethylene. Include the cost of UV protection or partial burial in your annual budget. Otherwise, the pipe's service life will drop from 10 years to 2–3 years, which is not cost-effective.

Pressure Drop Calculation

Before finalizing your purchase, have an irrigation engineer calculate the Head Loss (HFL) for the 90 mm pipe. If the pressure drop exceeds 10–15% of the design pressure, you must upgrade the pipe size to 110 or 160 mm. A small sizing error can drastically increase pump maintenance costs.

Frequently Asked Questions About 90 mm Pipes

Is a 90 mm pipe suitable for pumpkin cultivation?
Yes, a 90 mm pipe is suitable for main and sub-lines in pumpkin cultivation, especially if pumpkin trays (planting forms) are irrigated via drip emitters on tape lines. For exact requirement calculations, you can refer to the article on Pumpkin Seed Quantity per Hectare + Factors Affecting Density Read more. Because of its large size, cucurbita requires more water, and using lines of appropriate capacity such as 90mm pipes will ensure water reaches the end of the line.

Do the prices of irrigation pipes fluctuate in the market?
Yes, the price of polyethylene (PE) pipes is directly related to the prices of petrochemical raw materials and the exchange rate. We recommend purchasing 90mm pipes for the next season when prices are low and storing them in a dry location.

What is the cost difference between 90mm and 110mm pipes?
110mm pipes are more expensive than 90mm, but they have less pressure drop in long lines. If your farm is small and your pump provides adequate pressure, 90mm pipes will be more economical and efficient. On farms with a high slope, the cost of the additional electricity required to compensate for pressure drop on 90mm lines may be more cost-effective than purchasing 110mm pipes.

Does the spacing of the drippers affect the cost of 90mm pipes?
No, dripper spacing does not directly affect the price of 90mm main pipes, but it does affect the overall flow rate of the system. If the spacing is too close, the overall flow rate will increase and 90mm pipes may be insufficient, forcing a switch to larger, more expensive pipes.

Summary: Is investing in 90 mm pipes logical?

The cost of a 90 mm threaded pipe is not merely a fixed figure on a purchase invoice; it is a dynamic parameter linked to proper design, crop type, and your farm's conditions. For specific vegetable crops like zucchini or bulk crops like chickpeas, choosing the correct pipe size can create a narrow boundary between over-specification and under-specification.

The final recommendation is to model your budget based on different scenarios (purchasing 90 mm versus 110 mm pipes, surface versus subsurface installation) before buying. Also, use irrigation consultants to calculate pressure drop.
