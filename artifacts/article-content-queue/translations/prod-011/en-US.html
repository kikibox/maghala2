Introduction to the Role of 75mm Pipe in Farm Economics

One of the primary challenges for producers in modern agriculture is managing infrastructure costs. In drip and tape irrigation systems, a main line with a diameter of 75 mm is typically used in small to medium-sized farms, or as a sub-main line in larger farms. A precise understanding of the cost of 75mm threaded pipe and its associated components can make a significant difference in final profitability. Many farmers only consider the price per meter of the pipe, ignoring secondary costs such as fittings, hardware, and installation labor. This article aims to provide a practical guide by explaining all cost factors to help with a realistic estimate. Initial costs may seem negligible, but over a five-to-ten-year period, selecting the right pipe material and fittings can reduce operational and maintenance costs by up to 30%. Therefore, a superficial look at retail price tags without considering amortization (functionality) costs does not reflect the true cost.

The choice of pipe type (threaded or welded) and manufacturer brand directly impacts system durability and, ultimately, annual maintenance costs. Higher-quality pipes offer greater resistance to UV exposure and temperature fluctuations. Therefore, technical and economic details will be discussed in the following sections. To better understand the relationship between pipe type and efficiency, you can read the article on how to extend the useful life of tape lines ; a healthy main line ensures the proper function of sub-lines and pressure stability throughout the season.

Factors affecting the final price of 75mm threaded pipe

Material type and raw material quality

Polyvinyl chloride (PVC) pipes are available in various A and K grades. Pipes meeting higher standards cost more but offer greater resistance to pressure and environmental corrosive factors. In sandy farms or areas with acidic soil, selecting pipes with enhanced protection can reduce early replacement costs. Pipe wall thickness (SN) is also a critical parameter affecting the base cost. Choosing a pipe with a higher SN rating means a higher initial cost but a longer service life.

Pricing based on meter and carton

Manufacturers typically quote prices per carton (e.g., 4-meter or 6-meter lengths). When estimating costs, the cutting waste factor must also be considered. Normally, there is 5 to 10 percent waste associated with cutting and joints. Therefore, when budgeting, always add 10 percent to the calculated length for purchase. Additionally, transportation costs from the central warehouse to the farm can constitute a significant portion of the total purchase cost and should not be overlooked.

Practical tips for reducing threaded pipe costs

  1. Optimal network design: Using a simple tree pattern instead of a complex grid network in dry-climate farms can reduce pipe length. Each meter less of pipe means lower costs.
  2. Reduction of cutting losses: Using moving cut fibers and smart pipe storage reduces waste. Standard pre-cuts of 20 or 40 meters are better than short cuts.
  3. Proper use of connectors: Crane connectors (couplets) are cheaper than Number connectors, but for high durability at high-pressure points, buy Number connectors. Carefully combining the two optimizes cost.
  4. Seasonal maintenance: Cleaning filters and checking valves at the end of the season prevents pipe failure in the following year due to sudden pressure spikes. This directly prevents pipe replacement costs.

In addition to the above points, note the compatibility of pipes with drip tapes. If using specific tapes, ensure the operating pressure of control valves is compatible with 75mm pipes. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • Does the price of 75 mm threaded pipe include fittings?
    No, pipe prices are typically listed per meter excluding fittings. Fittings must be calculated separately based on the design layout.
  • What are the best brands for 75 mm pipes?
    Select reputable Iranian brands that meet ISO standards. Low-cost brands with thin wall thickness lead to higher long-term costs.
  • What is the service life of a 75 mm pipe?
    Under standard conditions, the service life is 10 to 20 years. Improper use of water pressure reduces this lifespan.
  • How do we calculate the line pressure?
    Calculate based on the field elevation and the length of the main line. For sloped farms, you need a pressure regulator.
