Identifying Common Types of Drip Tape Failures

Before any repair action, the specific type of drip tape failure must be accurately diagnosed. Drip tape failures generally fall into two main categories: mechanical and hydraulic. Mechanical failures include tearing, breakage, or damage caused by rodents and agricultural tools. Hydraulic failures are primarily related to emitter clogging, chemical sedimentation, or root growth inside the pipe. In ryegrass cultivation, where roots grow near the soil surface, the risk of emitter clogging by sediment particles is very high. Key signs of clogging include insufficient water flow from the lower emitters compared to the upper ones, or localized wetting of the soil under the tape. Additionally, water pressure fluctuations at the tape outlets may indicate partial blockages in the main line of the tape. For better diagnosis, the use of test screens and visual inspection of emitters with a magnifying glass is recommended. Sometimes, drip tape may appear intact but have low water flow; this condition is often due to biofilm formation or calcium deposits on the internal walls of the tape, which requires chemical flushing. Familiarity with these signs is the first step in maintaining the health of the irrigation system. There is also another category of failures involving damage from solar radiation and extreme temperature changes, which causes the PVC of the drip tape to become stiff and brittle.

Key Points for Military and Chemical Flushing of Drip Tape

The most critical aspect of drip tape maintenance is periodic flushing to prevent emitter clogging. Flushing is performed in two ways: physical (flush water) and chemical (acid or base). For physical flushing, increase the system pressure to the maximum allowable limit and keep the drip tape above the soil surface for several minutes to flush out suspended particles. This should be done immediately after each irrigation cycle, particularly before starting the next one, to prevent sediment from adhering to the inside walls of the tape. Chemical flushing is essential for removing mineral deposits (calcium, magnesium) or biological buildup (biofilm). The annual preventive use of citric acid or phosphoric acid at appropriate concentrations is recommended. Under specific conditions, the use of Virkon or biological disinfectants is necessary to eliminate microbial sludge. It is important to note that during chemical flushing, drip tape must never be exposed to direct sunlight, as the combination of acid and UV radiation can damage the tape's polymer. Additionally, ensure that chemical agents do not interact with the alfalfa inoculant and do not negatively affect soil stability; therefore, chemical flushing should be scheduled with sufficient time spacing from alfalfa fertilization and irrigation. Working Pressure Management and Safety Pressures

Incorrect pressure is the primary enemy of drip tape longevity. Drip tapes are typically designed for a working pressure of 2 to 3 bar, although some models can withstand up to 4 bar. Excessive pressure causes stretching, emitter bursting, and potential line rupture at weak points. Conversely, pressure below the required level can lead to uneven irrigation distribution and reduced efficiency. In alfalfa cultivation, which generally involves high seed density and requires continuous plant cover, maintaining uniform pressure throughout the line is critical. To manage pressure, the use of a pressure regulator at the system inlet and pressure gauges at various points along the line is recommended. When there is a significant field slope, centrifugal effects and elevation changes must be accounted for in local pressure calculations. Additionally, installing a screen at the tape inlet to prevent the entry of suspended particles larger than 20 microns is a crucial step in reducing failures. Be aware that in warm seasons, water vapor and thermal pressure can also affect internal line pressure; therefore, delay irrigation to the cooler hours of the day or adjust the pressure. A proper safety system also manages risks associated with fluctuations in the main water supply network. The use of smart solenoid valves can be very helpful in this regard, as the system can automatically stop the irrigation cycle if abnormal pressure is detected.

Leveraging drip irrigation systems to increase alfalfa yield

One main reason for the high maintenance costs of drip tape is its direct impact on clover yield. Drip irrigation allows precise control of root zone moisture. Clover roots are deeper than those of typical forage grasses, so gradual, low-volume water supply promotes deeper root growth and greater plant resistance to drought. Using drip tape instead of wide tape ensures water is injected only into the clover planting row, preventing waste in the surrounding space. This feature is particularly important in sandy soils with high permeability. In such conditions, irrigation frequency should be increased while the volume per irrigation is reduced. The irrigation schedule must be adjusted based on soil water potential data and daily plant water requirements. By following proper maintenance principles, a drip tape system can remain usable for up to 3 years or more without replacement, whereas degraded systems increase irrigation costs and pose a risk of failure in clover crops. Practical Tips for Maintaining Drip Tape Health During the Growing Season

  • Always lay drip tape at the correct pressure, ensuring it does not stretch excessively in stored sections, to prevent kinks or folds.
  • Avoid pulling or forcefully moving the tape during clover planting; lateral pressure can cause minor leaks.
  • At the end of each season, wash the drip tape, dry it, and store it away from sunlight in a cool environment.
  • Clean filters regularly and replace them immediately if clogging or tearing is observed.
  • Avoid exposing the drip tape to high temperatures from direct sunlight when the system is not in operation.
  • To prevent biofilm growth, use a moderate acidic solution in the final irrigation of the season.
  • Annually, inspect a sample of the drip tape emitters to ensure the internal condition of the pipe is healthy.

Frequently Asked Questions About Drip Tape Maintenance

  1. How long does drip tape last?
    With proper maintenance, high-quality drip tapes are usable for 3 to 5 years. Factors such as sun exposure, operating pressure, and soil type affect their lifespan.
  2. Does chemical flushing have a negative effect on soil quality and clover roots?
    No, if chemical flushing is performed at the appropriate concentration and sufficiently far from planting time, it has no negative effects. In fact, proper flushing reduces water stress and maintains plant health.
  3. When should drip tape be replaced?
    Drip tape should be replaced if it is broken, if its emitters are blocked and cannot be cleared by chemical and physical flushing, or if the pipe has become stiff and brittle.
  4. Is drip tape use recommended in heavy soils?
    It is recommended, but the drip rate should be lowered. In heavy soils, permeability is lower, and drip tape can concentrate moisture and encourage deeper root growth in clover.
