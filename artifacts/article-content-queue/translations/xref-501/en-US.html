Introduction: The Importance of Selecting an Appropriate Irrigation Method for Cotton

Cotton is one of the most significant industrial and food crops in semi-arid and hot regions, exhibiting high sensitivity to water deficits and improper moisture management. While many farmers are inclined toward modern systems such as drip irrigation, Surface irrigation still has extensive applications due to its low initial cost and compatibility with many climatic and regional soil conditions. This method, in which water flows uniformly over the soil surface, may appear simple but requires technical precision in land grading, accurate water volume calculation, and irrigation scheduling to achieve optimal yields. This article focuses on the practical and operational aspects of this method for cotton cultivation.

Making the correct choice between traditional and modern methods, such as furrow irrigation which is a subset of surface irrigation, can impact the final cost and the quality of cotton boll opening.

Technical Principles of Surface Irrigation in Cotton Farms

Surface irrigation includes channel, border strip, and furrow methods. For cotton, the two main methods—channel (border) and furrow irrigation—are more common. The spacing of cotton rows is typically between 70 and 90 cm, and this spacing determines the geometry of the channels or furrows.

Grading of longitudinal and lateral land slopes

The most important condition for successful surface irrigation is the uniformity of land slope. The appropriate longitudinal slope for soils ranging from clayey to sandy is typically between 0.1 and 0.5 percent. If the slope is too steep, water flow becomes rapid, causing soil erosion and reduced vertical infiltration at the beginning of the row. If the slope is too shallow, water velocity decreases, leading to water logging and reduced efficiency. The use of laser land leveling or even deep plowing and grading for leveling the land surface is a critical first step.

Calculating irrigation depth and duration

Cotton has varying water requirements at different growth stages. Maximum water demand occurs during the boll development and fiber elongation periods. The calculation of required water is based on the crop coefficient (Kc) and reference evapotranspiration (Eto) of the region. Deeper irrigation with longer intervals, especially in heavy soils, helps cotton roots grow deeper.

Applications and operational benefits in cotton cultivation

One of the main applications of surface irrigation is the ability to perform it simultaneously with other agricultural operations, such as inter-row tillage in certain furrow systems. This method does not require complex plastic or metal pipe infrastructure. For farmers with limited development budgets, this is a competitive advantage. Additionally, in areas where the water source is supplied by a river or main canal, direct transfer of water to field furrows without the need for high-pressure pumps reduces energy costs.

  • Low investment cost: Compared to drip irrigation systems In Erbil or other regions, surface irrigation does not require expensive metal infrastructure.
  • Compatibility with cotton seeding rate per hectare: Seed and plant density align with the canal spacing. To learn about planting density standards, you can refer to the Recommended seed rate per hectare guide, where similar principles also apply to cotton.
  • Fertilizer dissolution management: In fertigation, soluble fertilizers (such as ammonium nitrate) can be injected at the beginning of the water flow to enhance root uptake. However, care must be taken regarding concentration and potential damage to young plants.

Key points and common implementation challenges

The main difficulty in surface irrigation for cotton is distributing water uniformly along the furrow. In sandy soils, infiltration rates are high, and water may percolate deep into the soil before reaching the end of the furrow. To compensate, one can use the Short and Frequent Irrigations (Setback Method); this involves applying water sequentially at different points along the furrow (by closing the furrow head or moving the check structure) to allow water more time to infiltrate at the upper end of the furrow.

Inflow management

  1. The inflow rate of water into the furrow must be calculated based on furrow length and soil infiltration.
  2. Use adjustable gates to precisely control water flow to each furrow.
  3. Irrigation Timing: Irrigating during low-light hours (early morning or night) is recommended to reduce surface evaporation.

If you use metal or plastic hoses in the main lines of your farm's water delivery system, please consult the guide for 2-inch cotton irrigation hose which can help you select the appropriate size and pressure. Although the hose itself is not used directly for surface irrigation, it serves to transfer water from the main canal to the furrows.

Comparison with Modern Methods and Optimal Selection

Is surface irrigation always the best option? The answer depends on economic conditions, soil type, and climate. In regions with easy access to mobile water sources, micro-sprinkler or drip systems offer higher efficiency and promote more uniform vegetative growth. However, for large farms with suitable slopes and relatively heavy soils, surface irrigation remains cost-effective.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) on Cotton Irrigation

What should the spacing between furrows for cotton be?

Furrow spacing depends on planting density. If the row spacing is 75 centimeters, furrows are generally dug 30 to 50 centimeters from one side of the rows to allow water to distribute to both sides. The furrow width should be 15 to 20 centimeters.

Does surface irrigation promote weed growth?

Yes, increased surface moisture can promote weed germination. To prevent this, it is recommended to use herbicides or practice pre-emergent weeding before cotton planting and manage irrigation timing precisely.

How is water managed when cotton is grown alongside other crops?

If you use combined methods, coordinating water resources is essential. For example, if planted simultaneously with legumes such as beans, which have lower water requirements, you can avoid a shared furrow system and implement field zoning. Summary and final recommendations

Surface irrigation in cotton cultivation is a method that can lead to high efficiency when site preparation and flow rate calculation are performed with precision. The key to success is the coordination between land slope, soil infiltration, and timing. Farmers should prevent waterlogging and water stress by continuously monitoring soil moisture status (using simple methods like manual soil checks or low-cost devices). Combining traditional knowledge with modern irrigation principles, such as Fossil standard for closing crop fields or even smart monitoring, ensures the future sustainability of the farm. By selecting a method aligned with available resources, costs can be managed while improving cotton yield quality.
