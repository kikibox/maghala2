Introduction: Why Drip Irrigation in Millet Cultivation?

Millet is one of the widely used and important plants in the food and agriculture industries, and due to its relative tolerance to various climatic conditions, it is cultivated in different regions. However, proper water management in millet cultivation remains a major challenge for many farmers. In this context, drip irrigation as one of the innovative and precise irrigation methods, plays an effective role in optimizing water usage and increasing crop yield. This method, especially in arid and semi-arid regions, can make a significant difference in the sustainability and continuity of production. In the following, we will examine the key and practical aspects of drip irrigation in millet cultivation.

Definition and Principles of Drip Irrigation

Node Irrigation is an irrigation technology that uses small nodes or junctions in irrigation lines to release fine water droplets at specific intervals. Compared to conventional methods such as furrow or standard drip irrigation, this system provides more precise distribution and prevents water waste by reducing evaporation and runoff. In millet cultivation, where water requirements vary across different growth stages, this system enables smarter water resource management. By creating controlled moisture zones, it promotes better root development at appropriate depths and reduces surface weeds. Additionally, reduced surface moisture helps maintain optimal soil temperature and prevents the growth of surface fungi. These features allow farmers to produce higher-quality crops while reducing energy and labor costs. In Iran's climate, which faces water scarcity, using Node Irrigation is not only an economical option but an ecological necessity to protect groundwater resources. This system is also compatible with smart systems and agricultural automation, enabling real-time monitoring of soil and plant status.

Advantages of Node Irrigation in Millet Cultivation

  • Increased water use efficiency by 30 to 40 percent compared to surface irrigation
  • Reduced energy consumption for pumping due to lower operating pressure
  • Improved soil conditioning around millet roots through consistent moisture supply
  • Reduced risk of fungal infections caused by excessive surface soil moisture
  • Capability for Variable Rate Irrigation (VRI) based on row-specific needs
  • Improved and more uniform plant emergence throughout the entire crop row
  • Reducing the need for deep plowing and preserving soil structure

Key points in system design and implementation

Selecting suitable grids for barley

One of the most important factors in the success of grid irrigation is selecting the appropriate grid type with suitable outlet diameters. For barley, whose roots are located at a depth of 30 to 50 centimeters, grids with drip outlets spaced 10 to 15 centimeters from the soil surface are recommended. Additionally, the resistance of grids to sedimentation and clogging, particularly in water sources containing sand, is a vital parameter. The use of pressure-compensating grids (variable pressure) is recommended to maintain uniform flow rate along the pipe and prevent pressure differences between the start and end of the line. In areas with hard water, the use of disc and disk filters with appropriate mesh is essential to prevent the blockage of grid outlet holes. Regular maintenance and periodic flushing of lines are also crucial actions for maintaining the long-term efficiency of the system.

Determining the spacing of node pipes

The pipe spacing must align with the flax row spacing. In conventional flax cultivation, row spacing is typically 40 to 50 centimeters. Node pipes should be installed directly beneath each row or at a distance of 15 to 20 centimeters from them to ensure complete moisture coverage. Using drip tape alongside the node system can optimize this coverage. If ridge planting is used, the pipes can be placed on the crop surfaces to maintain access for spraying or harvesting. This layout flexibility is a key advantage of modern irrigation systems over conventional methods.

Pressure and flow rate management

Low-pressure delivery with controlled flow is key to optimal emitter performance. Excessive pressure causes droplet ejection and pollution, while low pressure reduces flow rate and creates imbalance between emitters. A pressure regulator valve (PRV) at the start of main lines is essential. Additionally, using variable-speed pumps can help maintain constant pressure in the network. Calculating flow based on area and crop water requirements must be done accurately to prevent drought or waterlogging. Regular monitoring of pressure at various points in the field and recording the data helps identify early signs of pressure drop issues.

Practical Tips for Farmers

Soil Preparation Before Planting

The soil bed must be fully leveled and free of large rock masses. Rock masses can divert water flow and reduce coverage. Where possible, using laser leveling systems is recommended to ensure drip lines remain at the same elevation along the route. Adequate soil drainage is one of the most important pre-installation factors, as temporary soil waterlogging causes unbalanced pressure on emitters. Deep plowing and surface application of organic fertilizer can improve water absorption and almond root growth. Additionally, planting seeds at the proper depth and ensuring firm soil contact around the seed are critical to prevent rapid root drying during the initial weeks after planting.

Irrigation Scheduling

Barnyard grass is sensitive to moisture during critical stages, particularly the booting stage (15 to 20 days after sowing) and grain formation. The drip irrigation schedule must be adjusted according to these sensitivities. Using soil moisture sensors for on-demand irrigation creates the most optimal condition. During hot seasons, irrigation intervals should be shortened, and the flow rate of each irrigation event should be adjusted in proportion to evaporation and plant transpiration rates. Using rainfall monitoring maps and weather forecasts helps farmers avoid excess irrigation on rainy days. This combined approach (based on need and timing) reduces costs and increases efficiency.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  1. Is nodular irrigation also recommended for drought-resistant barley?
    Yes, even in drought conditions, this system can meet the plant's critical needs by reducing flow rate and increasing irrigation intervals.
  2. What flow rate range is ideal for barley nodes?
    Typically between 1 to 3 liters per hour, depending on penetration depth and soil texture.
  3. What should be the depth of the nodes from the soil surface?
    Approximately 5 to 10 centimeters below the surface to prevent evaporation and accommodate barley root depth.
  4. Does this system require filtration?
    Yes, 120-mesh disc filters are essential.
  5. What is the main difference between node and drip irrigation?
    Node irrigation releases fine droplets in a narrow, controlled strip, whereas drip irrigation delivers water to a concentrated point. Node irrigation provides more uniform coverage along the line.
