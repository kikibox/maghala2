Introduction

Cultivating barley, a key economic and oilseed crop in Iran, requires sufficient water and nutrients. A primary challenge for farmers is managing water resources effectively under harsh climatic conditions and drought. Under these conditions, Irrigation of fruit orchards in barley cultivation can serve as a smart strategy that, while producing barley, creates suitable space for developing economic orchards. This integrated approach increases efficiency and reduces absolute dependence on conventional water sources. It also allows farmers to utilize the open space between orchard trees to exploit both deep and shallow root zones simultaneously. This synergy not only minimizes evaporation and runoff losses but also ensures farmer income stability by providing two main products: fruit and oilseeds.

Benefits of Combining Fruit Orchards and Barley

Integrating different crops in a single cropping system can offer numerous benefits for farmers and the environment:

  • Increased land efficiency: Utilizing different soil depths for tree roots and barley bushes allows for maximum soil capacity usage. Barley roots are primarily active in the upper soil horizons, whereas trees draw nutrients from greater depths.
  • Economic diversity: Entry into both fruit and oilseed markets reduces seasonal risks and price fluctuations. If the price of barley drops, fruit remains the main source of income, and vice versa.
  • Soil moisture conservation: The natural shade created by the trees reduces direct solar evaporation from the soil surface and preserves existing moisture.
  • Product quality improvement: Proper growth management and reduced barley plant density in the presence of trees contribute proportionally to improved final grain and oil quality. Additionally, the resulting biodiversity limits plant pests.

Drip and strip irrigation techniques

Drip irrigation is the ideal option for mixed orchard and cotton cultivation due to low water consumption and uniform water distribution in the root zone. In cotton farming, typically fixed-flow drip emitters are used to deliver water and nutrients directly to the roots of cotton and trees. This system allows the cotton planting bed to remain moist without causing waterlogging. It also enables Fertigation, which is critical for the rapid growth of cotton.

Strip Irrigation is also a suitable method for row crops such as cotton. In this method, water enters the soil as narrow strips along the crop rows. This method minimizes evaporation losses compared to sprinkler irrigation and is a good option for soils with relatively high infiltration rates.

  • Drip system: Suitable for water-scarce conditions, sandy soils, and intercropping with trees. It offers high precision in water distribution.
  • Tape Drip: Suitable for clay soils with medium permeability and semi-arid climates. Installation costs are generally lower than those of point drip systems.

Practical Implementation Notes

For Successful Implementation Irrigation of Fruit Orchards in Millet CultivationIt is essential to observe the following principles:

  1. Determine appropriate row spacing: The spacing between walnut rows should be at least 60 to 70 centimeters to allow for the correct installation of the drip system. For orchard planting, the distance between trees is adjusted from 3 to 5 meters depending on the variety selected. This spacing allows small machinery to move between the walnut rows.
  2. Calculate emitter flow rates: The flow rate of drip emitters must be commensurate with the water absorption capacity of the walnut trees. The use of 4 or 8 liters per hour emitters is usually the optimal choice for walnuts. It is important to ensure that the water flow rate for tree roots is not identical to that for the undergrowth or intercropped plants.
  3. Two-stage planting (Poddrodayu): Walnut trees are usually planted in two stages. The first stage is in early spring and the second stage is in mid-spring. At the time of the second-stage planting, the path of the drip pipes must be protected to prevent them from deteriorating.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is buckwheat compatible with all orchard tree varieties?
No, the best compatibility is with semi-shade-tolerant crops. Walnut, almond, and other hard-kernel trees are the best options for intercropping with barley. In hot climates, olive and mango varieties can also serve as suitable alternatives. Excessive shading of barley causes weakness.

What are the characteristics of suitable groundwater for barley cultivation?
Barley is sensitive to water with high settling potential. If the water pH is above 7 and TDS is above 1000, a flush system must be installed at the ends of drip lines to prevent emitter clogging. Water quality is critical for the operational lifespan of the irrigation system.

Does the presence of an orchard increase barley pests?
In the early years, yes, because the orchard environment creates greater insect diversity. However, in the long term, biodiversity prevents the spread of any single specific pest. The use of pheromone traps is recommended in intercropped orchards.

When is the best time to plant barley alongside an orchard?
Early spring (Esfand and Farvardin) is the optimal time for barley to complete its initial growth from the orchard's shade before the peak summer heat.
