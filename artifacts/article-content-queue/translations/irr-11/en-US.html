Introduction: Why Greenhouse Irrigation Must Be Smart

Greenhouse cultivation, due to controlled environmental conditions, requires precise management of water resources. Greenhouse irrigation is no longer a simple process, but a precise engineering discipline that directly affects quality, root health, and ultimately yield. Unlike open-field crops, any irregularity in water distribution in greenhouses can lead to localized dryness, physiological stress, or root diseases. This section of the guide examines how selecting the appropriate system can reduce operational costs and maximize efficiency.

The two main systems used in many greenhouse crops are drip and tape systems. While drip is suitable for trees and perennial covers with wider spacing, tape is a better option for annual crops with row planting (such as vegetables, cucurbits, and ornamental flowers). Understanding these fundamental distinctions is the first step in designing a sustainable and efficient system.

Technical Differences Between Tape and Drip in the Greenhouse Environment

The choice between tape and drip systems depends directly on the type of crop. Drip tape (Drip Tape) is usually thinner (2 to 3 mm) with a shorter useful life (one growing season). This characteristic makes its initial cost lower, making it ideal for frequent annual crops. In contrast, round and tube drip systems are thicker (4 to 8 mm) and have a multi-year lifespan, chosen for berry and grape trees and crops that require system longevity.

One key point in greenhouse management is pressure definition. Modern drip lines are now produced in double-layer or side-sealed formats, which improve flow stability. To ensure that your selected drip line meets the plant's root zone requirements, reviewing the benefits of side-sealed drip lines can enhance your technical understanding. These lines reduce clogging at the emitters and guarantee more uniform water distribution along the row.

Key points in greenhouse drip line installation

  • Emitter spacing selection: Emitter spacing must be suitable for plant rooting. For tomatoes and peppers, emitters spaced 15 to 20 centimeters apart are generally recommended.
  • Drip line orientation: Always position the drip line so that emitters face the main root zone rather than the stem, to prevent stem wetness and the increased risk of rot.
  • Connections: Use secure connections and standard fittings; leaks at connection points in high-humidity greenhouses can be a major source of fungal growth.

Managing Pressure and Flow: The Key to Uniform Distribution

In greenhouse systems, pressure drop along the main line (Mother Line) is a common challenge. If the input pressure to the system is low, the outlets at the end of the line will experience dryness. To solve this problem, pressure-compensated valves are used in distribution lines. These valves create mechanical resistance, keeping the flow constant and independent of the input pressure.

Calculating the total system flow rate is crucial. The total flow rate is equal to the sum of the flow rates of the active outlets on a main line. If the pump flow rate is lower than this value, the system pressure will drop. Practical tips for increasing yield and reducing losses

One common mistake in greenhouse irrigationis the use of outdated or unsuitable systems that result in water waste and nutrient loss. Modern systems with 'precision irrigation' capabilities provide real-time moisture control.

  1. Benefiting from fertigation: Direct all fertilizers with the water flow to the root zone. This practice saves on fertilizer costs and improves nutrient uptake.
  2. Soil moisture monitoring: Use appropriate filters (filter size 80 or 120) to prevent outlet clogging. Clogged outlets are the main enemy of drip tape.
  3. Relative humidity control: In summer, a decrease in greenhouse relative humidity can cause evaporative stress. Increasing irrigation frequency and reducing the volume per irrigation event is an effective solution.

Initial system installation costs should be evaluated against crop yield increases and loss reductions. Although the price of drip tape per hectare varies depending on quality and brand, investing in a high-quality system guarantees a return on investment within one to two growing seasons. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. Is drip tape reusable?
Standard drip tape is designed for one growing season. Use in a second or third season leads to reduced flow rates and clogging due to wall thickening and degradation. For permanent crops, use permanent drip lines.

2. How to prevent clogging of drip line outlets?
Using water filtration systems, appropriate filters, and regular flushing of reservoirs is essential. Additionally, fertilizer must be injected from a separate reservoir at a point where the water flow is rapid to prevent sedimentation.

3. Is drip irrigation suitable for growing forage corn in a greenhouse?
Forage corn is typically grown in open fields; however, if used in a controlled environment, the spacing between outlets must align with the planting distance.
