Cost Structure of Rain Drip Irrigation

The costs of this system are divided into two categories: fixed (capital) and variable (operational). The initial investment includes purchasing high-pressure pumps, tanks, and installing light trellises. However, the major cost is the energy consumption (electricity and fuel) for each irrigation cycle, which is very high in this system.

Energy and Equipment Costs

  • High-pressure pump: High pump wear due to excessive pressure during the season.
  • Fuel or electricity: Caloric energy consumption to lift water to the farm's absolute elevation.
  • Repairs: Rapid metal corrosion due to exposure to open air and impact with dust.

The important point is that while pressurized systems such as Pressurized irrigation have end-of-life repair costs, sprinkler irrigation involves frequent and expensive repairs that directly impact the budget.

Hidden cost of soil erosion

One of the largest overlooked costs in sprinkler irrigation of dill is soil erosion. Artificial rain generated by sprinklers causes soil crusting and creates ruts in the field. This erosion necessitates annual expenses for soil remediation and drainage. According to statistics, fields using sprinkler irrigation spend 15 to 25 percent more annually on soil remediation than drip-irrigated fields.

To reduce this cost, it is necessary to use Methods for determining drip zones In specific locations, either modify the system or convert the deepest points to drip irrigation so that water reaches the roots directly and soil leaching costs are eliminated.

Depreciation of sprinkler irrigation equipment

Sprinkler irrigation equipment, including sprinklers and nozzles, is exposed to mechanical and environmental stresses. Repair costs for these components must be included in your budget. In the initial years, repair costs are low, but over time, replacing pressurized pipes and worn-out nozzles becomes a continuous and significant expense. In contrast, subsurface systems have much lower depreciation.

Ultimately, the true cost of irrigation should be calculated by dividing total expenses by the crop weight at the end of the season (per hectare). Practical tips for reducing costs on the farm

If you are required to use sprinkler irrigation, these practical tips can minimize your costs:

  1. Uniform distribution: Ensure sprinklers are evenly distributed across the planted field so that no area remains dry, which would require extra water for re-sprinkling.
  2. Fertilizer management: Apply fertilizer only through the drip system, or if using sprinklers, inject fertilizer before irrigation starts to reduce chemical losses. To determine the exact fertilizer amount, refer to specialized guides such as Yield of cress It can serve as a model for calculating yields in other mixed cropping systems.
  3. Reducing energy consumption: Do not irrigate during peak electricity hours to minimize your metering costs.
