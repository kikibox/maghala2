Introduction to the Economics of Drip Irrigation in Green Onion

Selecting the appropriate irrigation system is one of the most critical economic decisions for farmers growing short-cycle vegetables such as green onion. While surface irrigation remains common, hot and dry regions in Iran, facing water scarcity, are shifting toward low-consumption systems like drip and micro-drip irrigation. Here, micro-drip irrigation (often recognized as pressurized drip lines or micro-drip lines) has gained attention due to its manageable initial costs and high efficiency. This article aims to provide a clear, data-driven analysis based on field observations of the cost of drip irrigation for green onion on the farm, enabling farmers to develop a more precise budget before planting.

Due to its high yield and short growth cycle, green onion requires precise management of root zone and nutrition. Using drip lines ensures water is delivered directly to the root zone, which not only reduces waste but also enables fertigation. However, the key question is whether the initial investment for purchasing and installing these systems is justifiable, given the fluctuating market prices?

Structure of Fixed and Variable Costs in Drip Irrigation

For a comprehensive understanding of the cost of drip irrigation for green onionCosts must be divided into fixed and variable categories. Fixed costs include the purchase of main equipment, installation of main lines, and filtration. For tubing systems, the primary cost is associated with the plastic tubing featuring shaped orifices, which have lower durability than black drip emitters. The average useful life of these tubes in single-season crops is approximately one season, unless special maintenance is performed. Conversely, variable costs include pump energy consumption, water fees, labor for installation and removal, and filter cleaning costs.

  • Tubing system cost: The price of tubing is usually calculated per hectare based on the emitter spacing (e.g., 20, 25, or 30 cm) and discharge rate. Selecting the appropriate spacing requires assessing planting density; in this regard, studying the seed amount of onion sets per hectare can help determine the final layout.
  • Filtration cost: Installation of disc and sand filters is mandatory to prevent clogging of the small orifices. This section accounts for approximately 10 to 15 percent of the total system cost.
  • Energy cost: Due to the lower operating pressure required for tubing orifices compared to standard drip emitters, high-pressure pump consumption is reduced, leading to lower electricity costs in the long term.

Cost per hectare analysis and return on investment

For precise calculation of chive drip irrigation cost, the following assumptions are considered: Chive row spacing is typically between 20 and 35 cm. In traditional methods, flood irrigation is performed, which has very high water losses. However, with drip tape, water consumption per hectare over the entire growth period (approximately 60 to 90 days) is reduced. This reduction in consumption directly lowers water and pumping costs. Economically, although the purchase price of drip tape is relatively low, the labor cost for replacing it each season is a significant economic variable. To reduce operational costs, some farmers use the drip tape installation method which is primarily used for vegetables with movable drip tape, but correct installation techniques to minimize plastic damage are applicable to all systems.

Experience shows that chive yield with drip irrigation can increase by 10 to 20 percent compared to surface irrigation due to uniform root development. This yield increase alone can offset the irrigation system costs in the first year. Additionally, reduced spraying costs due to adequate water availability at the root zone also lower chemical expenses.

Comparison with similar systems

Emphasis on system sustainability requires attention to the dynamic and non-rotational characteristics of the agricultural strip . While knot-tie tubing is cheaper, it has a shorter useful life. In contrast, black drip tubing is more durable but has a higher initial purchase cost. The choice between the two depends on the farmer’s short-term or long-term policy. If your farm is small and you plan to rebuild the system annually, knot-tie is the better option. However, for large and permanent farms, investing in more durable systems is more logical. Practical tips for reducing operational costs

In addition to purchase costs, daily operational costs account for a large portion of knot-tie irrigation costs for green onions . Adhering to the following points can significantly reduce these costs:

  1. Input pressure control: Drip emitters are highly sensitive to high pressure. Using a pressure regulator to maintain constant pressure (usually 1 to 2 bar) prevents emitter burst and reduces losses. Irregular pressure causes water to spray at some points while flow remains uneven at others.
  2. Main line pipeline improvement: Using polyethylene pipes with proper pressure rating and no excessive bends reduces periodic maintenance costs. Network design must be based on the water delivery method and the operation of each step to prevent pressure drop at the end of the lines.
  3. Regular filter cleaning: Filter clogging is the primary cause of drip system failure. The time and labor cost for washing is negligible compared to the cost of crop loss.
  4. Selection of the appropriate tape type: Tapes with larger nozzle diameters (e.g., 4 mm) are slightly more expensive but offer better resistance to clogging. An informed selection based on local water quality helps manage overall costs.

Additionally, using two-sided planting systems at short spacing for onion sets can improve the utilization of field space. In this planning, attention to the principles of standard row spacing for sugar beet can serve as a general reference for optimizing various densities in root vegetables, providing ideas for adjusting onion set layout. Although sugar beet is a different crop, the root space logic is similar.

Answers to Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is knotted tape less expensive than portable drip tapes?

Yes, the cost of knotted tape per hectare is approximately 30 to 40 percent lower than black drip tape with the same flow rate. This shorter service life must be considered in economic break-even calculations.

Is drip irrigation suitable for Kashke and Rasht green onions?

Yes, both green onion varieties require uniform root zone moisture. The drip system ensures uniform growth of seedlings by distributing water evenly along the row, which provides a competitive advantage for sale in fresh markets.

What is the best time to purchase equipment to reduce costs?

Generally, component prices are slightly lower during off-seasons due to decreased demand. Bulk purchasing of drip lines during these periods can reduce the cost per meter.

Are specific settings required for drip lines in green onion cultivation?

The emitter spacing on the drip line should match the planting distance. If green onions are planted at high density, an emitter spacing of 20 cm is more suitable to prevent relative dryness between two irrigation points.
