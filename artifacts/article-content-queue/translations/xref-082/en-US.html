Rosemary, a high-value medicinal and aromatic herb globally, requires specialized care in soil moisture management. Because rosemary roots are relatively shallow and the plant is highly sensitive to both waterlogging and drought, selecting an appropriate irrigation method is a major challenge for growers. While drip and microspray irrigation are popular and high-efficiency methods, surface irrigation still has specific applications under certain soil and climatic conditions that should not be overlooked. In this article, we provide a technical review of the use of surface irrigation in rosemary cultivation and explain key points for its correct implementation.

Understanding surface irrigation and its potential in rosemary cultivation

Surface irrigation is a method where water flows freely over the soil surface and moves downward through infiltration to the root zone. Although this method has lower efficiency and typically higher water consumption compared to pressurized systems like microspray, it offers certain advantages for plants like rosemary in specific contexts. Rosemary is a drought-tolerant plant, but it requires adequate moisture during its vegetative growth period. Surface irrigation in rosemary fields is generally recommended when the soil infrastructure allows for the correct construction of irrigation channels, or when growers seek to reduce the initial setup costs associated with drip systems. However, precise water management in this method is critical, as prolonged waterlogging of rosemary roots can lead to root rot and a reduction in product quality. Practical applications of surface irrigation in rosemary fields

Using surface irrigation for rosemary without a precise plan can lead to water waste and reduced product quality. Therefore, the effective application of this method must be based on sound irrigation principles. One key application is during the early growth stage, where young plants require even water distribution. Additionally, in areas with low soil slope and suitable beds, precise furrow formation can be used to guide water flow, ensuring that rosemary roots are uniformly exposed to moisture. It is important that irrigation timing prevents water from lingering on the soil surface and instead promotes deep infiltration. Alongside these points, a comparison with water-saving methods is necessary; for example, gaining a comprehensive understanding of drip tapes can broaden farmers' perspectives on the benefits of root-level water delivery versus surface distribution.

Specific benefits and limitations for rosemary

  • Reduced initial costs: Compared to drip systems, it requires less complex piping and expensive filters.
  • Improved soil aeration: Surface water flow can aid soil aeration if properly managed, provided that waterlogging is avoided.
  • Risk of nutrient leaching: One of the major drawbacks of surface irrigation is the leaching of nutrients from the soil surface, which is particularly hazardous for rosemary, a crop sensitive to soil fertility.

Practical tips for successful surface irrigation of rosemary

To achieve optimal results from surface irrigation in rosemary cultivation, adhering to the following execution points is mandatory. First, the field surface must be perfectly leveled, as any improper slope causes uneven water distribution, leading to water stress in some plants and waterlogging in others. Second, the distance between planting rows should be adjusted so that water penetrates the soil rapidly rather than lingering on the surface. Third, the irrigation schedule must be based on air temperature and evaporation rates; irrigation in the early morning or at night is generally recommended to minimize evaporation losses. Fourth, using slow-release fertilizers or adding organic matter to the soil can increase water holding capacity and offset the negative effects of leaching. Finally, continuous monitoring of the water level in the furrows is essential. Farmers seeking to optimize water resources can explore alternative methods, such as sub-surface irrigation, to achieve better farm management combinations.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is surface irrigation suitable for all growth stages of rosemary?

No, surface irrigation is generally suitable for early growth stages when the plant has a shallow root system. However, as the plant grows and the root zone expands, pressurized systems such as drip or laterals provide higher efficiency and prevent water loss.

How can I prevent fertilizer leaching in surface irrigation?

The best solution is to use slow-release fertilizers, incorporate fertilizers into the soil at the root depth, and reduce the water volume per application while increasing the number of smaller applications. Additionally, maintaining crop cover between rows can prevent soil surface leaching.

Does surface irrigation lead to weed infestation in rosemary fields?

Yes, this is one of the main challenges. Continuous surface moisture leads to rapid growth of weeds, which creates competition for water and nutrient uptake. Use of coltzar (cultivation harrow) or adaman (hand hoe) is recommended for weed control, or consider plastic mulching.

Conclusion and Final Recommendation

Surface irrigation in rosemary cultivation is a traditional method that can be practical under specific conditions when precise management principles are observed. However, given that rosemary is a crop sensitive to water quality and moisture levels, and the final goal is to produce high-quality medicinal dried herbs, it is recommended that farmers continually strive to upgrade their irrigation systems towards water-saving methods such as drip or band-tip irrigation. This approach not only reduces long-term irrigation costs but also supports the sustainability of regional water resources. By being aware of the limitations of surface irrigation and combining it with other management techniques, it is possible to achieve products of higher quality and better yield.
