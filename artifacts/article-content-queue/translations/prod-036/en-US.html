Introduction: The Importance of Selecting Appropriate Piping in Modern Irrigation

In modern irrigation systems such as Types of drip laterals and their functionsthe role of transmission pipes as the main arteries of the system is vital. Many farmers and agricultural engineers face the challenge of selecting piping that is technically reliable and economically optimal. Among the options, 90 mm (or 3/4 inch) High-Density Polyethylene (PEHD) coiled pipes have attracted special attention due to their widespread application in main and sub-main lines. Owing to their high flexibility and ease of distribution across various farms, these pipes have become the first choice for many developers of drip and drip tape irrigation systems. The correct selection of these components not only determines the initial project costs but also directly impacts long-term maintenance costs, energy consumption, and crop yield. Therefore, a precise and expert analysis of the technical characteristics of these pipes, and their comparison with alternative brands, appears essential.

Technical Analysis of the Characteristics of 90 mm Coiled Piping

The 90 mm pipe in irrigation systems is typically used for sub-mains or farm main lines in drip systems operating at relatively low to medium pressures. Below is a structural comparison of this pipe with standard brands and more affordable brands, each possessing specific technical characteristics:

  • Resin Material: Reputable brands use PE80 or PE100 resin with a controlled melt flow rate (MFR). These resins have a regular molecular structure that allows the pipe to retain flexibility at lower temperatures. In contrast, some small brands may use recycled or inferior resin, which reduces resistance to internal pressure and leads to premature pipe failure. Using low-quality raw materials also compromises water chemical safety and can cause sedimentation inside the pipe over time.
  • Wall thickness and SDR: The standard dimension ratio (SDR) directly affects working pressure. A 90 mm drip pipe with an appropriate SDR must withstand pressure fluctuations during pumping without permanent deformation. Some brands may reduce wall thickness to the minimum permitted limit to lower costs, causing pressure instability at the lower end of the farm. This pressure drop can lead to uneven dripping at the end-of-line emitters, altering soil moisture penetration depth and causing uneven crop growth.
  • Ultraviolet (UV) resistance: Many white or black drip pipes contain a UV masterbatch. In low-quality brands, this additive is either missing or used in incorrect proportions, causing the pipe to become brittle after sun exposure. The photodegradation process under sunlight causes fine surface cracking, which eventually leads to complete rupture. Impact of pipe selection on tape and drip line performance

Incorrect selection of the main pipe directly affects the health of drippers and the benefits of twin-tape irrigation. A 90 mm pipe, due to its suitable laminar flow characteristics, promotes uniform flow in lateral lines if manufactured by a high-quality brand. This uniformity is critical for maximizing twin-tape performance, as even pressure distribution along the entire line guarantees uniform moisture coverage under the drippers. When inlet pressure to the drippers fluctuates, the efficiency of different field zones will not be synchronized, resulting in some areas suffering from water deficit and others from waterlogging, which leads to both water waste and reduced crop quality.

During comparison, it should be noted that spiral-wound pipes, due to their helical structure, exhibit different mechanical behavior than flat or rolled pipes when being distributed in the field. Top-tier brands maintain their spiral form at ambient temperature and easily straighten out, whereas low-quality brands may become irregularly shaped after sun exposure, making installation difficult and time-consuming. Additionally, the bending quality of standard spiral pipes must be such that no internal pressure is exerted on the walls during storage to prevent deformation.

Practical points in installation and field comparison

  1. Cold flexibility test: Before purchasing, ask the seller to bend a section of the pipe at low temperatures. High-quality PE100 pipes do not break even in cold conditions and have good shape recovery. Low-quality brands are more brittle in winter temperatures and develop micro-cracks from minor impact, which become pressure leakage points in the long term.
  2. Color uniformity check: The presence of opacity or white streaks in black pipe indicates the addition of waste material. Standard 90mm spiral-wound pipe should have a uniform, deep black color (due to carbon addition). This color uniformity indicates even distribution of UV additives.
  3. Operational working pressure: Compare the labeled operating pressure (such as 6 or 10 bar) with the pump's actual requirement. Unlicensed brands sometimes print a higher operating pressure than permitted to mislead buyers. It is best to always request the resin material Certificate of Analysis (CoA) from the seller.
  4. Total cost: Although low-tier brand pipes are cheaper, the high failure rate in hot seasons increases replacement costs and crop loss. This has been field studies of drip irrigation observed many times. In addition to the cost of replacing rotted pipes, labor costs for system reconstruction and irrigation suspension during peak plant demand are hidden disadvantages of this incorrect choice.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is the 90 mm threaded pipe interchangeable for all brands?

No, the internal dimensions of couplings differ slightly among different brands. It is better to purchase the entire system from a single brand and manufacturer to prevent leaks at connection points. If you must combine different brands, always use compatible (universal) fittings and wrap sufficient PTFE tape tightly.

What is the difference in energy consumption between a 90 mm and a 110 mm pipe?

Smaller-diameter pipes have higher friction loss and consume more pump energy. However, in drip irrigation systems with low flow rates, a 90 mm pipe is often the most economical option, unless the mainline length is very long. In that case, increasing the diameter to 110 mm can reduce pump electricity costs and extend pump life.

Does the presence of a coiled structure have a negative impact on pipe lifespan?

No. If the resin is of good quality, the coiled structure reduces stress on the pipe, which actually increases its resistance to abrasion during replacement in later years. This feature also allows the pipe to return to its coiled shape after each use, making it easier to store and package.

How can you determine the quality of the resin by appearance?

High-quality pipes have a clear or semi-clear, uniform surface and do not emit a strong plastic or unpleasant chemical odor. Low-quality brands often have a bad smell, which indicates the use of recycled raw materials. Additionally, the pipe wall thickness should be consistent along the entire length, with no noticeable fluctuations.
