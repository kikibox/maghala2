Introduction: Why is Calculating ET0 Important for Farmers?

Optimal irrigation cannot be achieved without a proper understanding of plant physiology and climatic conditions. In drip and micro-sprinkler irrigation systems, accuracy in calculating water requirements directly impacts production costs, soil health, and final yield. The key parameter in this process is the reference evapotranspiration (ET0) value. ET0 indicates how much water a reference plant surface loses through evaporation and transpiration. To Calculate Water Requirements Based on ET0, this reference value must be multiplied by the crop coefficient (Kc) of the specific crop to obtain the crop evapotranspiration (ETc). This method eliminates guesswork and transforms irrigation into a precise science. Correctly understanding this process is the key to preventing root drought or over-irrigation, both of which lead to reduced economic efficiency of the farm. Basic Concepts: ET0, Kc, and ETc

For a deeper understanding, the three main concepts must be examined individually. First, ET0, which is a daily variable value measured in millimeters. Second, Kc, known as the crop coefficient, which indicates the difference in water requirements between the crop and the reference surface. Third, ETc, which is the product of these two values. Always remember the simple relationship below: ETc = ET0 × Kc. The plant coefficient (Kc) varies depending on the plant type, growth stage, and environmental conditions. For example, Kc is low during the seedling stage and peaks during the fruiting stage. Practical tips for optimization using ET0

  • Weekly ET0 monitoring: Use data from the local weather station to update ET0 values. These values change significantly with the seasons.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is ET0 constant or does it change?

ET0 is highly variable and changes based on season, climate, temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, and daily solar radiation. In midsummer, ET0 can be twice as high as in winter. These fluctuations directly affect your daily irrigation requirements and need precise adjustment.

How do I find my plant's Kc?

Use FAO reference tables or local irrigation guides. These coefficients vary by growth stage (seedling, vegetative, flowering, fruiting). If local data are unavailable, you can use global averages for similar crops and then calibrate by monitoring plant growth.

Do I need the full ET0 in a strip irrigation system?

No, usually you only replace a portion of ETc (for example, 70 to 80 percent) because part of the soil surface remains dry. Accurate calculation based on ET0 allows you to manage this reduction factor and prevent over-irrigation.
