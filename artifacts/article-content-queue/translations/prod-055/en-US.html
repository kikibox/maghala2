The importance of selecting the correct pipe in irrigation systems

In local irrigation systems, particularly drip-line maintenance and cost, the role of main and sub-pipes in distributing uniform pressure and flow is critical. Pipes with a 110 millimeter diameter are an ideal option for main lines in medium-to-large farms due to their higher water transmission capacity and reduced pressure drop. A correct selection at this stage directly affects pressure stability at drip emitters and drip-line consistency.

Basic technical specifications of 110 millimeter threaded pipes

Material and polymer type

Threaded pipes are generally manufactured from two main materials: Polyethylene (PE) in various grades such as PE50 or PE80, and sometimes PVC. For drip irrigation, PE pipes are preferred due to their high flexibility against temperature changes and impact resistance. PE50 pipes have a higher working pressure and are more suitable for main lines connected to high-pressure sources, while PE80 is ideal for sub-lines and medium-to-low pressures. In addition to material, resistance to chemicals present in the soil also plays a role in material selection.

Wall thickness and SDR ratio

One of the most important technical parameters is the SDR (Standard Diameter Ratio). This ratio represents the relationship between nominal diameter and wall thickness. The lower the SDR, the thicker the wall and the higher the pressure it can withstand. For 110 millimeter pipes, the selection between SDR 21 and 40 is determined based on input pressure and line length. Using a pipe with an inappropriate SDR can lead to leaks at joints or sudden pipe failure. Precise calculation of this ratio is essential for each farm condition.

Connection type and pipe welding

"Threaded" pipes essentially mean having a standard thread for connecting fittings. In 110mm pipes, the threads typically consist of 2 to 10 or 2 to 14 threads per inch. Choosing a pipe with ISO 7-1 standard or Iran's National Standard thread ensures that joints are sealed with no leakage and at optimal pressure. It is crucial to ensure that the fitting material is compatible with the pipe material; for example, brass fittings are suitable for metal pipes, while polymer fittings are suited for PE pipes.

Impact of 110mm pipes on drip and tape line performance

Pipe size selection must align with the total farm flow rate and the number of drippers. If the main pipe (e.g., 110mm) is significantly larger than required, pressure loss will be minimal but costs will be higher. If it is selected too small, severe pressure loss can result in insufficient water reaching the end of the line and crop dryness in that area. Precision at this stage is critical to ensure uniform irrigation across the entire farm area.

  • Uniform distribution: 110mm pipes are ideal for main lines up to 300-400 meters long with moderate flow rates. For longer distances, precise pressure loss calculations using the Darcy-Weisbach formula are necessary.
  • Reduced sedimentation: Adequate flow in PE pipes prevents the accumulation of suspended particles and sediments at the pipe bottom, directly increasing the lifespan of drippers.

Furthermore, for farms utilizing various types of drip lines, maintaining precise pressure at 110-to-75 or 75-to-40 mm fittings is critical to preserve the rated operating pressure of the emitters.

Practical Guide to Installation and Maintenance of 110 Threaded Pipe

  1. Correct Cutting: PE pipe must always be cut with a toothed saw or a precision blade to ensure a clean cut without burrs. Slanted cuts cause leaks in the coupling and reduce service life.
  2. Lubricating the Coupling: Applying silicone grease or PE-specific lubricant before closing the fitting prevents internal surface abrasion and extends pipe lifespan.
  3. Pressure Testing: After installation, the pipe must be held at a pressure 10-15 degrees higher than the nominal working pressure for at least 2 hours to identify any potential leaks.
  4. UV Protection: Black pipes are UV-resistant, but if white or light-colored pipes are used, they must not be exposed to direct sunlight during the day and should be coiled on the ground to prevent cracking.

Matching the pipe to crop and economic fields

The selection of a 110 mm pipe should align with your cropping plan. Divide this flow rate by the length of the line to determine the flow rate per meter of pipe. If the flow rate per meter of pipe exceeds the appropriate limit, there is a high risk of pressure loss and a need for a larger size. Conversely, if the flow rate is very low, a 110 mm pipe with a higher SDR (thinner wall) may be more suitable to reduce costs.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about 110 mm pipes

Is 110 mm pipe suitable for laterals?

110 mm pipes are typically used for main lines. For laterals, sizes of 50 to 75 mm are more appropriate. Using 110 mm for laterals results in higher costs and more space usage in the farm, which is not economically justified unless under specific high-flow conditions.

What is the useful life of 110 mm PE pipes?

With good quality and resistance to nominal working pressure, a useful life of 50 years or more is stated. However, factors such as UV, soil chemicals, and uneven pressure reduce this lifespan. Proper maintenance is the key to preserving this long-term service life.

What is the difference between 110 mm pipe and 110 mm SDR pipe?

SDR is the pressure rating specification of the pipe. A 110 mm pipe with SDR 21 withstands higher pressure and has a thicker wall. A 110 mm pipe with SDR 40 has a thinner wall and lower pressure rating. Selection is based on the pump inlet pressure and field elevation. In sloped fields, hydrostatic pressures must be considered in SDR calculations.

Conclusion

The 110 mm threaded pipe is the main column of many local irrigation systems. Its selection, based on material, SDR, and connection type, directly affects pressure stability, dripper performance, and the economic efficiency of your farm. Do not neglect accurate pressure drop calculations, and always use standard fittings and petroleum grease. For smaller farms, a 75 mm size may be sufficient, but for larger farms and high flow rates, a 110 mm pipe with the appropriate SDR is a smart choice. By adhering to installation and maintenance principles, this investment will serve for decades.
