The Importance of Selecting the Correct Pipe in Irrigation Systems

In local irrigation systems, particularly drip line maintenance and cost, the role of main and sub-mains in ensuring uniform pressure and flow distribution is critical. 110 mm diameter pipes are an ideal option for main lines in medium to large farms due to their high water transmission capacity and reduced pressure drop. The correct selection at this stage directly impacts pressure stability at drip emitters and drip line stability.

Basic Technical Specifications of 110 mm Threaded Pipe

Material and Polymer Type

Threaded pipes are generally manufactured from two main materials: Polyethylene (PE) in various grades such as PE50 or PE80, and occasionally PVC. For drip irrigation, PE pipes are preferred due to their high flexibility against temperature changes and resistance to impact. PE50 pipes have a higher working pressure and are more suitable for main lines connected to high-pressure sources, while PE80 is ideal for sub-lines and medium to low pressures. In addition to material, resistance to chemicals present in the soil also plays a role in material selection.

Wall Thickness and SDR Ratio

One of the most important technical parameters is the SDR (Standard Diameter Ratio) coefficient. This ratio represents the relationship between nominal diameter and wall thickness. The lower the SDR, the thicker the wall and the higher the allowable pressure. For 110 mm pipes, the selection between SDR 21 and 40 is determined based on input pressure and line length. Using a pipe with an inappropriate SDR can lead to leakage at joints or sudden pipe failure. Accurate calculation of this coefficient is essential for each farm condition.

Connection Type and Pipe Flare

Sewn pipes are essentially characterized by having standard threads for fitting connections. For 110mm pipes, threads typically include 2 to 10 or 2 to 14 TPI. Selecting pipes with standard ISO 7-1 threads or the Iranian National Standard ensures that joints close without leakage and at optimal pressure. Care must be taken to ensure compatibility between fitting and pipe materials; for example, brass fittings are suitable for metal pipes, while polymer fittings are appropriate for PE pipes.

Impact of 110mm Pipes on Drip and Tape System Performance

Pipe diameter selection must align with the total field flow rate and the number of drippers. If the main line (e.g., 110mm) is significantly larger than required, pressure loss will be negligible but the cost will be higher. If it is too small, severe pressure loss will occur, preventing adequate water delivery to the end of the line and causing crop dryness in that area. Precision at this stage is critical to ensure uniform irrigation across the entire field.

  • Uniform distribution: 110mm pipes are excellent for main lines up to 300-400 meters with moderate flow rates. For longer distances, accurate pressure loss calculations using the Darcy-Weisbach formula are required.
  • Reduced sedimentation: Adequate flow in PE pipes prevents the accumulation of suspended particles and sediment at the pipe bottom, directly extending the lifespan of drippers.

Also, for farms using various drip line types and their respective performance, precision in the outlet pressure of 110-to-75 or 75-to-40 fittings is critical to maintain the nominal working pressure of the drippers.

Practical guide for installation and maintenance of 110 threaded pipe

  1. Correct cutting: PE pipe must always be cut with a toothed saw or a precision cutting blade, ensuring a clean cut without extra rings. Slanted cuts cause leaks in the socket and reduce service life.
  2. Lubricating the socket: Using silicone grease or a special PE grease before tightening the fitting prevents internal surface wear and extends the pipe's lifespan.
  3. Testing: After installation, the pipe must be held under a pressure 10-15 degrees above the nominal working pressure for at least 2 hours to identify any potential leaks.
  4. UV protection: Black pipes are UV-resistant. However, if white or light-colored pipes are used, they must be protected from direct sunlight during the day and coiled on the ground to prevent cracking.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about 110 mm pipe

Is 110 mm pipe suitable for lateral lines?

Typically, 110 mm pipe is used for mainlines. For laterals, sizes ranging from 50 to 75 mm are more appropriate. Using 110 mm for laterals leads to higher costs and requires more space in the field, making it economically unjustifiable except in cases of very high flow rates.

What is the service life of PE 110 mm pipe?

With good quality and against nominal working pressure, a service life of 50 years or more is specified. However, factors such as UV radiation, soil chemicals, and pressure surges reduce this lifespan. Proper maintenance is the key to preserving this long-term service life.

What is the difference between 110 mm pipe and 110 SDR pipe?

SDR indicates the pipe pressure rating. A 110 mm pipe with SDR 21 withstands higher pressure and has a thicker wall. A 110 mm pipe with SDR 40 has a thinner wall and lower pressure capacity. Selection is based on pump input pressure and farm elevation. On sloped farms, hydrostatic pressures must be included in SDR calculations.

Summary

The 110 mm threaded pipe is the main trunk for many local irrigation systems. Selection based on material, SDR, and connection type directly affects pressure stability, drip emitter performance, and farm economic efficiency. Do not neglect precise pressure drop calculations, and always use standard fittings and PTFE grease. For smaller farms, size 75 may be sufficient, but for larger farms and high flow rates, a 110 mm pipe with appropriate SDR is a smart choice. By adhering to installation and maintenance principles, this investment will serve for decades.
