The importance of using smart irrigation in kale cultivation

Kale cultivation is one of the most nutrient-rich and sensitive leafy green products in modern agriculture. Due to its high requirement for consistent moisture and warm soil, it urgently requires precise water resource management. The use of traditional concentrated irrigation methods not only causes significant water waste but also leads to the growth of weeds and an increase in root fungal diseases. Smart irrigation for kale Leveraging advanced technologies such as timers, soil moisture sensors, and drip hose systems enables more precise control of the microclimate conditions in the planting bed. This approach helps farmers achieve the highest product quality at the optimal harvest time with minimal water and energy consumption. Below, we examine the benefits of this system and practical strategies for its implementation.

Defining smart irrigation systems and drip tape

Smart irrigation systems combine drip or drip tape hardware with control software that analyzes meteorological and soil data to automatically adjust irrigation dosage and timing. In kale cultivation, which is typically done in containers or raised beds, the use of drip tape appears to be the best option. These tapes, often produced in plastic with a 20-millimeter diameter, release uniform water droplets directly into the plant's root zone. This process prevents the soil surface from being exposed to direct sunlight, thereby minimizing evaporation. Key Benefits of Smart Irrigation in Celery Farms

Implementing smart irrigation systems in celery cultivation offers numerous economic, hygienic, and environmental benefits. Below are the most significant ones:

  • Water Consumption Reduction by 30 to 50 Percent: With precise droplet delivery, all water used is delivered directly to the root zone.
  • Prevention of Fungal Diseases: Keeping leaves and the growing medium dry significantly reduces Mildew and Botrytis.
  • Reduction in labor costs: Automating the irrigation process eliminates the need for daily operational labor.
  • Increased stability of temperature and substrate humidity: For fast-growing plants such as Kale, environmental stability directly affects fruit quality.

Impact on yield and crop quality

Research shows that Kale plants exhibit faster growth when their roots are not under water stress. Continuous drip irrigation at low doses allows plants to absorb nutrients uniformly. To benefit from the aforementioned advantages, installation steps must be performed with precision. The following is an operational checklist prepared for farmers:

  1. Hose network design: Hoses with a diameter of 25 or 20 millimeters are typically used for main lines, and thinner hoses for supplying the tips. A comprehensive guide on selecting 2-inch and 1/2-inch hoses Available options can help calculate pressure loss along the lines.
  2. Secure Filtration Installation: Sand or cartridge filters must be selected to match the water source. For sensitive systems, do not forget to include a disc filter.
  3. Connecting the Timer to the Power Source: Choosing the right timer varies depending on the number of zones and the required timing precision. Simple programmable timers are the best option for getting started.

Key Maintenance and System Flushing Tips

Drip irrigation and tubing systems will suffer from sediment buildup and emitter clogging if not flushed. An acidic or chlorinated system flush is recommended every two to three months. Additionally, at the end of the growing season, ensure the tubing is removed from the pipes and stored in a dry, cool place.
