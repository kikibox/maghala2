Practical and detailed training on the installation steps for 20 cm drip tape

Step 1: Ground Surface Preparation and Planting Spacing Determination

Before laying the tape, the ground must be carefully plowed, ridged, and leveled. Precise land leveling is essential to prevent water accumulation in low spots and ensure uniform flow. The drip tape must not be placed on rocks, weeds, or large particles, as these factors can cause irregular holes in the tape body and water leaks. For 20 cm drip tape, the planting spacing generally varies between 50 and 100 cm depending on the crop type and planting system. If your planting spacing is 80 cm, place the tape precisely at a depth of 15 to 20 cm alongside the crop row. This depth ensures that water reaches the active root zone directly.

Step 2: Connecting Ends, Fittings, and Laterals

A 1000 m roll typically requires connecting several sections to cover the length of the main line or lateral. Using high-quality polyethylene couplers and fittings is highly recommended over traditional threaded joints, as they offer greater resistance to pressure and corrosion. Connection points must be fully parallel to the tape axis to ensure uniform water flow without energy loss. Always use sturdy plastic or a flush filter to prevent sediment from entering the 20 cm tape holes. Proper filtration is of double importance, especially with well or qanat water sources that may contain suspended particles. Any clogging can result in ineffectiveness at the end of the tape.

Step 3: Water Pressure Adjustment and Land Slope Management

Modern drip tape lines are often equipped with Pressure Compensating systems. During installation, ensure that the ground slope does not exceed 5%; otherwise, this may negatively affect irrigation accuracy at the ends of 1,000-meter rolls, causing uneven water distribution. For steeper slopes, use pressure-regulating valves or divide the tape into shorter, horizontal segments. Monitoring input and output pressure before and after the mainline is the key to ensuring uniform water discharge. Sudden pressure changes can cause weak connections to burst.

Specialized tips for improving system performance and sustainability

  • Preventing UV degradation: Plastic drip tape exposed to direct sunlight becomes damaged and brittle. Never leave it uncovered on the soil surface; after planting, bury it under the soil or use protective covers.
  • Minimum dimensional pressure: System input pressure must be between 0.5 and 1 bar. If the pressure is lower, the emitters at the end may remain closed; if it is higher, the mainline will be damaged. To properly learn about precise pressure regulation, refer to the Drip Tape Water Pressure Regulation Guide Refer to the guidelines to ensure the accuracy of your pressure gauge equipment.
  • Number of planting holes: If you are growing sprouting plants (such as seed vegetables), position the drip tape 20 centimeters below the soil planting level to prevent direct evaporation from the soil around the holes. This distance is optimal for reducing unproductive water consumption and increasing irrigation efficiency.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) regarding 20-centimeter drip tape installation

  1. Can the 1,000-meter roll be used on slopes with a 10% grade?
    No; for slopes exceeding 5%, it is recommended to use constant-pressure drip tapes or divide the system into two sections. Otherwise, excess water delivery at the lower end can cause root flushing and fungal diseases.
  2. What is the optimal installation depth for 20 cm wide drip tape for tomato crops?
    The ideal depth is 20 to 25 cm, allowing deep tomato roots to access water without forcing the plant to rely heavily on the soil surface. This depth also helps develop a stronger root network.
  3. How can we prevent emitter clogging after several months of use?
    Always use an appropriate filter (120 to 200 mesh) and chlorinate the water before it enters the drip tape. Periodic flushing of the mainlines also prevents sediment accumulation.
