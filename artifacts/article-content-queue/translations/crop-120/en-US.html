Why does cotton require drip irrigation?

Cotton cultivation, especially in arid and semi-arid regions, faces significant challenges in water resource management. Traditional surface irrigation methods, such as flood irrigation, have consistently been associated with issues like high evaporation, weed growth, and fungal diseases caused by wet foliage. Drip irrigation systems, and specifically the use of Drip Tape, have revolutionized cotton farming. By delivering water directly to the Root Zone, this system maintains optimal moisture levels and minimizes plant water stress. In addition to water conservation, this method enables direct plant nutrition through Fertigation, resulting in more uniform growth and reduced fruit drop. Prerequisites for establishing Drip Tape for cotton cultivation

Before planting cotton seeds, proper preparation of the irrigation network is critical. Drip tape is a suitable tool because it has lower installation costs than fixed drip lines and offers a single-use lifespan (typically 1 to 3 years). One of the most important considerations is selecting the appropriate emitter spacing. For cotton, an emitter spacing of 30 to 50 cm on the drip line is generally ideal, as it ensures uniform water distribution around the plant. Additionally, you must consider pre-purchase guidelines for irrigation drip tape to prevent emitter clogging and ensure adequate pressure. Proper installation of the tapes should coincide with seed planting so that roots are in contact with moisture-retaining seeds from the start of the process. Main and lateral lines must be configured to deliver uniform pressure throughout the length of the tapes. Irrigation and fertilization schedule for cotton

Cotton irrigation management must be adjusted based on plant growth stages. Cotton requires regular and moderate irrigation, and severe drought causes flower and fruit drop. In the initial vegetative stage, short and frequent irrigations are more beneficial. As the plant reaches flowering and square initiation, water demand peaks. Combining irrigation with fertilization (Fertigation) via drip tape is a powerful tool. You can use nutrient solutions containing nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. Practical tips on planting and weed management

Cotton planting with the drip-tape method provides an excellent opportunity for weed control, as the shade cast by the drip line limits weed growth. However, management remains necessary. One effective method is the use of plastic mulch or biodegradable covers, though this is more costly. If mulch is not used, surface weeds must be controlled before the planting rows close and the soil is fully covered by cotton leaves. Mechanical methods, such as loosening the soil with appropriate tools or using high-precision pre-planting herbicides, are recommended. Remember that the greater the spacing between cotton planting rows, the more space is created for weed growth; therefore, dense rows with double-sided drip-tape coverage is the optimal state for weed control. Additionally, Ordering of drip-tape rows should be arranged so that the overlap of rows in furrows and sub-lines is minimized to maintain mechanical access.

Answers to Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • Is a tape drip line more suitable for cotton than a flushable drip line?
    For cotton, which is typically a one-year annual crop, tape drip lines are more cost-effective. Flushable drip lines (usually with 16 mm lateral diameters) are generally selected for longer-lived perennial crops such as fruit trees. Cotton requires a lower initial investment, and tape drip lines meet this need. Additionally, flushable drip lines sometimes have smaller orifices that are prone to clogging when water has high sediment content, whereas modern tape drip lines feature robust internal intake filters.
  • How much water can each drip emitter deliver to the cotton?
    The water volume per emitter is adjusted based on the spacing between emitters. Typically, a 40 cm spacing on 16 mm laterals delivers approximately 2 to 3 ml/hour per emitter. To achieve adequate root zone depth, irrigation duration must be adjusted so that the wetted zone depth is approximately 30 to 40 cm.
  • Can tape drip lines be used in consecutive cotton crops?
    Yes, but replacement is usually recommended every 1 to 2 seasons. Cotton roots grow directly along the tape line, and after harvest, the tape is either left in the ground or collected. The service life of standard tape drip lines in soils with high salinity and sediment is less than one season.
