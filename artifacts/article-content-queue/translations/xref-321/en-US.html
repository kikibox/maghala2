Rocket is one of the most popular salad vegetables in Iran, characterized by rapid growth and a short cultivation cycle. However, managing water resources for this crop remains a significant challenge for farmers, particularly in semi-arid regions. The use of drip irrigation with emitters in rocket cultivation is a modern and efficient solution for overcoming water scarcity and improving agricultural efficiency. This article explores the benefits, technical principles, and execution details of this method in greater depth. Due to its thin leaf structure and shallow root system, rocket requires consistent soil moisture; significant fluctuations in humidity can cause fruit splitting and reduce product shelf life. Therefore, selecting the appropriate irrigation method is not only about increasing yield but also a step toward preserving quality and plant health.

Why are drip emitters ideal for rocket?

Rocket is a plant with a shallow root system where roots are closely spaced near the soil surface. This characteristic makes uniform, localized water distribution directly within the root zone critically important. Modern drip emitters with fixed, adjustable flow rates enable precise delivery of water to meet plant needs without causing surface runoff or humidity stress fluctuations. Unlike sprinkler irrigation, which can lead to soil compaction and the spread of fungal diseases due to wet leaves, drip emitters eliminate these problems at the source. Additionally, keeping the soil surface between rows dry significantly reduces weed growth. This not only minimizes the need for herbicides but also maintains a cleaner and more hygienic field environment. Ultimately, precise moisture control promotes uniform growth of stems and leaves, which directly impacts product consistency in the market.

Increasing water use efficiency

one of the most prominent advantagesThe key benefit of this system is a significant reduction in water consumption. In drip irrigation, water is delivered gradually to the root zone at low pressure and in a controlled volume. This minimizes evaporation and deep percolation. Studies have shown that using drip emitters can save up to 40 percent of water compared to conventional methods. This saving not only reduces operational costs but also plays a vital role in the sustainability of regional water resources. By reducing stress on water sources, farmers can allocate their water budget to other crops or more critical growth stages. Additionally, reduced evaporation positively impacts lower pumping and energy costs.

Weed control

Another key benefit is the significant reduction in weed growth between rows. Because water is applied only in the root zone under the plant, other parts of the field remain dry, preventing weeds from germinating and growing. This allows farmers to reduce the use of herbicides in the drying sections and implicitly lowers input costs. Practical tips for installation and operation

To fully benefit from drip irrigation using emitters for mung bean cultivation, it is crucial to adhere to the following:

  • Hose material and diameter: It is recommended to use polyethylene hoses with UV inhibitors for durability against summer ultraviolet radiation.
  • Dripper spacing: Given the mung bean planting distance (typically 30 to 40 cm in-row), dripper spacing must be adjusted based on the selected flow rate to ensure uniform irrigation coverage.
  • Consideration of land slope: To prevent pressure variations in the drippers, leveling devices (pressure-compensating fittings) must be installed throughout the field.
  • Filter cleanliness: Because the diaphragms of your drippers are highly sensitive to sediment and sand, installing a filter with an appropriate mesh size (typically 120-150) is mandatory. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is drip irrigation suitable for paddy fields?

No, drip irrigation is generally not recommended for paddy fields and areas with highly saline water, unless the drip emitters are spaced very far apart or specialized filters are used. Mung bean is sensitive to salinity, and irrigation with saline water may cause new leaves to burn. Additionally, the accumulation of salts around the roots can gradually lead to a significant reduction in crop yield.

What is the maximum efficiency of mung bean cultivation using drip irrigation?

By applying the correct agricultural and technical principles, an increase of up to 25 to 30% in crop water use efficiency can be expected compared to flood irrigation methods. This increase in efficiency is due to reduced water loss and improved nutrient absorption by the plants.

Is it economically viable?

Yes. Although the initial installation cost of a drip system is higher than that of a sprinkler system, the reduced water consumption, lower need for herbicides, and increased crop yield result in a return on investment within one to two cropping seasons.
