The importance of precise moisture management in onion growth

Onion is a bulb crop whose growth is directly related to water uptake from various root zones. In recent years, climatic variability and water resource scarcity have forced farmers to adopt methods that not only reduce waste but also maintain product quality. Smart irrigation, utilizing soil moisture sensors and decision-making algorithms, ensures water is consumed exactly when and in the quantity required by the plant. This precision in moisture management is a key advantage, leading to uniform onion size and reduced waste.

The relationship between drip systems and onion root health

One of the main concerns in onion cultivation is root rot caused by temporary soil waterlogging. Drip irrigation systems, particularly when integrated with artificial intelligence or automation, prevent excessive water accumulation around the roots. Instead of wetting the entire soil surface, water is applied drop by drop to the root zone. This improves oxygen supply to the roots and makes the plant more resistant to fungal diseases. Practical tips for implementing smart onion irrigation

To benefit from the advantages of smart irrigation in onion cultivation, the correct implementation of the system, from technical design to maintenance, is vital. The following practical tips are provided:

Scheduling irrigation timing based on growth stages

  1. Initial vegetative stage: At this stage, frequent low-volume irrigation is recommended to prevent damage to delicate onion sprouts. The smart system must have the capability to adjust for low pressure.
  2. Onion bulb formation stage: This is the most critical period. Irrigation must be continuous and uniform for the onion to reach its final dimensions. Water deficiency during this period causes central hollowing of the bulb.
  3. Maturity and separation stage: Stepwise reduction of irrigation to help dry the outer skin and increase the shelf life of onions in storage.

Another important point is the periodic flushing of drip emitters. Salts in the water (when irrigating with dry soil well water) can clog the lines. New smart systems have the capability for automated reverse flushing, which is considered a significant operational advantage. Additionally, if using drip lines, ensure they are correctly placed under the leaves to prevent burning of terminal leaves by water spray.

Role of sensors in smart decision-making

The use of soil moisture sensors at different depths (e.g., 10 and 30 cm) is the key to the success of the smart system. These sensors send real-time data to the controller. Although onion is a medium root-density crop, precise knowledge of moisture at 30 cm depth allows the plant to develop deeper roots. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

You may also have concerns about investing in smart systems. In this section, we answer several common questions:

  • Is smart onion irrigation suitable for small farms as well? Yes, even on small farms, using smart timers and simple sensors can reduce water costs and prevent efficiency losses. The initial cost is offset by long-term savings.
  • Is drip irrigation prohibited for onions in sandy soils? Contrary to popular belief, drip irrigation is feasible in sandy soils if the system is precisely designed, but it requires closer spacing of drippers and more frequent irrigation. System intelligence is critical here to prevent water leaching into unreachable depths.
  • What is the difference between tape and drip irrigation for onions? Drip tape wets the cover crop more and can help suppress weeds in early stages, but precise moisture control during bulb formation is more effectively achieved with drip emitters. Choosing between the two depends on your soil conditions and weed management strategy.
