Understanding the Technical Specifications of 125 mm Pipe in Drip and Drip Tape Irrigation

Differences between threaded pipes and welded and screwed pipes

Irrigation pipes are generally categorized into two main types: screw (pipe) and tape tip (tape). However, for sizes larger than 63, 'threaded' pipes, more accurately described as polyethylene pipes with press joints (using standard press seals) and metal pipes with threaded (screwed) connections, are used. In modern tape tip systems, 125 mm pipes are typically made of HDPE polyethylene with a pressure rating of 6 or 10 bar. These pipes do not withstand sun exposure and require underground installation or protection from moisture and UV radiation. Due to high flexibility, easy installation, and resistance to expansion, these pipes are a better option for main farm lines compared to metal threaded pipes. Conversely, metal threaded pipes are used in the system inlet piping sections and pump stations where direct metal connections to industrial valves are required. To understand the variety of fittings, you can also take a look at the 90-degree pipe elbow which is used to transfer flow from the main pipe to lateral lines.

Why is size 125 the optimal point for medium-sized farms?

Pipe size selection is based on water flow velocity and pressure loss to the end of the system. For farms of approximately 1 to 5 hectares, a 125 mm pipe usually forms the Main Line. This size allows high water volumes to be transferred with lower pump pressure, resulting in reduced pump energy consumption. Selecting a smaller pipe (e.g., 63 mm) increases pressure loss, causing the end of the pipe to receive significantly less pressure than the start, which leads to uneven irrigation. For better understanding of size calculations, studying the method for calculating drip pipe sizes is recommended.

Key points in selecting and purchasing 125 mm pipes

Material, wall thickness, and production standard

One of the most important points in purchasing is identifying the polymer material used. High-quality pipes are produced from HDPE with code 500 or 8000, offering a long service life. Pipe wall thickness also affects its working pressure. For example, a 125 mm pipe with a wall thickness of 14/5 mm typically withstands a working pressure of 6 bar, while a thickness of 4/6 mm allows for a higher working pressure. When purchasing, always pay attention to weld connections (for metal pipes) or crimp connections (for plastic pipes). Using high-quality threaded or crimped connections prevents long-term water leakage.

Calculation bases for estimating the final price

The price of pipes depends on several key factors: the real-time price of HDPE roll stock, wall thickness, manufacturer brand, and service level. Instead of searching for the 'cheapest' pipe, you should think about the 'lowest cost over useful life.' Cheaper pipes with thinner walls may seem attractive in the first one or two seasons, but they may suffer cracking or UV radiation-induced cracking in the second or third year, leading to replacement and repair costs that exceed the initial expense. Additionally, in fields that require 125 mm pipes for main lines, calculating water maintenance costs can demonstrate the economic advantage of choosing this size.

Practical guide to reducing purchase and installation costs

  1. Precise calculation of pipe meters: Map your farm and identify main and lateral lines. Usually, one meter of main pipe is required for every meter of drip line length.
  2. Selecting pipes with ISO 5082 or 5817 standards: This ensures higher pipe durability.
  3. Using expansion joints: In subsurface farms, crimp connections with low expansion reduce re-entry costs.
  4. Price comparison per unit meter: Ignore per-kilometer pricing; sum all required pipe, fittings, and flanges in meters, then divide by the unit meter.

Frequently Asked Questions About 125 Pipe

Is 125 pipe suitable for small farms?

For farms under 1000 square meters, 125 mm pipe is excessively large and expensive. Typically, 63 mm or 80 mm pipes are sufficient for these fields. Using a larger size increases the water volume inside the pipe and requires faster system filling, which can increase peak demand and pressure on the pump. Therefore, always first Water management plan Calculate the farm and then select the size.

What is the difference between 125mm and 90mm pipes?

90mm pipes (twin-wall) are generally used for sub-branch lines and are more cost-effective. However, the main line, which is responsible for supplying water to multiple points, uses a 125mm diameter to maintain flow velocity and reduce pressure loss. In 90mm external pipe, the flow velocity is higher, which can be problematic for main lines, especially if the line length is short. Combining these two pipe sizes on large farms optimizes pressure and cost.

Which brands of 125mm pipe are available in Iran, and how do their prices compare?

The Iranian market offers a wide variety of HDPE manufacturing brands. When comparing prices, it is more useful to search for the real-time price per meter of pipe rather than by brand name. Prices fluctuate due to currency exchange rates. However, reputable brands with written guarantees usually do not differ significantly in price from unverified brands, but they do differ in service life and connection quality.
