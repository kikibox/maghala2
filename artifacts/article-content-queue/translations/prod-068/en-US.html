Introduction to the Importance of the Main Network in Drip and Tape Irrigation

The correct selection of various components in the design of drip and tape irrigation systems directly affects crop yield, water efficiency, and the useful life of the system. Among these, 110 millimeter threaded pipe plays a vital role as one of the main components for water transmission in main (mother) networks and high-flow sub-lines. Many farmers, when purchasing, pay attention only to the price or appearance of the pipe, unaware of how the quality of threaded connections, polyethylene density, and pressure resistance have a profound impact on system stability.

In the Iranian market, there are various brands of polyethylene pipes available, ranging from imported brands to domestic products with different standards. This article examines in detail the technical and practical differences between reputable brands of 110 millimeter threaded pipe and cheaper or lower-quality options. Our goal is to provide an impartial guide based on technical data so that you can make fully informed decisions. Remember that these pipes are generally used for higher pressures and medium to large diameters, and proper connection is the key to optimal performance.

Why 110 Millimeter Threaded Pipe?

Polyethylene pipes with a 110 millimeter diameter are often used in the main supply lines of large farms and industrial livestock operations due to their high pressure resistance and the ability to handle high flow rates. Threaded connections (screw fittings), compared to butt or welding connections, increase installation speed and are openable if repairs are needed. However, this feature means greater precision is required during installation and the use of PTFE tape or specialized adhesive is necessary to prevent leaks.

Technical Features and Raw Materials: Different Brands

The biggest difference between brands of 110 millimeter threaded pipe is the type and grade of polyethylene (PE) used. While all brands claim to use pure polyethylene, subtle differences in the percentages of stabilizers and antioxidants determine the useful life of the pipe.

  • Raw material grade (PE80 vs PE100): Some economic brands use PE80, which is not ideal for main high-pressure lines with a diameter of 110, as it has less flexibility at low temperatures. More reputable brands utilize PE100 with a higher pressure rating, which is more resistant to cold temperatures.
  • Stabilizer ratio: High-quality pipes (usually European brands or domestic export-quality brands) have a higher percentage of stabilizers. This prevents the pipe from cracking and turning white when exposed to direct sunlight.
  • Thread quality: For threaded pipes, the accuracy of the threading (tapping) at the pipe ends is critical. If the threads are rough, there is a risk of thread damage and water leakage when tightening the nuts. Top brands use more precise machinery for threading.

Visual comparison and label tags

One of the immediate indicators for assessing quality is the labeling on the pipe body. Standard pipes feature a black strip (for pure PE) or a blue strip (for PE100). Unbranded or lesser-known brands sometimes lack this strip or use the wrong color. Additionally, the metadata label must include the national standard, pressure rating, pipe spool length, and manufacturing date. The absence of any of these items is a warning sign.

The 110 millimeter thread pipe generates less friction due to its more regular internal cross-section compared to ribbed pipes. However, this advantage is realized only when connections are installed correctly.

Practical experience shows that cheaper thread pipes undergo slight longitudinal stretching under high temperatures and continuous pressure, which leads to loose connections. This phenomenon is less observed in more expensive, high-quality brands because their polymer molecular structure is denser. For agriculture in hot climates, it is essential to select a brand that maintains pressure stability at 50 degrees Celsius.

Practical tips for installation and maintenance of 110mm threaded pipe

Loosening the nuts during the installation of 110 mm threaded pipe reveals minor differences between brands. Unlike butt-joint pipes, which only need to be joined end-to-end, threaded connections require precision tools and greater accuracy. The following are the key execution points for maximizing system efficiency.

  1. Use of Teflon tape or hemp: Before connecting, always use high-quality Teflon tape. Using hemp or cheaper plastic tape leads to deterioration and leaks. The number of wraps must be optimized (usually 4 to 5 wraps for 1/2 inch threads and larger).
  2. Straightness of the connection: For threaded pipes, the angle of water entry and the nut must be perfectly perpendicular. Any lateral pressure on the pipe threads will cause immediate failure. Avoid placing the pipe on rocks or sharp tools.
  3. Calculating pressure while accounting for friction: When designing the network, always use the Darcy-Weisbach formula or Hagen-Poiseuille charts to calculate pressure drop in the 110 mm pipe. High-quality pipes have smoother internal surfaces, which reduces pressure drop.
  4. Calculating burial depth: If your irrigation system drip guide for cucumbers or you are implementing sensitive plants, main pipes should not be buried at shallow depths to protect them from mechanical stresses (such as soil subsidence) in fields.

Troubleshooting common leaks

If a leak is observed after installation, the first suspect should be the threaded connections. Check whether the Teflon tape has torn or if the plastic threads have sheared off due to excessive torque. In low-quality brands, pipe plastic is brittle and a hard impact can create micro-cracks that expand over time.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Below are answers to common farmer questions about 110 mm threaded pipes:

Is 110mm threaded pipe suitable for all lines?

No. 110mm pipe is generally used for main lines with a flow rate exceeding 1000 liters per minute. For drip irrigation sub-lines, smaller diameters such as 50mm or 75mm are more efficient. Using 110mm pipe for sub-lines increases the volume of water in the pipe and reduces pressure at the drip line head.

What is the difference between 110mm threaded pipe and 110mm square pipe?

The main difference is in the connection method. Square pipe is installed faster with square connections and has less leakage when installed correctly. However, threaded pipe is detachable, making it more suitable for locations requiring frequent maintenance or difficult access. Additionally, square pipe usually offers better pricing.

Can pipes from different brands be connected to each other?

It is generally not recommended to directly connect pipes from different brands unless standard adapters are used. This is because the wall thickness, thread length, and polymer temperature differ between brands. Doing so may create a weak point in the system. It is better to use metal (iron) fittings as an intermediary.

What is the lifespan of 110mm pipe?

With proper maintenance and burial at an appropriate depth, high-quality PE100 pipes can last for over 50 years. However, under conditions of direct sun exposure or repeated mechanical stress, this lifespan may decrease to 10 to 15 years.
