If your drip tape does not have inline emitters and you need to connect separate emitters, understanding the hydraulic principles of 'drip tape compatibility with emitters' is vital to prevent water waste and root rot. This article, focusing on technical and practical aspects, demonstrates how to utilize this combination in the best possible way.

Hydraulic principles of 20 cm drip tape compatibility

The 20 cm drip tape, due to its smaller diameter compared to 25 or 27 cm tapes, experiences a higher internal pressure drop along the 1000 m roll. This characteristic directly affects the performance of the emitters connected to it. When using separate emitters, the input pressure to the emitters may differ between the beginning and the end of the line. To maintain uniform irrigation, you must select emitters that have self-regulating orifices . These orifices compensate for slight pressure differences by creating controlled resistance and guarantee a constant output flow rate. Otherwise, simple emitters without self-regulating mechanisms will result in over-irrigation at the beginning of the lines and dryness at the end of the roll.

Impact of orifice on irrigation precision

The emitter is the heart of every dripper. In a combination of a 20 cm ribbon with lateral drippers, the water flow pattern within the main line is critical. If the drippers are installed at irregular intervals, or if the emitter orifice diameter is not proportional to the system pressure, 'flow turbulence' occurs. Therefore, when assessing the compatibility of a ribbon type with a dripper, pay special attention to the emitter flow rate (LPH). For 20 cm ribbons, emitters with a flow rate of 1.2 to 2.4 liters per hour are typically selected to prevent excessive pressure on the ribbon wall.

Practical tips for connecting and installing drippers

Correctly installing drippers on a 20 cm ribbon tube with a 1000 m roll requires precise care. Porosity (leakage) or the use of inappropriate fittings can cause pressure loss and reduce the overall efficiency of the system. Here are several practical tips to ensure full compatibility:

  • Use of appropriate fittings: Use press or clamp fittings specifically designed for 20 mm plastic ribbon tubes. Generic fittings may weaken the ribbon wall at the dripper connection point.
  • Allowable operating pressure: Most lateral drippers operate optimally in the range of 1 to 2 bars. Supplying pressure higher than this level without a pressure regulator can reduce the emitter's service life and apply disproportionate pressure to the ribbon tube.
  • Stringing: Where possible, use inline filters before water reaches the drippers to prevent sediment particles from entering the emitter orifice.

Additionally, when unrolling the 1000 m reel, ensure that the drippers are positioned on the correct upper or lateral side to prevent direct water impact with the soil at inappropriate depths. To better understand the correct layout, you can refer to the drip line installation guide to learn the principles of proper emitter placement.

Pressure management in lateral lines

Since the 1000 m reel has a very high length, pressure drop at the end of the line may reach up to 20% compared to the start. If you are using non-pressure-compensating self-compensating drippers, you must shorten the lateral lines or reduce the system’s initial pressure to keep the flow rate difference between the start and end of the line below 10%. This maintains irrigation quality and prevents wear of the drip line in high-pressure zones.

Challenges in filter maintenance and flushing

The compatibility of drip tape with emitters is not limited to technical dimensions; it also requires systematic maintenance planning. Lateral drip lines are prone to clogging by silt and sediment due to their small orifices and complex flow paths. In 1000-meter rolls, a single weakness in the filtration system can rapidly render dozens of emitters ineffective. Therefore, periodic flushing of sand and disc filters is a critical task. Additionally, a visual inspection at the start of each irrigation cycle to locate clogged emitters is essential. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is the 20 cm drip tape suitable for all land slopes?

Not necessarily. 20 cm tapes are generally designed for lands with low to moderate slopes and standard system pressures. On steep slopes, pressure drop at the end of the roll is critical, and even automatic pressure-compensating drippers may not fully offset it. In such cases, it is recommended to use drippers with higher flow rates or split the roll into two 500 m halves. For better selection based on field conditions, refer to the guide on Sponge yield per hectare and how system type affects overall efficiency. Although this resource focuses more on pistachio crops, its hydraulic principles are applicable to other orchards requiring precise water management.

Can I attach old drippers to new drip tape?

Connecting worn drippers or those with clogged orifices to new 20 cm drip tape poses a high risk of blocking main filters. Eroded polymer particles from old drippers easily pass through filters and clog the small orifices of healthy drippers. Always use new, standard-compliant drippers and ensure compatibility when purchasing equipment to avoid mismatches.
