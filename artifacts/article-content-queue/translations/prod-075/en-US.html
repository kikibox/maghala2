Why are 110mm threaded pipes selected for irrigation systems?

In the design of drip and tape-line irrigation networks, the choice of the mainline or pressure supply pipe (Main Line) is a critical engineering consideration. 110mm-diameter pipes, especially models with internal threaded couplings, are widely used in medium to large-scale projects due to their high pressure resistance and rapid, secure connectivity. Unlike socket fusion pipes, which require heat fusion and specialized equipment, threaded systems allow connections to be made quickly with simple tools, making them highly suitable for projects requiring frequent maintenance or rapid system repositioning.

However, a crucial point to note is that 110mm threaded pipes are typically designed for high pressure, and improper use can lead to joint leaks or even pipe rupture. Understanding the internal structure of these fittings and how they interact with flow pressure is a necessary prerequisite for any correct installation. An overview of threaded fitting types and fault diagnosis

Difference between a cap and an AC (American Coupling) fitting

In threaded piping, two types of screw joints (Couplings) are common. The AC (American Coupling) type is used to connect two pipe ends together and features threads on both sides. The cap (Blind Cap) type is similar but is used to close the end of a pipe. When purchasing, ensure that the fitting material is compatible with the pipe material (usually HDPE with an appropriate hardness rating). Signs of a high-quality fitting include perfectly smooth threads and the absence of polyurethane (grease or lint) on the threads.

Troubleshooting thread leaks

The most common issue when installing threaded pipes is leakage at the joints. This leakage is usually caused by improperly tightened connections, cross-threading, or the use of unsuitable sealing tape (thread seal tape). In high-pressure irrigation systems, even 1 mm of looseness can lead to serious leakage after a few days. Always bring the system to operating pressure after initial installation, and after draining, perform a "final tighten" on the bolts and nuts with careful inspection. Step-by-step installation of 110 threaded pipe

  1. Path Preparation: Before gluing or threading connections, the pipe path must be level and free of sharp stones or tree roots. Soil settlement in the path can gradually cause internal thread wear or pipe deformation.
  2. Precise Pipe Cutting: The pipe must be cut vertically (90 degrees) with a specific saw or precise blade. Any angle at the pipe end will prevent the connection from fully engaging the threads, resulting in permanent leakage. The cut edge must be clean and free of burrs.
  3. Joint Installation with Sealing Tape: When threading a fitting, use PTFE tape (Teflon tape) or thread sealant. Ensure the sealant does not enter the pipe bore, as it may clog internal irrigation system components. Wind the PTFE tape onto the thread in 3 to 4 turns in the direction of tightening.
  4. Connection and tightening: Hand-tighten the fitting to prevent initial loosening. Then, apply moderate pressure using the appropriate tool (for 110mm sizes, typically a box wrench or a large open-end wrench). Over-tightening can damage the pipe thread.

Practical tips for extending the useful life of the system

Protecting the hose from sun exposure

Ultraviolet (UV) radiation is the primary enemy of polyethylene. If 100mm or 110mm hoses are exposed outdoors, they must be covered with soil or replaced with UV-resistant (blue) hoses. Exposed hoses will quickly become brittle and rupture under the first pressure surge if subjected to direct sunlight.

Back-Seating of Hose Connections

An advanced technique to prevent leaks involves controlled back-seating of the connection after initial use. Within the first 24 to 48 hours, soil settlement and temperature fluctuations may cause the joint to loosen slightly. Re-tightening (not restarting the connection, but only securing it further) ensures system integrity. This step is critical in fields with significant diurnal temperature variations.

Open pipe end

Do not leave the end of the main pipe line open unless the pressure at that point is very low. In high-pressure systems, water spraying from an open end causes soil erosion and fatigue of the pipe head or cap. Using a standard cap is the best solution. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can NX 110 pipe be installed at low pressure?

Yes, but with caution. 110 pipes are typically high-pressure class (10 or 16 bar). Using them in low-pressure systems (below 4 bar) is economically expensive, but technically it causes no problems unless the connections are not correctly calculated. Thick pipes have greater flexibility against impact in low-pressure conditions.

What tool is needed to tighten a 110 connection?

For a 110 millimeter diameter, a 60 or 80 millimeter box wrench is typically used alongside a pipe wrench or an appropriate jaw wrench. The use of a pipe cutter is prohibited as it can damage the pipe body and threads.
