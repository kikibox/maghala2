Introduction: The necessity of optimal water management in intercropping systems

Modern agriculture in Iran and neighboring countries requires a precise and scientific approach to water resources. Combining orchard fruit cultivation with short-term seasonal crops like mung beans creates specific technical and economic challenges for farmers. On one hand, fruit trees, due to their longer growth cycle and need for nutrient accumulation, require deep root establishment and regular, precise irrigation. On the other hand, mung beans, as a crop with a very short growth cycle (usually 60 to 90 days), have variable irrigation stations that must be managed to avoid interfering with tree irrigation. Selecting an appropriate and integrated irrigation system can create a significant difference in the maintenance costs of tape and drip systems. The main objective of this section is to understand irrigation mechanisms that simultaneously maintain fruit tree health and prevent water stress while meeting the water needs of mung beans with minimal consumption and avoiding harmful root competition.

Technical comparison of drip and tape systems for orchards and farms

Many farmers hesitate when designing new irrigation systems regarding the choice between drip and tape. Both methods are part of pressurized systems but have different applications in various cropping contexts. In fruit orchards, drip systems are often the first choice due to uniform moisture distribution in the tree's main root zone and reduced evaporation. However, for mung bean cultivation, which has high plant density and rapid vegetative growth, tape systems can be a more economical option, provided that the depth of the wet soil layer is controlled to prevent roots from seeking water at depth and crowding tree roots.

Key differences in performance and cost

  • Drip system: Water is dispensed drop by drop from 4 to 16 liter-per-hour stations under pressure. This system requires a higher initial investment, but over the long term, it achieves significant financial savings by reducing evaporation and increasing water use efficiency. Self-regulating drippers are the best option for sloping orchards.
  • Drip tape: Plastic or polyethylene tapes that distribute water as a thin layer over the soil surface in a specific zone. This method is ideal for early-season crops with narrow spacing, such as mung beans. The installation cost is typically 30 to 40 percent lower than a full drip irrigation system; however, the risk of emitter clogging is high if water filtration is inadequate.

Field cost analysis: From initial investment to efficiency

The cost of field irrigation includes more than just the purchase of pipes and drip lines; it also covers installation, pumping energy, equipment depreciation, and annual maintenance expenses. Many farmers focus heavily on the high price tags of initial equipment, often overlooking the time savings achieved by replacing manual irrigation with these systems. Modern irrigation systems reduce manual labor by 50 to 70 percent and enable more precise watering. Cost analyses should also include the Return on Investment (ROI) to determine how many seasons it will take for the increased crop yield to offset the system costs.

Factors affecting increased yield and reduced costs

  1. Filtration Quality: Installing the correct filter (sand, disc, or screen) prevents clogging of drip emitters and tape lines. Upstream blockages significantly increase repair and replacement costs and reduce system efficiency. It is recommended to refer to tape maintenance and cost guidelines to plan precise filter cleaning and line inspections.
  2. Layout Design: Coordination of crop production line interference with irrigation lines. In intercropping systems, pathways for tractor and machinery traffic between trees and crop rows must be optimized to prevent damage to pipes.
  3. Equipment Lifespan: Using high-lifespan steel screws and fasteners reduces component replacement costs. Additionally, selecting pipes with appropriate thickness based on system pressure prevents sudden ruptures.

Practical tips for mung bean cultivation in orchard shade

Mung bean is one of the best options for intercropping in fruit orchards, particularly in the early years of orchard establishment when tree canopy shade is not yet dense. Because mung bean is less sensitive to shading than light-demanding plants such as green tomatoes or watermelon, it can effectively cover open spaces. However, the main challenge is managing root competition and differing water requirements. Fruit trees require stable moisture at depth, whereas mung bean is sensitive to repeated, lighter irrigation at shallow soil levels.

Practical solutions for irrigation management

  • Mulching: Using straw, plastic, or polyethylene film mulch on mung bean drip lines preserves soil moisture and prevents weed growth. This practice can reduce mung bean water requirements by up to 20%.
  • Irrigation scheduling and pressure: Since orchard drippers are typically installed on tree lines, the dedicated mulch drip tape section must be separated from the main network using independent control valves to prevent pressure drop and maintain uniform distribution. Cucumber irrigation scheduling should occur before the final fruiting of the trees to avoid severe competition.
  • Soil Monitoring: In arid regions, replace filters seasonally. Using soil moisture tensiometers at various depths helps farmers determine the exact time to irrigate when moisture reaches the minimum retraction point.

Important note: If you are using a flood system for the orchard and plan to add a mulch drip tape for cucumbers, verify whether direct connection to the central pressure network is possible, as the pressure required for mulch drip differs from that of flood irrigation. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is mulch drip suitable for pistachio orchards as well?

For pistachio orchards, drip irrigation is generally recommended due to the need for deep soil moisture and canopy control. However, strip line systems can be used for soil coverage in the inter-row strips to maintain deep moisture and prevent excessive soil saturation. To view practical examples and similar technical principles, see the article Knowledge-based in orchards and water issues that addresses technical challenges in modern orchard management.

How to reduce pumping costs?

Using high-efficiency centrifugal pumps and irrigating during cooler nighttime hours (with a timer controller) can reduce electricity costs by up to 15%. Additionally, reducing leaks by closing extra valves and using standard threaded fittings is crucial. Regular drip line flushing at the end of each irrigation cycle removes sediment from pipes and extends the system's lifespan.
