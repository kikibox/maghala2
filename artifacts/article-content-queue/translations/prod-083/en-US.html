Introduction to the importance of high-pressure pipes in irrigation

In modern agriculture, particularly in drip and belt irrigation systems, selecting the right high-pressure equipment is considered a fundamental pillar of success. 90 mm threaded pipe It is one of the key elements that facilitates water transfer from the surge or injection point to the belt tips. Due to its appropriate flow rate and acceptable resistance, this pipe size is widely used in many fruit orchards and industrial vegetable farms. In this section of the buying guide, our aim is to familiarize you with the structure, application, and technical details of this product so that you can make an informed investment.

Selecting a high-pressure pipe is not just about its price; connection quality, UV resistance, and dimensional stability are also important factors. 90 mm pipes are generally made of polyethylene (PE) with a high-pressure grade (SDR11 or SDR17) and are available in two types: without flanges and with flanges. However, for the 90 mm size, drill or heat poly connections, or threaded connections, are commonly used, which is the subject of our discussion.

Technical and structural features of 90 mm threaded pipe

To better understand whether this pipe is suitable for your application, we must examine its exact technical specifications. Pipes with threaded couplings have standard threads based on ISO, allowing direct connection with shut-off valves, vents, and meters. This feature is ideal for systems that require faster disconnection at various points in the network.

Raw material and chemical grade

The primary material of high-pressure pipes is usually high-density polyethylene (HDPE). The material grade (such as PE100) determines the useful life of the pipe and its resistance to impact and internal pressure. When buying, ensure that the pipe label includes the Iranian National Standard mark and the CE symbol. The absence of these marks may indicate the use of low-quality recycled materials that can lead to cracking and leakage over time.

Differences between fusion pipes and threaded pipes

For larger sizes like 90 millimeters, many experts recommend using fusion joints to ensure a leak-free connection. However, pre-made pipes with threaded joints offer faster installation and are very suitable for smaller or remedial projects. Inspection of pipe length and fittings

High-pressure pipes are usually produced in 100-meter rolls or 6-meter scissor cuts. 6-meter scissor cut pipes with nuts and bolts are very popular for repair works. When purchasing, always check that the screws are galvanized steel; galvanized or cast iron screws are better options due to the possibility of corrosion with the oceanic environment. Also, the length of the threads inside the pipe must be long enough so that after tightening, the flange and gasket are fully seated and no leak occurs.

Impact of diameter and pressure on selection

90mm groove pipe (OD) generally has a thicker wall compared to 63mm pipe in order to increase working pressure. If the irrigation pressure in your field is above 2.5 bar, always use SDR11 grade pipe. The use of SDR17 at high pressures can cause unwanted stretching or pipe rupture. Application in Tip and Drip Systems

90 mm pipes are typically used as sub-main lines in large-scale systems. If you use wider drip tapes in valley farms, this pipe size is your primary choice. Correctly connecting these pipes to drip tape components directly affects water pressure integrity across the entire row. Minor leaks in 90 mm threaded connections can cause dryness at the end of the drip tape row, leading to reduced crop yield.

Watering the Soil and Roots

The selection of high-pressure pipe size should be based on plant root depth and soil type. For plants with shallow roots, lower pressure and appropriately sized pipes are sufficient, but for deep-rooted crops, higher pressure is required. If your soil is clayey, you may need to increase the pressure slightly; in this case, the pressure resistance of the 90 mm SDR11 pipe is critical.

Flow and capacity calculation

The nominal capacity of a 90 mm pipe with a conventional flow velocity of approximately 2 to 3 m/s is very high. Therefore, this pipe is typically over-sized for the capacity required by a single standard strip, unless multiple strips are connected in parallel to one outlet. In this case, the 90 mm pipe can supply multiple 200 m or 300 m strips. Always calculate the total farm flow before purchase to prevent over-pressurization.

Answers to Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can a 90 mm pipe be left exposed to open air and direct sunlight?

  • Answer: Standard HDPE pipes are UV-resistant, but over the long term, their color fades and their strength gradually decreases. It is recommended that proper covering be used if the pipe is stored.

What is the difference between threaded and fusion connections for a 90 mm pipe?

  • Answer: Threaded connections allow for faster installation and are suitable for reversible repairs, but they have a higher risk of leakage. Welded connections offer a longer service life and complete leak resistance, but they require specialized equipment (thermoplastic welding) and repairs are difficult.

Is this pipe diameter necessary for buckwheat and marginal crops?

  • Response: For small-scale fields, a 63mm pipe may be sufficient. However, for fields growing varieties with high water demand, a 90mm pipe is the better option due to reduced pressure loss. You can estimate water demand by examining the buckwheat seed rate per hectare and its initial growth rate.
