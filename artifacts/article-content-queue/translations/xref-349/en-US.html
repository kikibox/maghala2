Introduction: The Importance of Moisture Management in Green Manure Cropping

Green manure (کشت گلرنگ) is a cornerstone of sustainable agriculture, aimed at fixing nitrogen, increasing soil organic matter, and controlling soil-borne diseases. However, the main challenge in this method is maintaining soil moisture balance. Excessive moisture can cause plant nodes to rot, while insufficient moisture halts leaf growth. Moisture Irrigation Targeted application is the key to success in this critical stage. Below, we compare two widely used methods, drip irrigation and tape irrigation, in terms of efficiency, cost, and impact on final yield.

The goal of comparing these two methods is to find the optimal option based on soil type, budget, and access to water resources. Green manure cropping is typically done with varieties of lentils, vetch, or fava beans, each having different sensitivities to moisture stress.

Technical Analysis of Moisture Irrigation Methods in Green Manure

1. Drip Irrigation: High Precision and Lower Long-Term Costs

Drip systems are the best option for clay soils due to their uniform water distribution at the plant root zone, where surface irrigation often causes the formation of a hard surface layer. In green manure cropping, subterranean or surface emitters placed at 20 to 30 centimeter intervals keep moisture precisely at the plant root.

  • Advantages: Reduces evaporation by up to 40%, prevents nutrient leaching, and enables the use of soluble fertilizers (fertigation).
  • Disadvantages: Higher initial cost, requires precise filtration to prevent clogging of emitters, and is sensitive to calcium deposits.

2. Drip Tape: High Speed and Wide Coverage

The drip tape method, which is essentially a temporary form of drip irrigation using thin tubing, is more suitable for grain crops and plants with moderate planting density. In cover cropping, 16 or 20 mm drip tapes are laid diagonally or parallel to the planting rows.

  • Advantages: Rapid installation, lower cost than permanent drip systems, and uniform soil coverage along the strips.
  • Disadvantages: Requires annual replacement; risk of dry zones between furrows in loose soils.

To better understand the yield differences between these methods, it is important to examine the factors affecting crop yield per hectare. If the soil is clayey and rainfall is low, drip irrigation has the advantage. However, if the soil is sandy and water access is expensive, furrow irrigation may achieve better efficiency by reducing water consumption. One key aspect of cover cropping is controlling damage from grazing animals and weeds, which can sometimes reduce yields. While modern irrigation systems are not directly linked to herbicide use, they promote uniform growth of the cover crop, increase soil coverage, and suppress weed growth. For example, if your goal is to increase celery yield per hectare, which requires high soil moisture, a drip irrigation system offers better control and lower risk compared to flood irrigation. In addition to water management, combining cover crops with other plants is also important. Some farmers integrate cover crops with grain crops. In this case, selecting the right irrigation system for both stages is critical. For example, if you plan to use cover crops as a pre-crop for products such as carrots, understanding carrot quantity per hectare and their water requirements will help you adjust irrigation pressure.

Key points for successful moisture irrigation

  1. Identify soil type: Before selecting a system, conduct a soil infiltration test. Loose soils require frequent, low-volume irrigation.
  2. Pre-irrigation: Before planting cover crops, apply a deep irrigation cycle to establish soil moisture at a depth of 30 cm.
  3. Use of moisture sensors: In jasmine cultivation zones, installing soil moisture sensors at 15 and 45 cm depths helps prevent over-irrigation.
  4. Pressure management: The output pressure of the drip irrigation system must be between 1 and 2 bar. High pressure causes emitter breakage, while low pressure leads to clogging.

One reason for crop failure in cover cropping is incorrect species selection. If the soil is acidic, leguminous species such as oats are the best option. Irrigation scheduling

The best time for wet irrigation is early morning to minimize evaporation. In border strip systems, the irrigation duration should be 30 to 45 minutes to ensure the soil in the covered strip is wetted, but water does not flow into the adjacent furrows. In drip systems, timing is adjusted based on the flow rate of emitters (usually 0.5 or 1 liter per hour).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is drip irrigation cost-effective for turnips?

In the short term, no, due to the initial costs. However, in the long term (at least 5 years), it becomes profitable gradually by reducing water consumption and increasing soil efficiency, thanks to the decreased need for liming and fertilizers. For single-season crops, a trickle line is a better option.

How can we prevent clogging of a trickle line?

Use hydroxyethyl cellulose as a biocide and install 120-mesh (micron) filters at the system inlet. Never use water with high sediment content in these systems.

What is the impact of irrigation on the roots of turnips?

Poor irrigation (prolonged flooding) causes root rot. Drip irrigation, by maintaining moisture at the permanent wilting point, helps roots breathe and strengthens the root system.

Conclusion and final recommendations

Choosing between Garden crop moisture irrigation with drip or tape methods, depending on soil conditions, budget, and the active season. For large farms and clay soils, a fixed drip system is a more valuable investment. For green crops in small farms or arid regions, tape systems offer greater speed and flexibility.

Always remember that the ultimate goal of garden cropping is to improve soil quality for subsequent crops. Therefore, precise moisture control is only the first step. The second step is properly managing main crops.
