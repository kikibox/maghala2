Why is choosing a 90 mm pipe critical in modern irrigation?

Operational stages for installing 90-millimeter pipe

Proper installation execution requires adherence to a logical sequence of operations. Negligence at any stage can jeopardize the entire project.

  1. Path reverse engineering: Before cutting, mark the final path of the main line on the ground from the reservoir to the first branch. The pipe slope should follow the ground contour to facilitate air venting. Downhill sections require dowel vents to collect air.
  2. Precise pipe cutting: Use a hacksaw or a dedicated pipe cutter to cut the pipe squarely, ensuring it is perfectly perpendicular to the longitudinal axis. A slanted cut prevents the O-ring seal from seating fully, leading to leaks.
  3. Deburring and cleaning: Clean the interior of the pipe of all grease, dust, and cutting chips. Any suspended particle may become lodged in the tip tape strip.
  4. Installing couplings: Position the pipe at the joint so the internal gasket is seated. Secure the connection by tightening the metal or plastic band over the pipe's spigot. Ensure the tightening torque does not deform the band.
  5. Installing branches (Tees and Elbows): Use appropriate branch connectors to connect drip or tape lines to the 90mm main line. Adjust the spacing between branches to match the outlet flow rate of each line so that pressure loss at the end of the lines remains within acceptable limits.

Safety notes and pipe support

A 90mm pipe under water pressure has significant weight. In open areas, use plastic or wooden supports every 3 to 4 meters to prevent the pipe from contacting the ground or sharp stones. On steep slopes, use cleats to prevent longitudinal pipe stretching. If your system includes moisture-sensitive crops such as spinach, precise control of branch flow rates through valves is critical to prevent root waterlogging.

Pressure testing and system troubleshooting after installation

After connecting all drip or tape lines to the 90mm main line, test the system in a closed state (with an end cap on the line). Start the pump and gradually increase the pressure to the system's maximum rated value (typically 3 to 5 bar for this type of pipe).

  • Leak check: As water flows, feel all coupling connections and branches with your hand. The smallest water seep indicates the need to reopen the connection.
  • Air test: With the air vents open, ensure the water flows smoothly and without bubbles. Air pockets in the system cause pipe vibration and fitting corrosion.
  • Flow stability: Measure the flow rate at the end of the lines furthest from the source, where flow is laminar. If the pressure drop exceeds 15 percent of the nominal nozzle values, optimize the route or increase the diameter.

Frequently asked questions about 90 threaded pipe installation

Is it possible to use a 90 mm pipe for 16 mm drip lines?

Yes, a 90 mm pipe is used as the main line to supply multiple drip lines. However, the number of branch connections must be controlled so that the pressure drop at the end of the drip lines does not exceed the acceptable limit (usually 20%). The use of flow control valves at branch points is also recommended.

What is the appropriate support spacing for a 90 mm pipe on straight paths?

On straight paths with minimal slope, place supports every 3 to 4 meters. In sloped areas or where the line crosses natural obstacles, reduce the spacing to 1.5 meters. Ensuring the pipe is not overly taut allows it to expand freely due to summer heat without subjecting fittings to tensile stress.

Can a 90 mm pipe be combined with 63 mm or 125 mm pipes?

Combining different pipe diameters using a ditch or reducing connectors is possible. However, in a standard drip system, it is best to avoid frequent diameter changes to keep the pressure drop predictable. If diameter changes are necessary, precise hydraulic calculations must be performed for these specific combinations.
