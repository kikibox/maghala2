Introduction to Surface Irrigation in Garlic Cultivation

Garlic is a key agricultural crop in Iran that requires precision and proper management of water resources. Among the various irrigation methods, surface irrigation is preferred by many farmers due to its simplicity and low cost. However, optimizing performance with this method requires adherence to specific principles, which we will examine in the following sections. Economically, this method is generally cheaper compared to modern systems, as seen in the cost analysis of drip line cost per hectare , which shows it involves a higher initial investment.

In surface irrigation, open water is applied to the soil and delivered to the field through surface channels. Although this method has a higher water consumption than drip and line irrigation, it can yield favorable results for garlic cultivation due to the crop's sensitivity to soil moisture. Key points in the timing of surface irrigation for garlic

Emergence from winter dormancy and start of growth

The first sensitive stage in garlic cultivation is the emergence from winter dormancy and the start of the variable vegetative stage. During this period, the plant is highly sensitive to moisture. Surface irrigation at this stage should be applied with a low volume (about 5 to 7 cm) at intervals of 10 to 14 days. This prevents root desiccation and growth stagnation. Precise monitoring of soil moisture with a soil moisture meter is critical at this stage to avoid excessive watering, which can promote soil-borne fungi.

Rapid growth and tillering stage

In mid-growth season, the plant's growth rate increases significantly. At this stage, the irrigation volume increases to 10 to 12 cm, and the interval between irrigations decreases to 7 to 10 days. It is essential to consider rainfall and air temperature to adjust these volumes. In regions with severe temperature fluctuations, as discussed in Hektia light maintenance (which refers to temperature stability), surface irrigation requirements must also be managed more precisely to prevent thermal stress from damaging the roots.

Tiller Formation and Water Reduction Phase

After complete tiller formation, moisture requirements gradually decrease. In the final weeks, irrigation frequency changes to once every 15 to 20 days to ensure better stalk development inside the spikes. Sudden water cutoff before the scheduled time can cause stalks to break during harvest. Therefore, soil moisture should be reduced gradually until reaching the wilting point threshold.

Practical Tips for Implementing Surface Irrigation

Proper Bed Preparation

Irrigation beds or furrows should be shaped so that the height difference from the head to the tail of a bed is less than 15 centimeters. This ensures uniform water distribution along the entire length of the bed. The use of laser technology is recommended for leveling beds. Inaccurate leveling causes water to stagnate at the end of the bed, leading to root diseases. In large-scale projects, utilizing principles Strip System Laying Steps may be useful even in modifying surface irrigation canals, as the principles of gravity and correct sloping are common to both methods.

Water Flow Velocity

Water flow velocity in furrows must not exceed 50 to 60 centimeters per second to prevent soil erosion and root damage. If the water flow is excessively fast, the velocity should be reduced by decreasing the depth or increasing the width of the furrows. Controlling flow velocity can be achieved by installing check dams or divers in main channels. This practice not only ensures soil protection but also allows water to enter the furrow at a more suitable temperature, which is of paramount importance for the sensitive roots of onions.

Drainage Management

In areas prone to waterlogging, surface irrigation requires the design of an appropriate drainage system. Regular discharge of excess water from the ends of furrows is essential to prevent root suffocation. The presence of excess water can lead to oxygen deficiency in the root zone, resulting in wilting and rotting of the shoots. Designing the field bottom with a gentle slope towards the drainage channels is one of the key factors for success in this regard.

Answers to Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is surface irrigation for garlic recommended in arid regions?

In arid regions, surface irrigation can lead to significant water loss. Under these conditions, sprinkler or drip irrigation methods are recommended. However, if you use surface irrigation, ensure that mulch is applied to reduce evaporation. Otherwise, water use efficiency will drop sharply, potentially making the practice economically unviable.

What is the best time to start surface irrigation after garlic planting?

After planting garlic, perform the initial irrigation within 3 to 5 days to ensure good root-to-soil contact. The subsequent irrigation schedule is then adjusted based on the plant's growth stage. Planting without an initial water supply causes cloves to focus on survival rather than rooting, which slows their growth.

Can non-potable water sources be used for surface irrigation of garlic?

Using non-potable water for surface irrigation is not justifiable from an economic or resource perspective due to high water loss. It is better to utilize appropriate water sources. Additionally, water quality must be assessed; because surface water comes into direct contact with roots, saline water can cause root burn and reduce yield.

Conclusion

Surface irrigation for garlic is a traditional method, but it can yield good results if key points are observed, such as proper ridge formation, precise timing, and water flow control. However, given the water crisis, farmers can reduce water consumption by combining traditional and modern methods. Utilizing specialized guides, such as photonic maintenance tips and other educational articles, can be helpful. Ultimately, understanding local field conditions and adapting irrigation principles to them is the key to success. Continuous monitoring of soil moisture and plant status is an essential requirement, even when using traditional methods, and should not be overlooked.
