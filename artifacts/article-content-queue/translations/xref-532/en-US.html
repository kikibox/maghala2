Why is orchard irrigation important alongside onion cultivation?

In recent years, intercropping and modern irrigation systems such as drip irrigation and tape systems have become one of the most critical topics in agricultural water resource management. Many farmers believe that these two methods are suitable only for water-saving farms, but the reality is that their correct combination in intercropping systems, such as growing onions in orchards, can significantly increase economic efficiency and yield. Onion, with its shallow root system and requirement for uniform soil moisture, is considered an ideal option for utilizing surface-mat irrigation systems like tape and drip. While fruit trees require root depth and root temperature regulation, onions need rapid water supply in the upper soil layers. In this section, we compare the advantages and disadvantages of conventional ground-based irrigation with modern systems to understand the superiority of pressurized systems.

Introduction to tape systems and their application in onion farming

Technical principles of tape systems

Drip tape is a type of drip irrigation system in which water flows uniformly and continuously through open slits or very small orifices along the entire length of the tape. Unlike closed emitters that release a specific flow rate at fixed points, drip tape creates a thin, continuous water ridge that comes into direct contact with the soil surface. This feature ensures the soil surface remains consistently moist, which is critical for onion bulb emergence and growth during this most sensitive phase.

In onion cultivation, using drip tape with low flow rates (e.g., 1.6 liters per hour) and regular emitter spacing can prevent seed displacement and soil two-phasing. Farmers seeking to optimize resources must pay attention to quality of agricultural drip tape fittings and cables. with particular precision, as even the smallest leak in these systems can lead to water waste and reduced yield per hectare.

Drip Irrigation: The Primary Solution for Fruit Trees

While drip tape works on onion surfaces, fruit trees located in inter-row spaces require more stable and deeper systems. Drip irrigation with subsurface emitters or wiggler emitters is recommended for this section. The key point is that the tree drip system must not obstruct the penetration of shallow onion roots toward food sources in the middle soil layers. Balancing the working pressure (FPI) of the tree drip system ensures that trees are protected from root drought caused by onion roots (which may compete with tree crown roots) and maintains the water balance for both crops.

Proper water pressure management and the installation of suitable regulators prevent emitter clogging and ensure uniform water distribution. This uniformity is critical for preventing bulbing in onions and promoting even fruit development in orchards.

Practical tips for implementing a combined system

To ensure successful irrigation of fruit orchards alongside onion cultivation, correct technical execution must begin with the initial design. The most important implementation points are as follows:

  • Lateral spacing: Place the drip tape exactly in the center of each onion row. The distance between the onion row and the nearest tree dripper must be at least 50 to 70 centimeters to minimize root interference.
  • Irrigation scheduling: Onions require frequent irrigation with short duration, while trees require less frequent but longer-duration irrigation. It is essential to use separate valves and timers for each section.
  • Precision filtration: Because tape emitters are sensitive to clogging due to their small orifice size, installing sand and disc filters (or pressure-compensating filters where available) is essential to prevent clogging.
  • Flow Rate Monitoring: Always unroll a section of the tape and measure the actual flow rate in a mesh container to ensure there are no complete blockages or pressure fluctuations.

Additionally, selecting an appropriate dripper with a flow rate equivalent to the tape's flow rate prevents water pooling under the onion roots, which can cause neck rot.

Impact on Performance and Economics

Using orchard irrigation systems for onion cultivation results in water savings of over 40% compared to conventional irrigation. This reduction directly lowers pumping costs and energy expenses. Furthermore, minimizing evaporation and deep percolation maintains soil moisture during hot seasons, which is critical for uniform onion bulb development and sizing. For performance comparisons, it is also worth noting yield metrics such as those seen in sugar beet production per hectare; although this example pertains to vegetables, it demonstrates the same principles of moisture and nutrient management applicable to irrigation-sensitive crops. Onions watered by tape irrigation typically exhibit uniform, smaller, and denser bulbs, resulting in higher market profitability.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is drip tape suitable for onion cultivation in all regions?

Yes, drip tape is suitable for nearly all climates due to the high water efficiency at the soil surface. In areas with very heavy clay soils, the flow rate should be reduced to prevent surface runoff. In sandy soils, a higher flow rate is required.

Can I use fixed emitters instead of drip tape for onions?

Using hanging emitters on drip tape for onions is not economically or operationally optimal. Drip tape offers lighter weight, lower cost, and greater flexibility for onion harvesting and annual plowing. Fixed emitters are more suitable for biennial vegetables or permanent crops.

Is a specific filter required?

Due to the very small openings of the drip tape (usually less than 1 mm), the use of high-efficiency disc or sand media filters (120–150 micron precision) is essential. Using simple or weak filters leads to rapid clogging of the drip tape, which increases maintenance costs.

Conclusion and final recommendations

The combination of drip irrigation for fruit orchards and tape irrigation for onion crops intercropped in the gaps is one of the smartest modern strategies for water and soil resource management. This method not only reduces reliance on surface water sources but also enhances crop quality and yield, providing significant economic value to farmers by maintaining soil moisture equilibrium. The success of this system depends on precise installation, regular monitoring of flow rates, and proper filter maintenance. By following these principles, you can achieve healthy, high-grade marketable produce even under conditions of limited resources.
