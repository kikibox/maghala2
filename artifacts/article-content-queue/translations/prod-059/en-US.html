Technical Guidelines for Selecting the Appropriate Drip Tape

When purchasing, do not consider price alone. Technical specifications determine the final quality of your system. A high-quality drip tape must have strong crimp connections at both ends of the roll to prevent leakage. Additionally, the emitter spacing must be appropriate for your crop type.

Checking Clog Resistance

One of the biggest concerns for farmers is clogging of drip emitters. Modern drip tapes have internal filtration systems (Maze Channel) that disrupt flow and wash away sediments. When purchasing, always ask the seller whether the tubing is self-purging or not.

Compatibility with low pressure

Drip tapes typically operate at lower pressures (e.g., 1 to 3 bar). If your network pressure is higher, you need a Pressure Regulator. Pressure mismatch leads to dripper failure and uneven irrigation. In this regard, reviewing the types of drip tape helps you better understand pressure variables.

Key points in installation and operation

A good purchase is a beginning; correct installation is not the end, but the start of optimal performance. When laying a 20-centimeter strip on a 1000-meter roll, pay special attention to the following points:

  • Preventing misalignment: When unrolling the tape, avoid excessive stretching as this causes the dripper spacing to change.
  • Safe Connections: Use standard fitting connections and avoid securing the tape to the main pipe with Tape Thread, as this causes leaks.
  • Pressure Test: Before planting, fill the system with water and wait 24 hours to detect leaks.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is 20 cm drip tape suitable for trees?

20 cm drip tape is generally designed for low-growing and row crops such as vegetables, melons, and greenhouse plants. For trees with planting spacings of 50 or 60 cm, tapes with widths of 50 or 60 cm are typically selected to ensure better root zone coverage. However, this tape can be used for young tree seedlings.

How is the sale of a 1,000 m roll calculated?

Sepher, the calculation is based on the length of your planting line. If your plot length is 400 m and the line spacing is 1 m, you need approximately 1,000 m for one hectare. Therefore, one 1,000 m roll can cover one hectare. Always account for an additional 10% for connections and losses.

Can drip tape be flushed?

Yes, by installing a backwash system at the end of the network, you can flush out sediment and extend the life of the tape. This should be performed at regular intervals (e.g., every 2 weeks).
