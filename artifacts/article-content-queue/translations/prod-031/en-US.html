Understanding Slot Drip Tape and Its Applications

Slot drip tape is a type of drip irrigation tape that delivers water through slots on the tape body rather than through lateral lines. These slots penetrate very close to the soil surface, preventing water from reaching the plant foliage while moistening the deep roots. This feature is highly suitable for crops such as potato, corn, squash, and certain root vegetables.

Slot drip tape is typically produced in two types: lumen (with internal drip emitters) and non-lumen (with direct holes or internal slots). In the slotted type, small plastic emitters are placed inside the tube, causing water to flow slowly out of the holes at low pressure. This design reduces pressure loss and enhances water distribution uniformity along the length of the tape.

Key Advantages of Slot Drip Tape

  • Low cost Compared to tube drip tapes
  • Irrigation speed Higher for large water volumes
  • Suitable for free-draining soils With high permeability
  • Simple structure And relative repairability (in case of open plates)

Key criteria in selecting plate-type tapes

When purchasing a flagged drip tape, you must pay attention to several important factors. The first and most important factor is the material composition and the manufacturer of the drip tape. Most tapes are made from polyethylene (PE) or polypropylene (PP) with anti-UV additives.

Technical considerations in selecting material and thickness

  • Tube wall thickness (e.g., 0.8mm, 1.0mm, 1.2mm): The greater the thickness, the more resistant the tape is to pressure and abrasion. For repeated use, a thickness of 1.0mm or more is recommended.
  • Anti-UV additive grade: For fields exposed to sunlight for extended periods, definitely use tapes with strong anti-UV additives to reduce premature deterioration of the tape.
  • Tape WidthThe standard width of a perforated drip tape is usually 25 to 30 mm. The appropriate width should be selected based on the crop row spacing.

Perforation Spacing and Flow Rate

The spacing of the perforations (holes) on the tape is a critical parameter. This spacing must be adjusted according to the crop planting pattern. For example, for crops with narrower plant-to-plant spacing, the perforation spacing should be shorter to ensure water reaches each plant. The flow rate of the tape (e.g., 8 liters per hour or 16 liters per hour) must also correspond to the flow capacity of the main irrigation pipeline.

Impact of Planting Type on Drip Tape Selection

The crop type and field conditions directly affect the selection of perforated drip tape features. For root crops such as potatoes, a tape with deeper perforations that direct water to a depth of 15 to 20 cm is more suitable. In contrast, for crops with shallow root systems, such as certain leafy vegetables, shallower perforations perform better.

Selection Guide by Crop

  1. Potatoes and maize: Tape with a thickness of 1.0 mm or greater, emitter spacing of 20 to 30 cm, and deep emitters.
  2. Pumpkin and watermelon: Tape with a thickness of 1.2 mm, variable emitter spacing (depending on planting density) and medium-depth emitters.
  3. Leafy vegetables: Tape with a thickness of 0.8 mm, emitter spacing of 10 to 15 cm, and shallow emitters.

Price analysis of plated tape drip lines and influencing factors

The price of plated tape drip lines is influenced by several key factors: raw material quality, thickness, level of UV additives, length per roll, and currency exchange rate fluctuations. Generally, tapes with greater thickness and stronger additives have a higher price. However, from an economic perspective, choosing a more durable tape can reduce long-term costs.

Price comparison and tips for economical purchasing

  • Some domestic brands With competitive prices, they offer acceptable quality for one growing season.
  • Imported brands With stronger UV additives, they typically last 3 to 5 years and have a higher total cost, but are more economical in terms of durability.
  • Bulk Purchase: Buying longer rolls or larger quantities may increase the price discount.

Note that market prices are variable; always obtain quotes from multiple suppliers before purchasing. Practical Tips for Installation and Maintenance

After purchasing a perforated drip line, proper installation plays a key role in its performance. Ensure that the main line (lateral line) is connected to the perforated tapes with appropriate pressure. If the pressure is too high, the tape may rupture. If the pressure is too low, the perforations may not open properly, causing water to leak from the holes.

Ways to Extend Perforated Drip Line Lifespan

  1. Filter Cleaning Weekly flushing to prevent emitters from clogging.
  2. Use of anti-freeze fluids In cold regions before the onset of winter.
  3. Periodic inspection of tape lines To check for large holes or broken emitters.
  4. Do not use hot water or water containing harmful chemicals.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) About Plastic Flat Ribbon Tape

Is plastic flat ribbon tape suitable for all soil types?

Yes, but for heavy or low-permeability soils, tubular drip lines may be more efficient. For well-drained and light soils, plastic flat ribbon tape is a better option.

Why is water coming out less than usual from the holes in my plastic flat ribbon tape?

This issue is usually caused by a clogged filter in the pipeline system, sediment accumulation on the plates, or low water pressure in the main line. Clean the filters first and check the water pressure.

What is the useful life of plastic flat ribbon tape?

With proper maintenance, ribbons with strong anti-UV additives can last 3 to 5 years. Regular ribbons usually last one to two years.

What is the difference between licensed tape and fixed spray tape?

Fixed Spray Tape features hidden or non-removable nozzles, typically designed for surface irrigation. Licensed tape has holes regulated by internal or external plugs, allowing water to reach greater depths. To better understand the difference, you can read the article on Fixed Spray Tape .

How can I reduce the cost of licensed tape?

Reduce costs by selecting an appropriate thickness (avoiding overspecification) and purchasing in bulk. Additionally, periodic filter cleaning and preventing tape damage with equipment will extend service life and eliminate the need for frequent replacement.
