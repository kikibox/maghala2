Introduction: Why Is Mango a Strategic Fruit Crop in Modern Agriculture?

In recent years, global demand for tropical and subtropical fruits has increased significantly. Mango (Mangifera indica) has become a primary choice for orchardists due to its unique taste, high market acceptance, and considerable economic potential. However, growing this tree involves managing seedlings that are sensitive to water and soil stress. If water resources are not managed correctly, yield quality and quantity will decline even in orchards with suitable climatic conditions. The objective of this section is to familiarize you with the critical prerequisites for starting a successful mango orchard with a focus on proper irrigation.

Assessment of Climatic and Soil Prerequisites

Before installing any irrigation system, you must understand the climate and soil profile. Mango requires moderate to warm temperatures and adequate humidity. In arid regions with low annual rainfall, ensuring water supply from underground or surface sources of appropriate quality is the first prerequisite. Water quality is also critical; saline water or water with heavy sediment can damage roots and cause clogging of irrigation system emitters. Therefore, water testing and proper filtration (gravel and sand filters) are a requirement, not an optional feature.

On the other hand, the soil must have adequate drainage. Mango is sensitive to waterlogging, and its roots will quickly suffer from rot if excess water remains. Soil salinity is also a common issue in arid regions, making salt management through regular root zone leaching essential. Selecting the Appropriate Irrigation System: Drip vs. Micro-Sprinkler

One of the most important decisions in orchard development is the choice of irrigation system. Two main methods include drip irrigation and strip irrigation. Both methods have their own advantages and disadvantages that should be considered based on soil structure, row spacing, and budget.

  • Drip irrigation: This method delivers water drop by drop directly to the root zone of the tree. Its main advantage is the significant reduction in evaporation and optimal water usage. It also allows for precise water allocation to each tree. For crowded orchards or high-density plantings, drip irrigation is a good option.
  • Strip irrigation: In this method, wide strips of water flow between the rows. This system is generally applicable in farms with wide row spacing and light soils. Its initial cost is usually less than a full drip system, but it requires precise water flow management to avoid the loss of excess water.

When choosing between these two, keep in mind that strip irrigation requires an appropriate field slope and proper channeling. If the field is flat, a subsurface drip system may be the optimal choice. Before starting, you must know how much water each tree requires in every growth phase. Bamiye is most sensitive to water deficiency during the flowering and fruiting season. The shallow roots of this tree require adequate moisture at a depth of 30 to 60 cm. Using a soil moisture sensor or calculating psychrometric formulas for soil moisture monitoring is recommended. Practical and operational tips for success

Correct implementation of an irrigation system is not just about installing pipes and emitters. The following tips can make a significant long-term difference:

  1. Regular filtration: Inspect and clean screen and sand filters every 6 months. Nozzle adhesion caused by sediment buildup is a primary reason for reduced efficiency.
  2. Use of soluble fertilizers: Drip and band systems are excellent infrastructure for Fertigation. Injecting nitrogenous fertilizers during the growth phase and potassium during the fruiting phase significantly boosts yield.
  3. Draining and closing the system in winter: In cold regions, completely drain water from the pipes to prevent freezing of pipes and nozzles.
  4. Monitoring soil salinity: Collect soil samples annually from the root zone depth (50 cm) and measure EC. If EC is high, a deeper leaching flush is required.

Plant waste and weed management must also be performed alongside the irrigation system. Weeds are the primary competitors for water and nutrients. In arid regions, using mulch is highly effective for reducing evaporation from the soil surface.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. Can I use a low-yield well for irrigating my honeydate palm orchard?

Yes, but only if the well's discharge is sufficient during the peak demand season (summer). You must perform a well discharge and water level calculation during the warm months before installation. Pressure drop or reduced discharge can cause water stress and reduce fruit quality.

2. What should be the spacing of drippers around a honeydate palm tree?

In the early years, use low-flow drippers in a smaller radius. As the canopy grows, increase the coverage radius. Typically, one dripper every 15 to 20 centimeters is recommended for mature trees in arid regions.

3. Are surface waters (regional) suitable for drip lines?

If the water is from a river or canal, filtration is critical. Drip lines are prone to inlet nozzle clogging due to high water flow volume. Using a screen filter at the water inlet is mandatory.

4. How can I prevent water loss?

Irrigating during cool hours (early morning or night) and using a drip system instead of a sprinkler system are the most important methods. Additionally, shortening the irrigation duration while increasing frequency reduces water stress and improves efficiency.
