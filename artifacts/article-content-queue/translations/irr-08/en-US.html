Introduction to Surface Irrigation

Surface irrigation is one of the oldest and most common irrigation methods in Iran, where water is conveyed to cultivated fields via canals without pressure. This method is still used in many regions of the country due to its simplicity, low installation cost, and lack of need for complex mechanical infrastructure. However, for optimal efficiency and to prevent the waste of valuable water resources, a precise understanding of surface irrigation principles, including flow rate calculation, proper channel design, and avoidance of water loss, is essential. Principles and Mechanism of Surface Irrigation

The primary mechanism of surface irrigation is based on the movement of water over the soil surface via gravity. This method is mainly classified into 'flood' (Furrow) and 'basin' (Basin) systems. In the flood method, water is introduced into the irrigation furrows from main channels and moves in parallel. The primary objective is to create uniform and homogeneous soil moisture at the root zone. Ignoring the principles of this mechanism results in asymmetric water distribution and reduced performance.

Key design criteria

To design an efficient surface irrigation system, the following criteria must be carefully examined:

  • Longitudinal soil gradient: The appropriate gradient is typically between 1 and 2 percent. Excessive gradient causes accelerated flow and soil erosion, whereas a low gradient leads to water accumulation and waterlogging in the upstream area.
  • Soil type: Water absorption in sandy (coarse) soils is slower, which is more suitable for surface irrigation as it allows for more uniform guidance. In clay soils with low permeability, irrigation timing must be managed with greater precision.
  • Irrigation timing: It must be calculated how long it takes for the water to travel the length of the field to reach the end of the lateral line. This duration must be adjusted so that moisture does not reach the root depth of the plants, preventing deep percolation.

Optimizing water use in surface irrigation

One of the main challenges of surface irrigation is its relatively low efficiency coefficient compared to pressurized methods. To improve efficiency, focus must be placed on three main axes: system calibration, slope management, and precise irrigation timing. Below is a short guide for calculating irrigation requirements:

  1. Soil Moisture Test: Check the moisture level using simplified methods or digital equipment to prevent redundant irrigation.
  2. Calculating Required Flow Rate: Adjust the water inlet flow rate based on the area and soil type. High flow rates are dangerous for clay soils.
  3. Monitoring Zones: Prevent repeated water intrusion into drier patches and direct the flow to ensure uniform coverage.

For crops with deep root systems, such as dill, surface irrigation can lead to optimal water absorption from deep soil layers when timed appropriately. Practical tips

For better implementation of surface irrigation in farms, it is recommended to observe the following points to prevent common issues:

  • Laser Leveling: Level the land surface before each irrigation to ensure uniform water flow and prevent local pooling.
  • Bund Building: In furrow irrigation systems, construct appropriate earth banks around each section to control the speed of water discharge and ensure uniform irrigation time.
  • Stepped Water Supply: For slopes exceeding 2%, use stepped canals (check dams) to reduce soil erosion and ensure slow water movement.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is surface irrigation suitable for all crops?

No. This method is more suitable for crops with deeper roots, such as corn, wheat, and barley. For shallow-rooted grain crops, subsurface systems like tape or drip are usually more effective. What is the primary cause of water loss with this method?

The main reason is excessive water movement due to uncalibrated flow rate or an incorrect field gradient, which leads to deep percolation into the soil profile and prevents plant roots from accessing it. This issue can also cause salinization of the surface layers.
