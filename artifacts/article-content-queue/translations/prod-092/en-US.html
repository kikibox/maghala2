Introduction to 125-mm Flexible Pipe in Irrigation Systems

In modern agriculture, selecting the right equipment can make the difference between excellent efficiency and poor performance in an irrigation system. 125-mm flexible pipe Owing to its adequate flow capacity, structural resistance, and collapsibility for transport, it has become a main pillar in designing lateral lines and some main lines of farms. These pipes are typically made from polyethylene (HDPE) or polypropylene random copolymer (PPR) and are available on the market in two types: smooth and drip tape.

Using 125-mm pipe does not mean completely replacing 50-mm or 100-mm pipes; rather, it is sometimes used to reduce pressure loss in long lines or to transfer water to distant points of the farm. 1. Technical specifications of 125 mm flexible hose

The term 125 hose refers to the nominal internal diameter of the pipe. These pipes typically have a wall thickness of 1.5 to 2.5 millimeters, selected based on the standard working pressure (e.g., 10 bar or 16 bar). A distinctive feature of this pipe is its high flexibility compared to rigid pipes. This means it can be transported while coiled or bent, which is highly beneficial for farms with difficult access.

High-density polyethylene (HDPE) is the primary material for manufacturing these pipes, offering resistance to climatic conditions, UV radiation, and chemical agents. The white or gray color of the pipe results from the addition of anti-UV pigments.

2. Calculating Flow Rate and Pressure Drop

Accurately calculating the flow rate of water through a 125 pipe is key to the correct operation of the system. The required water volume for your farm depends on various factors, including area, crop type, weather conditions, and the irrigation coefficient. To determine the precise amount, you can use hydraulic formulas or consult reliable technical guides. For example, in crops requiring concentrated water supply, such as specific vegetable planting, accuracy in calculating seed quantity per hectare and water requirements is of paramount importance.

For a 125 pipe, the appropriate flow velocity is generally considered to be between 1 and 2 meters per second. If your system flow rate is very low (e.g., less than 10 liters per minute), using a 125 pipe may not be economically optimal, and 100 or 75 pipes could be better choices. However, on farms with flow rates above 50 liters per minute, a 125 pipe can significantly reduce pressure drop.

  1. Calculating water velocity: Total flow rate divided by the pipe's cross-sectional area.
  2. Checking the pressure drop per 100 meters of pipe, considering the farm's slope.
  3. Selecting the wall thickness based on the expected maximum pressure.

3. Drip tape and 125 mm pipe application

In drip tape systems, thin tubes with small holes are laid out in the field. The 125 mm pipe may serve as a sub-main line, distributing water to narrower drip tape lines. This combination increases irrigation precision.

The connection of drip tape to the 125 mm pipe should be made using standard fittings. Using high-quality fittings reduces leaks. If you intend to manage your system as retractable, knowing how to install drip tape with a water pipe is crucial so that you can retrieve the pipe at the end of the season and prepare it for the next season.

  1. Use the 125 mm retractable pipe as a sub-main line.
  2. Connect 16 mm or 19 mm drip tape to the main line.
  3. Design the line architecture to prevent pipe congestion.

Correct Structure of a 125 mm Foldable Pipe in a Farm

In the design of a complete drip irrigation system, the 125 mm pipe plays a crucial role. The system architecture begins with the pump or main source, leading into the mainline, which is the largest pipe, typically sized 160, 200, or 250 mm. The 125 mm sub-mainline receives water distribution from the mainline and transfers it to smaller laterals, including 63 or 75 mm pipes or drip tape. This layout helps deliver water to distant farm areas with minimal pressure loss.

Additionally, in large farms with high temperatures and harsh conditions, the use of flexible pipes such as the 125 mm pipe allows for adjustments in water routing under various conditions. You can turn or redirect the pipe as needed without requiring complex cuts or special fittings. Flexibility is a significant advantage, but it also has drawbacks. If the liquid pressure exceeds the permitted limit, the pipe may bulge or, when exposed to heat, it may deform.

Generally, the 125 mm pipe is an excellent 'middle point'; it is not a very large pipe, nor is it a very small pipe. For medium-sized farms with a system flow rate between 25 and 60 liters per minute, this size is a logical and cost-effective choice in terms of flow capacity and cost. However, if your farm has a very high flow rate (above 60 liters), you will likely need a 150 mm pipe to control pressure drop in the mainline. And if your system flow rate is less than 25 liters, a 100 mm pipe is a better option.

Another point: Due to its larger size, the 125 mm pipe requires more space in the field and creates large folded loops on the farm. The 100 mm pipe is more flexible in this regard. Finally, the 150 mm pipe is less flexible due to its wider diameter.

Practical tips for installation and maintenance of the 125 mm folded hose

Proper use of the 125 mm hose involves several key steps in installation, maintenance, and system management. These points help you maximize the hose's service life and ensure stable system performance.

  1. Installation: Store the hose in the shade and avoid prolonged direct sunlight exposure. This is particularly important in regions with extreme temperature fluctuations. When connecting the hose to couplings, ensure it is seated correctly without excessive stretching. Use appropriate seals and locks to create secure, permanent connections.
  2. Coupling selection: Choose metal corrosion-resistant couplings rather than plastic ones. Metal couplings offer significantly better pressure resistance and durability compared to plastic. Additionally, use drain couplings when a change in direction is required to allow water to flow freely.
  3. Wall thickness selection: The 125 mm hose is suitable for working pressures up to 10 bar. If your farm involves high elevations with extreme temperature fluctuations and high pressure requirements, select a 1.5 or 2.0 mm wall thickness. A 2.0 mm thickness is suitable for higher pressures up to 16 bar.
  4. Rolling and storage: At the end of the season, wash the hose, drain any excess water, then roll it up and pack it to prevent long-term degradation. If desired, use a protective cover during storage to shield the hose from sunlight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a 125 flexible pipe in a drip line system?

Yes, but a 125 pipe cannot be directly connected to a 16 or 19 mm drip line. The 125 pipe must function as a sub-main pipe from which further branches, such as 50 or 63 pipes or drip lines, are supplied.

Is a 125 flexible pipe suitable for specific orchard cropping?

Yes, due to its good flow rate, a 125 pipe is a suitable option for large orchards and higher temperatures. In specific orchard cropping such as seed counts or drip lines for beans,selecting the pipe size is of double importance.

Is a 125 pipe more cost-effective than a 150 pipe?

Yes, 125 mm pipe is more cost-effective in most cases. The cost of 125 mm pipe is usually 20 to 30 percent lower than that of 150 mm pipe. However, if your system flow rate is less than 25 liters per minute, a 100 mm pipe is a better choice, as it significantly reduces the cost associated with 125 mm pipe at this specific flow rate.
