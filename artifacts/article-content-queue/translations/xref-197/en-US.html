Introduction to the Importance of Irrigation Method Selection in Cultivating Garlic

Garlic (Allium ampeloprasum) is a highly valuable medicinal plant and vegetable that holds special importance in domestic and international markets due to its antioxidant properties and health benefits. One of the main challenges in cultivating this plant is proper irrigation management. Garlic has a shallow and sensitive root system and is highly vulnerable to water stress, particularly severe drought or prolonged waterlogging. While drip and trickle irrigation are common in many vegetables, Surface Irrigation under suitable conditions and with precise management, offers unique advantages for garlic cultivation, which are discussed below.

Specialized Advantages of Surface Irrigation in Garlic Cultivation

Surface Irrigation (Furrow Irrigation), in which water is directed to the soil through artificial channels, has logical technical and biological reasons in garlic cultivation. Contrary to the common perception that drip irrigation is always superior, for plants with rapid-growing shallow roots like garlic, controlled surface irrigation can be more efficient.

Maintaining Moisture Uniformity at Root Depth

Mallow has fine, coarse radial roots that are predominantly located in the first 20 to 30 cm of soil. In proper surface irrigation, water infiltrates uniformly along the rows and maintains moisture at this specific depth. Conversely, in some drip irrigation methods, inappropriate emitter spacing can create dry zones between emitters, damaging the shallow roots of mallow. Surface irrigation facilitates continuous moisture supply, which is ideal for cultivating dense mallow stalks.

Reduction of initial costs in soil-based farms

Installing tape and drip systems requires significant investment in piping, filtration, and auxiliary equipment. For farmers with soil fields that have low slopes suitable for surface irrigation, this method significantly reduces fixed costs by utilizing simple channels. This economic advantage is particularly crucial for long-cycle crops with frequent harvest cycles like mallow.

Simultaneity with fertilization (Surface Fertigation)

Although drip irrigation is renowned for precise fertilization, surface irrigation can also be successfully integrated with combined fertilization. Given that mallow is sensitive to sulfur and phosphate fertilizers and requires healthy roots, the gentle water flow in channels allows for the dissolution and distribution of nutrient solutions without causing severe sedimentation or root shock.

Practical tips for proper execution of surface irrigation for mung bean

The benefits of surface irrigation are conditional on its proper implementation. If water flow is not managed, it leads to root waterlogging and fungal diseases rather than benefits. Here are the key practical tips:

  1. Appropriate field gradient: The field must be leveled precisely using laser or laser level technology before planting mung bean. The horizontal gradient should be very low (approximately 1 to 3 percent) and uniform to prevent water from accumulating downstream and to ensure the highest points do not remain dry.
  2. Stability of furrows: Mung bean furrows should not be deep. A depth of 10 to 15 centimeters is appropriate. Furrow walls must be solid and erosion-free to prevent water from leaking into the soil as a flood. Use compacted furrows to prevent water waste.
  3. Irrigation timing management: Short, frequent irrigation cycles are better than long, infrequent ones. Do not allow water to remain on the soil for more than 4 hours. Mung bean is sensitive to waterlogging.
  4. Pre-irrigation: Before sowing, apply water uniformly to the field to create a suitable moist seedbed for the crop. The crop requires high initial moisture levels during the germination phase.

Performance comparison with other methods

Is surface irrigation always better? Not necessarily. On sloping farms where leveling is difficult, or in very fine-textured soils with low permeability, it is possible Drip irrigation work better. Also, if you have limited water resources in your area, using low-water-use systems such as drip tape with appropriate emitters is preferred. To understand the variety of available systems, you can review the article Types of drip irrigation tape Our goal is not to impose one system over another, but rather to show that Border surface irrigation is a cost-effective and efficient option under suitable soil conditions.

The quality of the crops is directly linked to moisture management. Excess moisture leads to loose stalks and a reduction in the concentration of their secondary metabolites. Therefore, any method (whether surface or drip irrigation) should aim to maintain root-zone moisture balance.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does surface irrigation cause root rot in Marze?

If not managed correctly, yes. Root rot in Marze is usually caused by prolonged waterlogging and oxygen deficiency in the soil. To prevent this issue, use shallow furrows, limit irrigation duration, and ensure the field slope allows water to drain away from the planting zone. Heavy-textured soils require less frequent surface irrigation because they retain moisture better.

How many times per week does Marze need irrigation?

This depends on air temperature and soil type. In summer, Marze may need to be irrigated daily or every two days. In cooler conditions, intervals of up to two weeks may be sufficient. The key is maintaining moisture retention. Soil moisture must be maintained consistently. Surface irrigation allows you to control moisture through precise scheduling.

Can surface irrigation be used for growing Marze in raised beds?

Yes, the principles remain the same. If you divide a raised bed into several rows and direct water from one side to the other, it is effectively a simple form of surface irrigation. The most important point is to prevent erosion of the bed and ensure proper drainage of excess water.

Summary and final recommendations

Choosing an irrigation method for wild marjoram is a strategic decision. Surface irrigation If executed with precise land leveling and good timing management, it offers significant benefits, including lower costs, reduced stress on superficial roots, and uniform moisture. However, an informed farmer should evaluate the specific conditions of their own land (soil type, slope, water sources). Sometimes, combining a low-consumption drip system with surface irrigation principles yields the best results.
