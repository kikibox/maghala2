Why does triticale require special care?

Triticale is a plant with relative resistance to various climates but has high sensitivity to stress caused by fluctuations in soil moisture. The roots of this plant are more sensitive to short-term dryness than many other cereals. Drip tape ensures that roots always have access to an adequate moisture source by creating a continuous moist band along the planting row. However, without regular monitoring, this system can become a weakness. Important points before installing drip tape in fields It can help you start from the beginning with a correct framework. Common installation errors, such as excessive pressure or improper placement of the tape, result in the bursting or clogging of microscopic holes, which leads to the death of plants in the dry band around the holes. Additionally, carefully checking the quality of the source water before it enters the system is a primary operator duty.

Regular inspection and early problem detection

Active maintenance means preventing problems from occurring rather than treating them after they happen. For triticale, its rapid growth cycle can be divided into three main stages: emergence, full canopy, and grain development. During the emergence stage, drip tape must be installed precisely within a radius of 10 to 15 centimeters from the main root. In this stage, monitoring water pressure is very critical. Any abnormal drop in pressure at the end of the line may indicate blockage or the accumulation of mud and silt in the tape. We recommend checking the last outlet of the tape once a week. If the water flow is uneven, the system may need reverse flushing or the water purification filters may need to be replaced. Weak filtration systems are the main enemy of drip tape and can block all holes within a week. Documenting the history of problems helps you identify recurring patterns.

Plant disease management through precision irrigation

One of the hidden benefits of drip tape is the reduction of fungal disease incidence. In sprinkler systems, continuous wetting of the leaves creates an ideal environment for pathogens. However, with drip tape, water is delivered directly to the soil surface and the leaves remain completely dry. For triticale, which is sensitive to rust and rhizoctonia, this feature is vital. However, an important point is that if water pressure is low, the water in the tape may not distribute properly, causing large water droplets at the discharge points. These droplets can burn the roots. To ensure even distribution, it is recommended to use tapes of appropriate thickness and a standard operating pressure (usually between 1 to 2 bar). If root burn is observed, slightly increase the distance of the tape from the stem and adjust the pressure. Paying attention to the soil color around the roots can also be an indicator of saturation or dryness.

Harvesting and Grain Collection: Technical Notes

When the harvest season arrives, the condition of the drip lines can affect crop quality and machinery performance. If drip lines are left in the field, they may be damaged by combines or machine wheels. Therefore, a pre-harvest protocol for collecting drip lines is critical. Conversely, if you intend to prepare the field for the next planting season, you must prevent soil damage. Unused drip lines, when stored under appropriate conditions, can be reused for the next season. Drip line storage should include cleaning, cooking, and storing them in a cool location away from direct sunlight. High heat and UV radiation cause the plastic to become brittle and significantly reduce its useful life.
