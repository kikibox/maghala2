The Importance of 90mm Ribbed Pipes in Modern Irrigation Systems

Among the various components of irrigation systems, water transfer pipes play a pivotal role. 90mm ribbed pipes, due to their high mechanical resistance and appropriate flexibility, are a common choice for main transfer lines in farms using drip or tape irrigation. These pipes feature a wire-wound mesh of steel or nylon fabric, encased in layers of polyethylene (PE). The objective of this article is to provide a complete checklist for Maintenance of 90mm Ribbed Pipes so that users can monitor their system's performance with greater accuracy.

Why is selecting the right pipe the first step in maintenance?

Many failures that occur years later stem from initial incorrect selection or improper installation. 90mm pipes are designed to withstand medium to high pressures; however, if used for very high pressures without proper wall thickness calculation, they will fail rapidly. Additionally, the type of internal pipe lining directly affects its useful life against sediment deposition and algae growth.

Main factors in the failure and wear of ribbed pipes

To properly maintain 90mm ribbed pipes, you must first understand the factors that damage them. Recognizing these hidden enemies is the first step in prevention.

  • Direct Sunlight Exposure: Ultraviolet (UV) radiation degrades the polymer in the outer pipe wall. This process causes the pipe to become brittle and crack over time. Although most industrial pipes contain UV stabilizers, continuous exposure to intense sunlight without shading reduces their lifespan from 8 to 10 years down to 3 to 5 years.
  • Pressure Spikes and Surges: Sudden shutdown of high-pressure pumps can create pressure waves. While flexible pipes absorb some of this shock, repeated events cause material fatigue and micro-tears within the pipe structure.
  • Sedimentation and Crystallization: If the water used has high hardness or unbalanced chemical solutions, salts will precipitate inside the pipes. These deposits not only reduce flow rate but also make the inner pipe surface rough, leading to increased mechanical wear.
  • Physical Damage: Agricultural machinery driving over surface or semi-buried pipes can cause cuts and punctures. These damages often occur during harvest and post-planting periods and are difficult to detect.

In systems using drip tape maintenance, the health of the main pipe is vital for the longevity of the tapes. If the threaded pipe leaks, pressure drops, disrupting uniform water distribution along the drip tape.

Practical strategies to extend pipe lifespan

1. Planning periodic flushing

One of the most important principles Maintenance of 90 mm threaded pipeis regular flushing of lines. Calcium and microbial deposits gradually form layers inside the pipes. It is recommended to flush the pipes outward every 3 to 4 months at a pressure higher than operating pressure. This process detaches and removes salts and algae from the walls. For this purpose, keep a ball valve at the end of the lines or a flush connector open so that water discharges under pressure through it.

2. Pressure management and safety valve adjustment

Every pipe has a nominal maximum pressure (PN). Using pressure relief valves at the end of lines prevents sudden pressure accumulation when line-end valves are closed. These valves automatically discharge excess pressure, protecting the pipe walls from stretching and plastic deformation. Be sure to test the function of these valves annually.

3. Covering and Physical Protection

In sunny and windy areas, using shade cloth or digging shallow trenches for transmission pipes significantly reduces maintenance costs. Additionally, installing protective fencing in areas where tractors pass prevents mechanical damage. If pipes are underground, using thin plastic protective sleeves for connections increases the lifespan of the joints.

4. Monitoring and Predictive Maintenance

Instead of waiting for a burst, use monitoring methods. Minor leaks in threaded pipes are often identified by the hissing sound of water. A monthly visual inspection of connections and weak points can prevent severe water loss and crop damage. Regarding Key Points in Drip Irrigation Operation, it must be known that the health of the main pipe directly affects the accuracy of water distribution in the drippers.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is a 90 mm threaded pipe repairable on-site?

Yes, for small holes and superficial cuts, specialized plastic repair kits or pipe fittings with industrial adhesive are used. However, in the event of a long tear or internal threaded pipe damage, the damaged section must be cut and replaced with a healthy section using a coupling method. It is best to always keep spare pipe sections on hand.

When should we drain and dry the pipes?

At the end of autumn or the beginning of winter, when irrigation stops, completely drain the pipes. Water remaining in the pipes will freeze in severe cold, increasing internal pressure and potentially rupturing the pipe walls. Additionally, persistent moisture creates an environment for mold and bacteria that degrade the pipes.

Does pipe color coding affect their lifespan?

The blue or black color of pipes is primarily intended to reduce ultraviolet (UV) light absorption. Black pipes, due to the presence of carbon black, offer better UV resistance than transparent or light-colored pipes. If your pipes are older and their color has faded, their UV protection is diminished; you should promptly protect them with shade coverings.

Is it safe to use chemical agents such as chlorine gas for washing?

Chlorinating source water can be harmful to certain specific plastics. Before chemical flushing, always check the compatibility of your pipe type using the Chemical Compatibility Chart. Generally, polyethylene pipes are compatible with low chlorine concentrations, but high concentrations can cause wall embrittlement.

Conclusion and Final Recommendations

Maintenance of 90 Millimeter Threaded Pipe This is not merely a repair task but a continuous management process. By adhering to principles of regular flushing, physical protection against light and pressure, and precise monitoring of connections, you can multiply the useful life of this critical equipment. Investing in proper maintenance not only prevents water waste but also significantly reduces pipe replacement costs and emergency repair expenses. Ultimately, the selection of seed quantity and suitable cropping is also dependent on the health of the irrigation system, as water-sensitive crops require precise distribution that is only possible with healthy pipes under uniform pressure. Caring for your pipes is caring for your crop and your investment.
