Introduction: Why is the cost of sprinkler irrigation for green onions important?

Green onion cultivation is one of the highest-demand products in Iran's wholesale markets. This plant is sensitive to soil moisture, and proper water resource management can be the difference between profitability and loss. The sprinkler irrigation method, due to its apparent simplicity, is still common in many green onion farms. However, is this method economically optimal? In this section of the article, we break down various farm costs to enable farmers to make more informed decisions.

Common Challenges in Sprinkler Irrigation

Sprinkler irrigation requires precise bed (topography) preparation. The slightest unevenness in the field can cause water to accumulate in one corner while leaving another corner dry. This moisture heterogeneity, in addition to reducing the percentage of effective water displacement, leads to uneven root growth. On the other hand, manipulating the soil with mechanized equipment to create a suitable bed doubles fuel costs and machinery depreciation, which must be considered in the final production calculation.

Detailed Cost Analysis of Sprinkler Irrigation for Green Onions

To better understand the cost of sprinkler irrigation for green onions, costs should be divided into three main categories: energy (water) cost, labor cost, and equipment and maintenance cost. We examine each of these in the following sections.

Energy and pumping costs

Although surface irrigation does not require high pumping pressure, in many regions, the pump head (vertical lift) is low. However, pump efficiency decreases with long surface irrigation conveyance lines. Calculations indicate that the average electricity cost per kilogram of onion seedling is 15 to 20 percent higher for surface irrigation compared to pressurized methods. This extra cost is solely to compensate for water loss in conveyance channels and low pump efficiency.

Labor costs

One of the most expensive aspects of surface irrigation is the labor required to control inlet flow rate and prevent seepage. In each irrigation cycle, at least one person must be present to supervise channels and repair ridges. Assuming 15 to 20 irrigation cycles per season for onion seedlings, the labor costs for this staff will be a significant part of the farm budget.

Economic comparison with modern methods: Drip and tape

In recent years, progressive farmers have shifted toward pressurized systems. The potato tape system is recognized as a successful model for root crops and onion seedlings. The tape system delivers water directly to the root zone. For onion seedlings, where roots are shallow and surface-level, this method maintains moisture uniformly and consistently at a soil depth of 10 to 20 centimeters.

In contrast, drip systems, which are also used in other crops such as white beans, offer higher precision in water distribution. However, for onion seedlings, which require significant bulbing (flesh development) around the plants, the tape system often provides better equipment and maintenance value. The tape system allows moisture to be directed toward the onion neck without causing hollowing or cracking of the bulbs due to excess moisture at the center of the plant.

Initial equipment costs and return on investment

Investing in pressurized systems may involve higher upfront costs. However, by reducing the need for labor and lowering water and electricity consumption, the return on investment is typically achieved within 3 to 5 growing seasons. Additionally, utilizing modern systems enables better nutrient management (fertigation), which can reduce chemical fertilizer costs by up to 30%. This is particularly critical for green onions, which have high sensitivity to soil salinity. Practical tips for cost optimization in green onion farms

If you plan to use surface irrigation for green onions, the following tips can help reduce ancillary costs:

  1. Soil testing: First and foremost, measure soil permeability. In compacted soils, surface water flow rates must be very low, which increases irrigation duration and consequently raises labor costs.
  2. Use of transport pipes: In the initial sections of the farm, use cast iron or polyethylene pipes for main water transmission. This reduces water loss during transit and lowers pressure on the pump.
  3. Mechanized soil preparation: Use mechanized equipment with appropriate plowshares for plowing and ridging to reduce soil preparation labor costs.

However, if you transition to modern systems, consider the following points:

  • Determine the appropriate flow rate for the drip line based on the line diameter and the onion bulb canopy radius. The line should run down the center of the row so that water moves outward.
  • Use an appropriate filter to prevent drip line clogging; the narrow profile of the lines causes drainage contaminants to get trapped in their channels.
  • Plan irrigation based on oceanic surge (daily weather surge). Onion seedlings have a higher surge during the vegetative growth stages, and the surge decreases in the final stages (bulb bulking). Adjusting the drip line flow rate according to these stages yields the optimal result.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does drip tape on onion transplants cause onion bulging?

No, on the contrary, the precise placement of drip tape in the center of the furrow prevents asymmetric moisture distribution. Onion bulging in surface irrigation systems is caused by water accumulation on one side of the furrow or dryness on the other, which is resolved by the drip tape method. If you have worked with similar crops such as garlic , which belong to the same family as onions, you will notice that uniform moisture management is crucial for final product quality.

How much more expensive is drip tape compared to drip irrigation?

Typically, the cost of drip tape for onion transplants is lower than drip irrigation because drip systems require a very high number of emitters. A 10 to 15-meter-long drip tape covers the needs of an entire furrow. This reduces maintenance costs and results in lower system pressure requirements.

Can a combination of surface irrigation and drip tape be used?

Simultaneous use of these two systems is not recommended because the flow and pressure management for the two systems are completely different. However, many farms use surface irrigation in the long term for flushing post-harvest fertilizers. Therefore, it is suggested that surface irrigation channels be maintained solely as flushing channels.

What is the best time to irrigate green onions to reduce costs?

Irrigating during low-sunlight hours (early morning or late evening). This practice reduces evaporation and improves water use efficiency, ultimately lowering energy and water costs.

Summary: Moving toward a more sustainable and profitable farm

Finally, calculating the cost of surface irrigation for green onions indicates that this method is unprofitable in the long term due to hidden labor costs, high water and energy consumption, and noticeable efficiency losses. Innovative solutions such as managing other crops across hectares have also shown that precise management of the root zone and soil moisture is the key to increasing efficiency. By transitioning to tape drip or drip irrigation, farmers gain better control over the root environment of green onions, eliminate excessive labor costs, and supply the market with a higher-quality product (more uniform size and hollow-free). This initial investment is not a cost but a guarantee of more sustainable long-term profitability.
