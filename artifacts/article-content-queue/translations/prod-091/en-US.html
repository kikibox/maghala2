Technical and Structural Features of 75 mm Threaded Pipe

Polyethylene Material and Resin Quality

The most important factor distinguishing different brands is the type of polymer used in pipe manufacturing. Standard pipes use medium density polyethylene (MDPE) or high density polyethylene (HDPE). Reputable brands use high-purity grade resins that ensure higher chemical and mechanical resistance. Low-quality pipes may use recycled resins, which significantly reduces their useful life when exposed to UV radiation and temperature fluctuations.

Wall thickness and internal ribs

The 75 mm threaded pipe, in addition to the smooth pipe, features internal ribs that increase surface contact area and resistance to lateral pull-out. When comparing brands, attention should be paid to the depth and consistency of these ribs. Brands utilizing advanced injection molding technology produce more uniform and robust ribs. This feature is critical for main lines that withstand higher pressures. Practical tips for installing and connecting 75 mm pipes

Importance of using Cap Card

The biggest issue when installing threaded pipes is leakage at the joints. Using a standard seal collar appropriate for the pipe thickness is essential. Some manufacturers produce proprietary seal collars that may not be compatible with couplings from other brands. Therefore, when purchasing 75 mm pipes, ensure that the fittings are compatible with the couplings you have available. Leakage in main lines can lead to water loss and reduced pressure at the end of the line.

Layout and Allowable Bending

75 mm pipes have good flexibility, but sharp bends can cause deformation of the threads and reduced flow rate. In main line design, avoid bends with a small radius. The pipe path should be routed to avoid rocky or sharp areas. Additionally, the pipe must be laid on an appropriate bed to prevent contact with the ground in areas with deep ruts. Working pressure and Sagging Risks

Due to the presence of threads, threaded pipes offer higher tensile strength compared to smooth pipes, but they remain sensitive to internal pressure. During startup, increase the pressure gradually to prevent sudden bursts at weak points. Also, in cold seasons, water accumulation in the pipe and freezing can cause increased pressure and pipe rupture. Draining the pipe after irrigation is complete is a critical step.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is a 75 mm pipe suitable for lateral lines?

Generally, 75 mm pipes are designed for main lines and primary distribution. For lateral lines directly feeding drip tape or drip lines, 50 mm or 40 mm sizes typically offer better performance. Using larger pipes for laterals reduces flow velocity and fails to ensure uniform distribution, unless the lateral length is very long.

How can I verify brand authenticity?

Always look for standard certification labels, the manufacturer's name, UV symbols, and production dates on the pipes. Reputable brands use a unique code for each pipe roll. Additionally, purchasing from authorized dealers or official distributors reduces the risk of buying counterfeit products. Can a 75 mm pipe be repaired?

No, polyethylene pipes such as 75 mm are generally not suitable for heat fusion or soldering, especially those with internal threads. In the event of a burst or damage, the only solution is to remove and replace the damaged section. Therefore, pipe durability is a determining factor for your operational costs.
